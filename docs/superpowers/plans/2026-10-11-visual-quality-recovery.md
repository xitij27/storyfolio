## The next prototype must answer a design question

Can one object from the person's work explain something through its form,
surfaces and transformations that the current row labels do not?

Before implementation, identify the source-supported work relationship to
show, the object's physical/visual identity, and the evidence that belongs
on it. Retire the coordinator's six-row, fixed-camera and static-phone
constraints for this new prototype. Preserve them in the old fixture.
Do not invent circuit topology, performance measurements or ownership to
make the scene look technical. Clearly illustrative behavior can still be
spatial, detailed and interactive.

Build in a new, explicitly identified workspace with source snapshots in
the repository. Do not change the rejected actor output or pretend a manual
redesign is an unchanged-agent rerun. Begin with the existing custom Piece,
face drawing, formation and extra hooks. Add engine API only when a concrete
prototype requirement cannot reasonably be expressed by those hooks.
The shared deformation and live repaint limitations are candidates to
investigate, not an instruction to copy the original globe or maze.

| Beat | Required visual evidence |
|---|---|
| Opening | An identifiable object from the work, authored surface detail, intentional lighting/depth and a clear relationship to the person's opening question |
| Explanatory middle | A materially different use of the same object that makes a sequence, relationship or mechanism legible; inspect the actual transition, not only its endpoint |
| Ending | The object's final arrangement supports the answer to the opening, with enough continuity for the visitor to recognize how it got there |

Use the original's composition, material detail and useful exhibits as the
quality reference. Its exact colors, paper subject, chapter count or effect
count are not universal requirements. A decorative imported model would
not fix the current failure by itself.

## Visual acceptance before expansion

Review the three beats together, desktop movement in both scroll directions,
and the mobile composition. Save representative frames and movement evidence
with viewport, source revision and capture conditions. Compare with the
reference and answer the existing design tests explicitly:

- **Backdrop:** Does the scene explain a relationship or show meaningful
  evidence, beyond repeating the adjacent headings? Accessible HTML can and
  should preserve that meaning when the scene is unavailable.
- **Depth:** Is there a beginning, middle and end, with the same object
  developing the story across them, rather than a single repeated scene?
- **Swap:** Are the object, surfaces and behavior specific to this person's
  work, or could another name and labels replace them without changing it?
- **Detail and hierarchy:** Are the important artifacts large enough to
  inspect, with readable surfaces and visual variety between beats?
- **Spatial quality:** Do form, occlusion, light and motion make spatial
  structure useful to understanding the work? This is an additional visual
  criterion, distinct from the design's narrative Depth test.
- **Phone and still modes:** Keep a meaningful visual explanation at narrow
  widths. Choose an appropriate lighter scene or authored still treatment;
  a text-only fallback does not prove the intended phone experience.

Record the author's actual assessment of this prototype before expanding
the rest of the site. A simulated look reply in an evaluation is not that
approval. Factual and accessibility checks remain necessary alongside this
gate. Do not convert image counts or a visible canvas into a quality score.
