# A portfolio introduces the person

22 October 2026. Settled in discussion with the author
([record](../../history/2026-10-22-a-portfolio-introduces-the-person.md)).
Amends [the skill design](2026-10-10-portfolio-skill-design.md) and corrects
[Each scene pictures its section](2026-10-22-scenes-picture-their-section.md).
Not yet in the skill.

## What was wrong

Every Stage 3 site was a deep dive into one project's test bench, told
through rigid pieces that rearranged. The author: "is that how you introduce
a person?" Two causes. The skill built the site around one question and the
sections of one document from the person's trade, and let the Who and Where
from roles drop when the material was thin. And every test used a person
whose whole CV is one employer and one project, with the employer and dates
then removed.

## The order

Agreed by the author: "that order is perfect".

| # | Chapter | What it holds |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hello | Name, what they do in one line, what they are looking for |
| 2 | Where from | The places they have lived and moved between |
| 3 | What they studied | Education, and what in it shaped them |
| 4 | Where they have worked | Roles over the years: where, what, when, what they owned |
| 5 | What they are good at | Their field and its depth |
| 6 | What they have made | Every project in brief, one or more in depth |
| 7 | What others say | Their words, if there are any |
| 8 | Get in touch | Email, CV, links |

A chapter without material is asked about at the first stop, not dropped
silently. How much life and how much work depends on what the person gives:
"everything can be shown in brief and the user can get more information by
clicking on the project or stuff like that". The scroll stays brief; detail
opens on a click (a project window or page).

## The through-line

The author: "A single idea tying it together sounds cool, but honestly,
your generated ideas are terrible. You need to do better over there. I
mean, a simple introduction also is okay, but it feels like it's missing
something." A through-line is wanted, and the bar for it is high; a plain
introduction is the fallback, not the aim.

## What the author's own site shows about the 3D

V3 is one object, the nine pages of a paper, and the author made it the way
they wanted. Its creativity is in what the pages become, chapter by
chapter. Each is a picture of the chapter, often a working one:

- the opening: the front pages of the papers the author learned from hang
  in the air and fly in to pile up as the paper;
- where they have lived: the pages bend into the gores of a paper globe;
- how they work: the pages lie as a desk, and data points scattered over it
  gather into clusters as the reader scrolls;
- projects shown working: a maze printed across the pages as a floor, solved
  by a search as the reader scrolls; a speech detector running on the
  sheets' mesh as a spectrogram, with a noise level the reader sets; one
  photograph across all nine pages, grey on one side of a seam and colour on
  the other;
- every project: the pages hang as a wall of plates, each opening the
  project's window;
- outcomes: the pages stand as cards, one per outcome.

The lessons:

1. **The object must be able to become pictures.** Sheets bend, lie flat,
   tile, print anything and fold into a globe. Rigid bars, tiles and blocks
   can only be rearranged, which is what every Stage 3 agent chose. One
   object is right when it can become each chapter's picture; otherwise
   each chapter takes its own scene from the person's world.
2. **Each scene is a literal picture of its chapter,** recognisable with the
   captions hidden: a globe for places, the work itself for projects.
3. **Some work is shown working,** driven by the scroll or a control: the
   project runs in miniature.
4. **The opening shows where the person comes from,** as the reading becomes
   the paper.
5. **Brief on the scroll, depth on a click.**

This corrects the 22 October scenes amendment where it set "one world, not
one object": one object stays allowed, judged by whether it can become each
chapter's picture.

## Ideas

The agents' concepts were weak because they were reasoned from caution and
from a trade document's section names, and judged by whether they risked a
claim. Concepts should be judged against the V3 bar: for each chapter, does
the scene become a picture a visitor would name, and does at least one
project run? An agent should produce many candidate pictures per chapter
before choosing, and drop any concept whose scenes are rearrangements.
