# Storyfolio

Makes one kind of portfolio site: one long scroll that introduces the person in 3D, each chapter's scene a picture of what it is about.

Start from the recorded handover on a continuation. For a requested build, the site runs from stop 2 and grows at each stop after. A planning-only request still ends at the storyboard.

## Stops

Between stops you work by yourself. At each stop the person decides one thing. You go on only when their answer is in the conversation and you have written it into the file.

| Stop | First open | You put to the person | You go on when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Read | `references/reading-a-profile.md` | What you found, as facts to confirm and questions to answer. No concept or design | They have answered, and `docs/story/facts.md` holds their answers |
| 2. Concept | `references/concept.md`, `references/diagrams.md` | One mix of gallery pieces, running for a requested build, and why | They have accepted it or said what to change, and `docs/story/concepts.md` records it |
| 3. Storyboard | `references/storyboard.md` | The script of the whole page | They have said yes to it |
| 4. Look | `references/building.md`, `engine/README.md` | The whole site in two or three looks | They have chosen a look |
| 5. Words | `references/building.md` | Pages that open on a click, and three whole-beat wording versions | They have chosen the wording |
| 6. Finish | `engine/README.md` | The checked site and what still needs their review | Their actual review is recorded |

**When starting from profile material, your first reply is the first stop, and no more than that.** Brief build-setup notes do not advance the design. People ask for the whole plan at once: the idea, the scroll, the colours. Do not give it yet, and say why in a sentence. A plan made now is made from your reading of a resume, and a resume leaves out exactly what a site must not get wrong: whose result a number was, what a title hides, who took a photograph. Once they have answered you will have a better idea, and it will not have to be taken apart.

## Ask or decide

Ask the person when a thing is:

1. a fact only they know,
2. a claim that could embarrass them if it were wrong,
3. taste on what a visitor sees first: the concept, the look, the opening words,
4. anything that publishes, costs money, or shows another person or an employer's work.

Everything else you decide. Write each decision into `docs/story/decided-for-you.md`: what you decided, why, and how to change it.

If the person will not answer, or tells you to decide everything, open `references/asking.md`.

## What you keep

In the person's own folder, so that a later session can go on from the files alone:

| File | Holds |
|---|---|
| `docs/story/facts.md` | The fact sheet, each fact with where it came from, and the person's answers |
| `docs/story/concepts.md` | The mix, why each piece, and their answer |
| `docs/story/storyboard.md` | The script |
| `docs/story/decided-for-you.md` | What you decided without asking |
| `docs/story/handover.md` | What exists, what the person has said, what is next |

## What holds from the first stop on

- **Nothing about the person is invented.** You know that a number, an employer, a title and a quote are facts. So is every small thing a sentence says on its way: what was wrong before they came, how often a thing is used, which came first, why, how a thing was left, what it taught them. That sounds like writing, and it is a claim. Before handing over any reply or file, check each claim against the fact sheet, including introductory summaries, working titles and words in the scene. Read the supporting line and its unresolved questions, not just its reference. It must establish the whole claim: a topic attached to one item does not establish the topic of another. Wording that depends on an unanswered question stays out of the introduction and the planned page, even if copied from a profile. Remove it or list it as a question to confirm. An illustration of the kind of thing they work with, labelled as one, claims nothing: a plated dessert, a status page. A particular design, a measured value or an event does. An empty scene is no safer.
- **A number is used as the person confirmed it,** with what it measures and whose result it is.
- **What is someone else's is asked about before it is planned for:** a photograph a photographer took, a friend's drawing, a quote, an employer's screens, a story with a patient or a client in it.
