## 1. The bring-up fixture — recommended

**Question:** How does a firmware decision become something you can check?

**Form:** Bring-up checklist. Five chapters in order: Prepare (opening/name/question and lesson), Command (who/how: firmware and power states), Access (what: Mosslink firmware contribution and fixture, colleague's PCB division), Observe (how/outcomes: radio-awake observation; no cause or resolution), Record (evidence limits and close: two-year projection versus 72-hour run, sample/check status, email/CV).

**Object:** Generic bring-up fixture formed from seven solid, machined-looking segments with solid bases and upright probe contacts. Labels: Firmware, States, Command, Fixture, Observe, Estimate, Run. Explanatory geometry, not actual fixture wiring, dimensions or employer hardware. It is recognisable assembled, not flat printed material.

**Three transformations:** Prepare assembles the fixture from separated segments, expressing Ada's lesson that decisions need observations. Access separates command/access/observation groups to explain the fixture relationship. Record separates Estimate and Run before bringing the fixture back together; distinction remains in labels and HTML.

**Opening and close:** Opening identifies Ada, firmware work, Mosslink contribution and question, with shortcuts to evidence and contact. Close answers that a firmware decision needs physical observation, and a claim must respect its evidence. Email and corrected HTML CV.

**Stands on:** F2,F3,F4,F5,F6,F8,F9,F11 and confirmed user corrections. Project outcomes are attributed to project, not all to Ada.

**Needs:** Concept selection only. No missing photo or unapproved artifact needed. Supplied fixture teaching SVG can serve as labelled fallback; no trace invented.

**Why theirs:** The fixture Ada built connects her firmware scheduling to the unresolved radio observation and the specific distinction between Mosslink's battery projection and observed run.

## 2. The power-state assembly

**Question:** What can a power-state plan tell you—and what needs an observation?

**Form:** State table. Five chapters: State (opening/question/name), Responsibility (who: Ada firmware versus colleague PCB), Transition (how: teaching Sleep/Measure/Transmit sequence), Observation (what/outcomes: unresolved radio-awake observation and evidence limits), Evidence (close: estimate versus run, contact).

**Object:** Generic sensor assembly of seven thick modules with physical pins and housings. Labels: Firmware, Sleep, Measure, Transmit, Observe, Estimate, Run. Not actual Mosslink component layout, enclosure or circuit. Modules are explanatory partitions, not a claim that firmware states occupy separate physical modules.

**Three transformations:** State assembles a sensor from its modules. Transition opens modules into a teaching loop plus observation branch, without durations or values. Evidence places Estimate and Run separately, then rejoins the sensor while keeping their labels distinct.

**Opening and close:** Opening establishes firmware contribution and hardware division. Close explains that planning and observing support different claims; no final qualification claim. Email/CV.

**Stands on:** F2,F3,F4,F6,F8,F9,F11. Every teaching loop is explicitly illustrative.

**Needs:** Selection only; supplied power-state SVG available as explanatory fallback. No private circuit or actual firmware required.

**Why theirs:** It centres Ada's scheduling responsibility while correcting sensor-design ownership and leaving the failed-transmission observation unresolved.

## 3. The observation loom

**Question:** What does a physical observation add to a firmware decision?

**Form:** Timing-diagram reading, without pretending to show an actual timing diagram. Five chapters: Command (opening/question/name), State (how: working documents and teaching states), Observation (what: Mosslink fixture access), Interpretation (outcomes: unresolved radio observation and project evidence limits), Limits (close: observation bounds a claim, email/CV).

**Object:** A generic measurement loom made of seven cylindrical probe connectors and short cable sections. Labels: Command, States, Fixture, Observe, Problem, Estimate, Run. Derived from access/bench observation in Ada's fixture work; does not reconstruct real cable paths, probe positions or electrical connections.

**Three transformations:** Command gathers the connectors into a loom. Interpretation opens ordered command/observation/question positions; distances are not elapsed time and no waveform appears. Limits returns the loom to a whole with separate Estimate and Run labels.

**Opening and close:** Opening names Ada's working documents and firmware/fixture contribution. Close states what evidence does and does not support, including engineering-sample versus pilot distinction. Email/CV.

**Stands on:** F2,F3,F4,F5,F8,F9,F11. This is a conceptual reading, not invented measured data.

**Needs:** Selection only. Teaching fixture SVG supplies labelled fallback. No trace or photo needed.

**Why theirs:** It follows the specific failed-transmission observation without inventing its cause, and ends on the projection-versus-run lesson Ada wants to express.
