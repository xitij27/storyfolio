PROJECT / ENVIRONMENTAL SENSOR

Mosslink.

Firmware, power states and the connection to a physical observation.

ContributionExplanationsObservationEvidence

01 / CONTRIBUTION

My work and its boundary.

I wrote Mosslink’s firmware, scheduled the power states and built the bring-up fixture. My colleague designed the printed circuit board and analogue front end.

Bring-up means checking a new board’s basic operation. My tools include C, oscilloscopes, logic analysers and board bring-up. My working documents are a state table, a timing diagram and a current trace.

02 / EXPLANATIONS

Two teaching drawings.

These supplied local drawings explain relationships. They are not photographs, original wiring, recovered firmware, a current trace or test results. The fixture photograph remains unavailable.

Drawings supplied under the repository’s MIT licence. The colleague’s circuit diagram is excluded.

03 / OBSERVATION

A radio that stayed awake.

A current trace showed the radio staying awake after a failed transmission. No cause, fix, timing or later resolution is confirmed. No trace file is available to publish.

The lesson I want to express: a firmware decision needs a physical observation. An estimate and an observed run support different claims.

04 / EVIDENCE

Keep each claim within its evidence.

Battery projection
Two years, projected from bench current readings and an assumed reporting cycle. No field endurance or weather testing supports a two-year claim.
Observed run
The longest continuous run was 72 hours.
Engineering samples
Forty assembled; six tested on the bench. None went to customers. This was not a field pilot or a product launch.
Emissions
One preliminary check passed. The full qualification programme is unfinished. The device is not described here as certified or qualified.

Sample counts and check outcomes describe the project. They do not assign all assembly, testing or qualification work to me.
