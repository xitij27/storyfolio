# Three concepts and selection

User delegated uncovered choices in the follow-up. Selected: A, the ferment log. No website or visual prototype exists.

Trade forms considered: prep list, ferment log, recipe sheet, ordering sheet, weekly menu, workshop handout. Only prep list, ferment log and weekly menu are confirmed personal working forms. Ordering is a sourced past responsibility; the other forms are possibilities, not claims about Ines.

## A — The ferment log (selected)

- Question: What happens before the menu is written?
- Form: ferment log. Six chapters: Ferment log (opening/who), Started (where from), Salt (method), Check days (work and measured outcome), Ready (work shared and recommendation), Next entry (answer/contact). Opening and closing headings are editorial framing; Started, Salt, Check days and Ready derive from the fields Ines named. This is a designed reading of the log, not a claim to reproduce its actual page layout.
- Object: eight paper log sheets, always present, with printed log-field names, owned larder photographs and source-backed project identifiers. No invented batch values, recipes, handwriting or stains. The paper format is a design assumption; the actual log medium is unknown.
- Transformations: at Started, sheets form a dated sequence using only confirmed career facts; at Salt, they spread into a diagram of the named log fields; at Check days, they form a comparison with the whole-kitchen waste result and its measurement note; at Ready, a readable work index; at the close, a collected log with the weekly menu as its last sheet.
- Opening: an owned larder/label photograph sits on the front sheet, identifying the real working context before the sheets unfold. No invented childhood-to-career narrative.
- Close: the prep list and log precede the weekly menu, using Ines's explicit account. Three inquiry destinations: workshops, apprenticeships, press/writing.
- Stands on: user answers about prep list/log/menu order; personal phone photos; corrected Head Chef date and stage; confirmed kitchen waste measurement; published Brine/Sunday Pot descriptions; Callum's authorized quote; confirmed audiences.
- Needs: selected owned images checked for prices/faces; contact address; ideally a redacted log/prep-list sample. A sample improves fidelity but is not required to understand the proposed structure. No Brine drawings or Priya images required.
- Why hers: the ordering comes from her menu-last practice, and the faces carry her larder, her measured kitchen result and her own work rather than interchangeable chef imagery.

## B — The prep list

- Question: What work has to happen before service?
- Form: prep list. Six chapters: Prep list (opening/who), Tasks (background), Order of work (method), Checks (work), Complete (outcomes/quote), Next list (contact). Apart from the confirmed document name, these are proposed editorial groupings, not verified fields in her list.
- Object: six long task slips clipped together. Each carries source-backed work; no invented checkmarks suggesting completed tasks.
- Transformations: Tasks makes a chronological row; Order of work lays the prep list and log before the menu; Complete aligns the kitchen result and quote as separate evidence slips.
- Opening/close: slips gather from an owned larder image into a list; the final menu slip appears last and the whole list returns alongside inquiry links.
- Stands on: user-confirmed prep list and menu order, dated career facts, waste result, owned photos and quote.
- Needs: ideally an actual redacted prep list; contact address. Existing material does not establish its fields or layout.
- Why hers: the final menu is deliberately subordinate to her documented prep work and larder.
- Tradeoff: a direct account of work, but less specific about time and repeated checks than the ferment log.

## C — The jar labels

- Question: What can a jar label tell you about the work behind a menu?
- Form: the ferment log read through jar labels; six chapters: Labels (opening/who), Started (background), Salt (method), Check days (work), Ready (outcomes/quote), Next entry (contact).
- Object: eight flat label cards forming a single label rack; not floating jars or a generic food sculpture. Actual label photos provide texture; new design text remains separately identified, never passed off as original label data.
- Transformations: Started arranges the cards chronologically; Salt pairs each named field with the corresponding log explanation; Ready forms an index of confirmed work, with the kitchen result on a separate evidence card so it is not mistaken for a batch result.
- Opening/close: actual labels from owned phone images assemble into the rack; the rack collects again beside the menu-last process and inquiry links.
- Stands on: owned label photos, confirmed log fields, menu-last practice, waste result, named work and quote.
- Needs: sharp owned label images with identifying details checked; contact address. No right to restaurant-owned imagery assumed.
- Why hers: her actual labels supply the object instead of the chef's conventional plate, hat or tool.
- Tradeoff: strongest material texture, but less room for readable case material than full log sheets; enlarged labels risk implying batch details we have not confirmed.

## Choice and tests

Choose A over B and C: it gives time, method, evidence and the final menu a coherent order while leaving enough face area for useful work. All three keep one object throughout; transformations organize information instead of decorating it. Names, practice, one proof and contact are available without completing the scroll. No concept depends on unconfirmed individual ownership of team results or pending artwork permission. Editorial fields and graphic layouts are design decisions, never presented as historical documents.
