Body about 18–20 px desktop, 17–18 px phone; line length 55–65 characters. Strong readable hierarchy; no oversized word that crowds out identity/evidence/actions. Preserve actual handwriting only in source images, with transcripts; do not manufacture handwriting in her voice.

## Motion, performance and future validation

One scene and the same eight leaves throughout. Lazy-load optional 3D after useful HTML renders; pause rendering when offscreen, tab hidden or scene settled. Avoid heavy physics, real-time refraction and continuously moving light. Images responsive/compressed, font subsets and lightweight geometry. Phone defaults to simpler presentation; load scene only where it can run smoothly.

Future acceptance: reading/contact work with no JS and no WebGL; reduced-motion has no camera/page travel; all links/controls work by keyboard/touch; text survives 200% zoom; no scroll traps; correct team attribution/time/scope beside waste figures; Fjaerland never framed as research; excluded prices/faces absent; credits and approvals complete. Test a modest phone and desktop; target Core Web Vitals LCP <=2.5 s, INP <=200 ms, CLS <=0.1. These are targets, not measured results.

## Exact interaction specification

- Header: name/home anchor, chapter menu, Contact anchor, motion toggle. Skip-to-content link appears on keyboard focus. Active chapter indicated by text/shape as well as colour. No forced custom cursor.
- First screen: question, identity and team-evidence preview all in HTML; preview jumps to Ready. Contact goes straight to three enquiry paths.
- Started: pot photo opens a readable inline detail panel with ownership/identification; no personal story beyond confirmed influence. Use ordinary expandable HTML so it also works without JS.
- Salt: “Read the log” expands transcript, “Read the prep list” expands the redacted list. Field labels can highlight the corresponding actual record area when motion is enabled; never required to read it.
- Checks: “View larder photograph” opens larger accessible figure; “Read record” expands transcript. No clickable jar that implies a recipe or unsupported readiness judgement.
- Ready: measurement scope visible by default; optional expanded note repeats attribution, waste categories, measurement period and excluded bones. No chart tooltip needed to understand numbers.
- Log note: quote and attribution readable immediately. No carousel, additional endorsement or invented link.
- Menu: apprentice enquiry, workshop booking/enquiry and writer contact. Prefer supplied booking URL and real email links; clearly visible address as backup. Do not add a form or payment processor without a defined operational need.
- Footer: back to top, contact, credits, applicable privacy/legal information. Credits describe only displayed assets. No dead links or placeholder destination in a launched version.
- Any future overlay must support Escape, close button, focus containment and return focus. Prefer inline disclosure for transcript/image details so the baseline needs none of that machinery.

## Content and operation deliverables for a later implementation

Asset manifest: filename, source, owner, permission status, credit, alt description and redaction check. Pot/larder photos are primary; authentic prep/log excerpts secondary. Keep a private original separate from any public redacted export. Prices and identifying staff content stay out of public files, including source images and downloadable attachments.

A hosting/domain choice, HTTPS, canonical address, deployment/rollback process and domain renewal responsibility will be needed later. Nothing purchased or deployed. First release can be static with email/external booking links; no database or CMS assumed. Keep contact destinations, record excerpts, photo captions/credits and future workshop details independently editable. Record a simple update procedure and check external links periodically.

Final copy will be a separate editorial task grounded in facts.md; this plan supplies the question, headings, beat subjects and approved quote, not invented first-person captions. English is the initial language. No recipes, salt values, timelines or food-safety instructions are manufactured.
