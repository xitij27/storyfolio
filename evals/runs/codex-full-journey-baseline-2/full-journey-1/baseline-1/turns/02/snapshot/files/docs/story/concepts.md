- Question: How do you check what firmware does on a real board?
- Form: bench checklist. Six chapters: Setup (name, question, contribution), Basic operation (bring-up meaning), Power states (scheduling and labelled teaching drawing), Observation (radio awake after failed transmission; cause/fix unknown), Evidence limits (projection/run, samples/test count, preliminary check), Handover (principle, email and CV).

- Three transformations: Setup brings the posts into a fixture assembly; Power states arranges the same posts beside the labelled teaching sequence; Evidence limits separates paired posts for projected/observed and assembled/tested distinctions. The fixture reunites at close.
- Opening: assembled fixture with Ada's name, contribution and question; identifies explanatory geometry immediately. Close: firmware decisions need physical observation, accompanied by email/CV.
- Stands on: confirmed fixture construction, firmware scheduling, working checklists, observed problem and explicitly bounded project evidence.
- Needs from Ada: concept preference only. No unavailable photo or third-party permission needed.
- Why theirs: the fixture connects Ada's own firmware and fixture contribution to Mosslink's specific evidence boundaries.
