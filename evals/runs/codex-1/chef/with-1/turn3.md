**The idea and the object**

The site follows the order of your work: origins, dates, recording, checking, readiness, then what comes next. The menu appears after the preparation because that is how you say the kitchen operates.

One assembly of **six paper log leaves** carries the story. They open, spread into a timeline, align as working records, become a grid of evidence, compare measurements and gather again.

This is a designed interpretation of your ferment log. We have not seen the actual document, so the site will not pretend to reproduce its layout, handwriting or entries. Its fields come from you: date started, salt, check days and readiness.

The 3D has a specific job in each chapter: establish sequence, explain the record, reveal work or compare evidence. All important information also appears as readable page text.

**1. Started — the opening**

Approximately 1½ desktop screens.

The first screen contains:

- **Ines Okafor**, Head Chef at Saltmarsh, Leeds.
- Your focus on fermentation and apprentices.
- The central question.
- A compact proof point: the kitchen’s weekly waste falling from 14 kg to 5 kg, with team credit and its measurement context.
- An immediately visible enquiry link.

Beside this, the six leaves sit in a shallow, slightly open stack. Your photograph of your grandmother’s ogiri pot occupies the first visible face when supplied. A second face names the books you identified.

As the visitor scrolls, those leaves gently separate. The opening connects the object to your stated influences without inventing a childhood scene or a lesson you have not described.

**Visitors can:** jump to any chapter or go directly to enquiries. They do not need to wait for the opening animation.

**The look**

The selected palette is **Log paper**:

| Use | Colour |
|---|---|
| Background | Warm paper — `#F2EBDD` |
| Text | Dark ink — `#252A24` |
| Links and emphasis | Olive — `#46563D` |

The leaves have matte surfaces, faint ruling, subtle grain and soft edge shadows. Photographs provide the colour variation. Texture stays quiet enough to preserve readability.

Two typefaces:

- **Source Sans 3:** headings, paragraphs, navigation and buttons.
- **IBM Plex Mono:** dates, log fields and measured values.

Body text is approximately 18–20 px with comfortable line lengths. Headings have presence without overwhelming the photographs or records.

Two alternative palette studies remain documented: pale larder-label stock with green-blue accents, and cream paper with clay accents. The selected direction is Log paper; there is no need for a visitor-facing theme switcher.

**How scrolling and clicking feel**

On desktop, one scene stays alongside the text through the chapters. Scrolling changes its arrangement, with readable resting positions between movements.

The whole experience is approximately 8½ screens, adjusted to the actual content. Scrolling remains native: no forced slides, scroll interception or timed waits.

A slim navigation rail links to every chapter. Direct chapter URLs work. A contact shortcut stays easy to reach.

All actions have conventional buttons or links. Visitors never need to drag a 3D object to uncover essential information.

**On a phone, and without 3D**

| Situation | Experience |
|---|---|
| **Phone** | One column, with a compact chapter illustration above the text. The chronology becomes vertical, the evidence grid becomes stacked cards, and the two waste figures remain easy to compare. |
| **Small phone** | Static chapter illustrations by default. No prolonged pinned scene or sideways reading. |
| **Reduced motion** | Static arrangements replace scroll-linked movement. The chapter order and evidence stay intact. |
| **No WebGL / 3D unavailable** | Still images and ordinary paper-like panels replace the scene. All content and links work. |
| **No JavaScript** | A complete document with headings, photographs, native disclosure controls, chapter anchors and contact links. |

Touch targets are at least 44 px. No interaction depends on hovering. Keyboard focus is visible, images have useful descriptions, and the page supports zoom and reflow. Screen readers receive the complete text without duplicate narration from the decorative rendering.
