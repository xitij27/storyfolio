# Concepts — choice delegated to assistant

Ines delegated unanswered design decisions in U1k. Selected: **The ferment log**. Selection is recorded here before the storyboard; final storyboard review remains pending.

Trade forms considered: ferment log, prep list, recipe sheet, order sheet, costing sheet, menu, workshop handout. Ines explicitly works from the first two. The menu is written last (U1j), so a tasting-menu structure would misrepresent her process.

## 1. The ferment log — selected
- Question: **What happens before the menu is written?**
- Form and chapters: Started (opening/influences); Date (who/where); Salt (method); Checks (work); Ready (outcome/testimony); Next batch (close/invitation). Salt means an actual log field, not a metaphor for personality. Date and Started deliberately separate origin from dated experience. Ready includes the consequences of the documented kitchen practice, not a fabricated batch result. Next batch is the website's closing extension to the form.
- Object: six thin paper log leaves, one persistent assembly from opening to close. These are a designed interpretation of the log; its actual binding and layout are unknown. Each leaf carries real sourced words or an owned photograph. Simple planes with thickness and restrained paper bends.
- Transformations: a dated line in Date; a spread of real log fields in Salt; an inspection grid in Checks. Every movement reveals, orders or compares evidence. All six remain in the scene, with the inactive leaves as a small stack.
- Opening: pot photograph, when supplied, and the named influences on the first exposed leaves establish where Ines says she learned. No claim that the pot itself started her fermentation practice. Close: the leaves return to a working stack, with the invitation routes in accessible HTML alongside it.
- Sources: F1–F3, F10–F17, F19–F20, U1a–U1j; omit unconfirmed metrics.
- Needs: owned pot/larder photographs; contact destinations. Restaurant photos and Brine illustrations are optional and blocked on permission. Text faces carry the design without them.
- Why hers: the log's salt, checks and readiness lead to a menu explicitly written last, alongside her ogiri-pot influence and accurately qualified kitchen result.

## 2. The prep list
- Question: **What work comes before a week’s menu?**
- Form and chapters: Date (opening/identity); Tasks (practice); Order of work (experience); Checks (examples); Complete (outcome/quote); Next list (close). These are proposed editorial headings, not claimed headings from her actual list.
- Object: six narrow task-list slips forming one prep-list bundle; each carries actual supplied material about the larder, log, teaching or preserving.
- Transformations: chronological strip for experience; ordered checklist for method; paired before/after sheets for the confirmed waste measurements.
- Opening: a slip names her grandmother and books beside her owned pot photograph. Close: the bundle collects into the next prep list beside the three contact routes.
- Sources: F1–F3, F10–F17, F19–F20, U1a–U1j. No invented task timings or completed checkboxes claiming real events.
- Needs: an actual redacted prep-list sample for richer faces, plus owned images and contact destinations. Text-only slips work without the sample.
- Why hers: the prep list is her declared operating document, and the menu follows its work rather than leading the story.
- Tradeoff: immediately legible, but less able to explain the waiting and repeated checking recorded in her ferment log.

## 3. The log on the shelf
- Question: **What does it take to know a ferment is ready?**
- Form and chapters: Started (opening); Date (background); Salt (method); Check days (work); Ready (outcomes); Next entry (close). The last two must distinguish documented practice and kitchen-wide outcomes from any individual batch.
- Object: six folded jar-label sleeves, laid together as a record set; labels translate the confirmed log fields. This is an interpretive display, not a claim that her real labels have these fields.
- Transformations: ordered date tabs; an unfolded field diagram; a shelf-like row of photographic records. No jars materialise into dishes and no invented recipe is demonstrated.
- Opening: the sleeve faces expose the owned pot photograph and influences. Close: the sleeves return to an ordered set beside invitations to learn or enquire.
- Sources: F2, F16, F19–F20, U1a–U1j.
- Needs: readable, cleared label photographs and a real redacted entry to go beyond a field-level explanation. Without these, it risks promising a batch story the material cannot yet support.
- Why hers: her jar labels, checking schedule and cupboard-sized koji room provide the substance, rather than a chef's emblem.
- Tradeoff: more tactile than the log, but a narrower readiness question needs a specific batch record we do not have.

## Selection and tests
Choose 1 over 2 and 3: it can tell the complete story with confirmed material now; the log gives scroll a reason to unfold and revisit evidence. The question has a beginning (origins), middle (recording/checking) and answer (the menu follows preparation and readiness).

All three use multiple states of one assembly, with evidence on its faces. The accessible HTML and static evidence preserve meaning when 3D is absent; deleting the evidence itself would remove meaning. No occupational emblem, borrowed tasting-menu narrative, or unrelated sequence of effects. A persistent identity/proof/contact summary handles the thirty-second visit.
