**The visual language.** Use warm paper (`#F2EBDD`), near-black ink (`#252722`), muted larder green (`#4E5B45`) and a restrained brine amber (`#A45A2A`). Paper and ink carry the reading experience; green and amber identify links, labels and small accents. Check final text combinations for accessible contrast.

Set headlines in **Newsreader**, with its literary, slightly irregular character, and body copy in **Public Sans** for clarity. Small factual labels can use the sans serif with tabular numerals. Actual handwriting should come from your labels and scans, rather than a handwriting font.

Pair carefully shot dishes with your phone photographs of the larder. That difference in polish tells a useful story. Keep texture subtle and preserve clean reading surfaces. Credit Priya Nair’s photography wherever appropriate.

**How the motion should feel.** Slow, tactile and limited to meaningful transitions: approaching a jar, connecting a process to a dish, separating the zine covers. The opening is the main 3D moment. Standard scrolling, visible navigation and ordinary links remain available throughout. No loading sequence before your name appears, custom cursor, or scroll hijacking.

**On a phone.** Use a single-column story with food and labels large enough to inspect. Show *Brine* covers as a simple grid with tap-to-read pages. Avoid horizontal scroll sections and hover-dependent information.

Reduced-motion mode gets still compositions and simple transitions. The complete site must remain readable and navigable without WebGL, using keyboard controls and assistive technology.
