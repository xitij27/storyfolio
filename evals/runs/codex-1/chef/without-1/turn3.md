The site should be called **“Before service”**, with a simple organising idea: **the prep list leads to the ferment log, the ferment log helps shape the food, and the menu is written last.**

Visitors follow that sequence through your work. Prospective apprentices see how the kitchen operates, workshop visitors get a clear booking route, and food writers find specific stories and accurately credited material. The 3D makes the working process tangible; the records, photographs and your voice carry the substance.

This is the complete design and content plan. Nothing will be built at this stage.

**1. Header and navigation**

A compact header shows **Ines Okafor** on the left, with links to **The kitchen**, **Apprenticeships**, **Workshops**, **Brine**, and **About & press**. “Book a workshop” is the most prominent action.

On desktop, the header stays available as a slim bar while scrolling. Anchor links move directly to the relevant section, with headings positioned below the bar.

- **3D:** None in the navigation.
- **Visitors can click:** Your name to return to the top, each section link, and the workshop action.
- **Phone:** Your name, a workshop link, and a labelled menu button. The expanded menu uses ordinary text links.
- **Without 3D:** Identical navigation and behaviour.

**2. Opening: the prep list**

The first screen introduces you without requiring a visitor to wait for an animation:

> Ines Okafor  
> Head Chef at Saltmarsh, Leeds.  
> **The menu is the last thing we write.**

Supporting copy names your practice: fermentation, whole-vegetable cooking and teaching apprentices.

Beside the text is a selected extract from a real prep list, with a jar partly visible behind it. Use an approved extract containing enough detail to feel specific, with supplier prices and private information excluded.

- **3D:** One jar, based on an actual vessel and label, sits in a shallow scene. Early scrolling moves the viewpoint slightly closer while the prep list shifts aside. Your introduction remains readable throughout.
- **Visitors can click:** “See how the kitchen works”, “Apprenticeships”, or “Book a workshop”. A labelled motion control allows animation to be paused.
- **Phone:** Introduction and actions come first; the prep list and jar composition follow. Start with a lightweight still image and enhance only when appropriate.
- **Without 3D:** A photograph or rendered still accompanies the same text. Nothing is withheld.

Avoid a grand theatrical entrance. The first impression should be a working kitchen with a distinctive point of view.

**3. The working larder: from task to record**

The opening jar becomes one specific example from your larder. Beside it, a readable log shows the fields you actually use: when it was started, its salt entry, scheduled checks, and readiness.

The content follows three steps:

1. What came into the kitchen and why you kept it.
2. What you did and what you checked.
3. How you decided it was ready and where it went.

Use one complete, approved example as the main story. Two additional examples offer breadth without turning the homepage into an exhaustive catalogue of 40-plus jars.

- **3D:** The jar turns a small amount to bring its label into view, then settles. As the visitor scrolls, the relevant log entries are highlighted alongside it. The contents do not magically “ferment” in response to scrolling.
- **Visitors can click:** Named buttons to switch between the three examples, a label photograph to enlarge it, and “Read the full record” to open an accessible detail view.
- **Phone:** Show the jar image, explanation and log vertically. Example selectors are large text buttons. All key information is readable without opening the detail view.
- **Without 3D:** A still image changes with the selected example. The same log and narrative remain available.

Scheduled checks and completed observations must look different. Explain specialist terms briefly in the text. Published records are examples of your practice; they should not appear to be complete home-preserving instructions.

Include the small human detail here: **“The koji room. It’s a cupboard.”** Pair it with your photograph if a suitable one exists.

**4. The food: the menu arrives last**

Now the page opens out into generous dish photography. Select three dishes that make the connection between the larder and the plate especially clear.

Each entry contains:

- The dish name and date or season.
- A finished-dish image.
- A relevant process image or log detail.
- A short explanation of the ingredient and decision that shaped the dish.

The photographs should make the food desirable, while the captions explain why it belongs in your portfolio.

- **3D:** The jar scene ends as this section begins. No new 3D scene is introduced. On desktop, a process image can stay briefly in place while the matching dish and caption move alongside it.
- **Visitors can click:** Each dish to expand its story and photographs; a secondary link to Saltmarsh for current restaurant information.
- **Phone:** Process and finished dish appear in sequence, with captions immediately beside their images. No pinned image or horizontal scrolling.
- **Without 3D:** The section is unchanged.

These are dated examples of your work, not an implied current menu. Saltmarsh remains the source for restaurant bookings and current dining information.

**5. What the kitchen changes**

A quieter section brings together the larder’s scale and the waste measurement. Use plain typography, a photograph of the working kitchen, and a small comparison graphic.

Suggested wording:

> We reduced whole-kitchen prep and plate waste from 14 kg to 5 kg a week, measured over one month in 2023. Bones were excluded because they went to stock.

Keep the date, scope and exclusions next to the figures. A separate line can describe the **40+ ferments, vinegars and garums on rotation**, once you have confirmed that figure is still suitable to publish.

- **3D:** None. The comparison uses two simple, directly labelled bars, without an animated counter.
- **Visitors can click:** An optional “How we measured it” disclosure, if you can supply useful supporting detail.
- **Phone:** The comparison and explanation stack cleanly.
- **Without 3D:** Identical.

This is a shared kitchen result. The writing consistently says “we” for that achievement.

**6. Apprenticeships: learning the kitchen**

This is one of the page’s main destinations. Show what joining the kitchen involves: prep lists, labelling, ferment checks, staff meal, service and the relationship between preparation and the weekly menu.

Include your teaching approach, a realistic account of the work, and the programme’s confirmed history: two apprentices a year since 2023.

Your own stage belongs here as a short passage:

> In 2018, I spent ten weeks on an unpaid stage at Fjaerland Fermentation Lab, washing and labelling jars and helping with two batches of garum.

- **3D:** None. Use documentary photographs of workspaces, tools and hands, with restrained transitions.
- **Visitors can click:** “Apprenticeship details”, then the current application or enquiry route.
- **Phone:** A short introduction leads into practical details and clearly labelled questions and answers.
- **Without 3D:** Identical.

Before launch, this section needs confirmed information about eligibility, pay, duration, working hours, support, the application process and current availability. Your unpaid stage must remain clearly separate from the terms of your own apprentice programme.

Staff faces appear only after you have asked the people concerned. Photos of hands and workspaces should also be checked for identifying or private details.

**7. Workshops: learn with Ines**

Give workshops a full section with a clear practical structure. Begin with a short description of what you teach, followed by the next confirmed sessions.

Each listing shows its title, explicit date and time, location, duration, price, availability and a brief account of what participants will do. “First Monday of the month” describes the usual rhythm; published dates confirm the actual schedule.

- **3D:** None in the listings or booking flow.
- **Visitors can click:** A workshop to read its full details, then “Book this workshop”. Provide an enquiry route for accessibility, dietary or other participation questions.
- **Phone:** Single-column workshop cards with obvious booking buttons.
- **Without 3D:** Identical.

Workshop detail pages should explain the intended experience level, what is provided, what participants take home, venue access, relevant food or allergen information, and cancellation terms.

If no dates are available, show that plainly and offer a workshop enquiry link. Do not leave an expired event presented as bookable.

**8. Brine: preserving in print**

Introduce *Brine* as your zine about preserving, with four issues published from 2022 to 2025. A short passage explains its relationship to your kitchen practice.

With Olu’s permission, show the four covers and selected spreads. Credit his drawings visibly.

- **3D:** A small paper-depth effect lets the four covers separate gently as the section appears. The covers then remain still and selectable. This should be a lightweight effect rather than a second substantial 3D scene.
- **Visitors can click:** Each issue to open its own reading page. Offer accessible text and a downloadable PDF where permissions and production quality allow.
- **Phone:** A simple cover grid or vertical list. Reading pages use normal scrolling, with optional enlarged scans.
- **Without 3D:** Static covers with the same links.

If permission is pending, publish a text-only introduction and issue information you have verified. Do not use scans containing Olu’s drawings as placeholders.

**The look**

Use a warm paper background, near-black text and restrained colour:

| Role | Colour |
|---|---|
| Main background | Warm paper `#F2EBDD` |
| Main text | Dark ink `#252722` |
| Links and section accents | Larder green `#4E5B45` |
| Selected details | Brine amber `#A45A2A` |

Check the final combinations for accessible contrast, especially small labels and button states.

Use **Newsreader** for expressive headlines and personal passages, with **Public Sans** for body text, navigation and records. Log entries use aligned numerals and clear units. Actual handwriting comes from your labels; essential information also appears as readable text.

The composition alternates between close, information-rich records and spacious photographs. Priya’s polished dish images and your phone photographs serve different parts of that rhythm. Texture comes primarily from the material itself: paper, tape, glass and food.

**Motion, phones and accessibility**

The opening jar and log form the principal 3D sequence. Elsewhere, movement stays modest. Ordinary scrolling remains under the visitor’s control, and every section can be reached through navigation.

On phones, preserve the complete content and simplify the staging: a vertical sequence, large labels, no hover requirements and no horizontal scroll narratives. Forms and bookings use familiar controls.

For reduced-motion preferences, disabled JavaScript, unavailable WebGL or less capable devices, show still compositions and ordinary document flow. Load the introduction and useful links immediately; 3D arrives later and must never block them. Essential text lives in the page, outside the 3D canvas.

Keyboard navigation, visible focus, descriptive image text, accessible disclosures, readable zoomed layouts and screen-reader testing are launch requirements.

**Assumptions I have made**

- Workshops are the primary action; apprenticeship information is equally prominent in the navigation. Press information is easy to find but does not dominate the opening.
- You want one substantial homepage with focused supporting pages, rather than a large archive at launch.
- Existing booking infrastructure should be reused if available.
- The first release does not need a shop, newsletter, visitor accounts or a live feed from the ferment log.
- Published records will be selected historical examples, not a promise that the site reflects today’s kitchen.
- Only the two text documents have been reviewed so far. Final photography, jar modelling and image crops depend on reviewing the actual assets.
- Unconfirmed availability or permission will lead to an honest text treatment or enquiry route, not invented content.

Before implementation, the review package should contain the full page copy, an annotated desktop and phone layout, the selected assets with their permissions, and a storyboard of the jar-to-log sequence. That makes the proposed experience concrete enough to approve while keeping this stage entirely within planning.
