# Three concepts

User delegated outstanding choices in F24. Selected: A, The ferment log. These are proposed design forms, not claims about the exact physical format of Ines's existing documents.

Trade forms considered: ferment log, prep list, weekly menu, recipe sheet, ordering sheet, station handover and workshop handout. Ines explicitly works from the first two and writes the menu last (F23). All three candidates use her confirmed forms; none uses a tasting-menu structure.

## A — The ferment log (selected)

- **Question:** What happens before the menu is written?
- **Form:** A ferment log expanded into six chapters: Started (opening, who and where from); Salt (method); Checks (work and teaching); Ready (work in view); Review (outcome and testimony, explicitly a website addition to the log); Next batch (answer and contact, also an editorial addition).
- **Object:** Six paper log leaves, recognisable as a single open, clipped log at rest. Flat sheets and a simple binding; real words and available personal photographs on their faces. Six is a design choice, not a claimed batch or project count. Log columns come from F23; we do not claim that these are scans of her actual log.
- **Transformations:** At Started the leaves separate into a short sequence of influences and dated experience; at Checks they align as log columns; at Review two leaves hold the measured weekly values with the scope beside them. All six remain the same pieces; inactive leaves remain gathered at the edge.
- **Opening:** Open log, Ines's identity, question and confirmed kitchen result; first leaf introduces grandmother and the named books as influences, with her own pot photograph if supplied. No invented childhood scene or book-cover reproduction.
- **Close:** Leaves return to the open log. The final leaf carries the relationship between prep, checking and menu-last, plus routes to workshops, apprenticeship enquiries and writing enquiries. No imaginary menu dishes.
- **Stands on:** F01, F03–F05, F10, F15–F16, F18, F20–F24.
- **Needs:** Actual personal image files for richer faces; safe log excerpt optional, not required. Real contact and workshop destinations before launch. No dependence on Priya's photographs or Olu's artwork.
- **Why hers:** The relationship between her ferment log, menu-last practice, teaching and a carefully attributed kitchen waste result makes the page specific to Ines.

## B — The prep list

- **Question:** What belongs on a prep list before it belongs on a menu?
- **Form:** A proposed expanded prep list: Date (opening and current role); Carry-over (influences and stage); Tasks (method); Checks (larder and teaching); Completed (outcomes and quotation); Handover (answer and contact). These are proposed chapter names, not asserted headings in her real list.
- **Object:** Six long paper prep-list slips, gathered under one clip. Simple rectangular strips carry her words, personal larder images and the measured result. We would explain that this is an editorial rendering.
- **Transformations:** Carry-over lays the slips into a dated sequence; Tasks separates them into parallel activities without invented staff assignments; Completed aligns the result and quotation beside checked rows. The rows describe site chapters, never fictional completed kitchen jobs.
- **Opening:** The clipped list introduces Ines and her emphasis on before-service work, with influences on its first slip.
- **Close:** Slips gather into a complete list; the last item is the menu being written, followed by audience-specific contact links.
- **Stands on:** F01, F03–F05, F10, F15–F16, F18, F21–F24.
- **Needs:** Her own photographs and contact destinations; an actual cleared prep-list excerpt would make the object more personal. No fabricated task durations, quantities or handwriting.
- **Why hers:** The longest document in the story would be prep, while the menu arrives last, connecting her stated practice to the kitchen's measured waste result and teaching.

## C — The hanging log

- **Question:** What can a jar's record tell someone learning to cook?
- **Form:** Her ferment-log sequence rendered as movable labels: Started (identity and influences); Salt (method); Checked (personal stage work and checking); Ready (larder and preserving); Recorded (kitchen-wide outcome and quotation); Start again (answer, apprentices and workshops). Recorded and Start again are explicit editorial extensions.
- **Object:** Six rectangular paper tags gathered on a ring, an illustrative portable version of the log rather than a claim that she uses this exact format. Tags use real supplied words, personal photographs and log-field names, with no invented recipes or batch records.
- **Transformations:** Started fans the tags to show influences; Checked aligns them as sequential inspection fields; Ready makes a readable contact sheet of the work represented. No jar wall, cupboard door or separate scale appears as another animated object.
- **Opening:** Her own larder photo on the first tag establishes where labels belong; text names her grandmother and books as influences without implying that this particular tagging system came from them.
- **Close:** Tags return to their ring and present learning and visiting routes; the answer points to records, checks and readiness as parts of her practice.
- **Stands on:** F01, F03–F05, F10, F15–F16, F18, F21–F24.
- **Needs:** Legible cleared personal label photographs would strengthen it; the plan works with typographic field names until then. Contact destinations still needed.
- **Why hers:** Jar labelling during an honestly described stage connects to her named ferment-log fields and her present apprentice audience.

## Choice and tests

A wins over B because the log supplies a confirmed beginning-to-end sequence; the prep list's exact structure is unknown. A wins over C because a readable log can hold the work and evidence without inventing a tag system or making small text too important on phones.

All three pass the swap test through F23's menu-last practice and Ines-specific evidence. Their pieces carry the actual content relationships, so deleting them requires an equivalent static structure, rather than merely deleting a background. Each begins with influences and work, proceeds through method, and ends with results and a relevant invitation. None relies on a chef emblem. No candidate invents recipes, outcomes, research credentials or cleared rights. Every opening reserves space for identity, work, proof and direct contact access.
