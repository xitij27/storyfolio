The strongest idea is **“Before service”**: a portfolio that takes us inside the work that happens before a plate reaches the table. Your jars, scraps, handwritten labels, apprentices and zine become the visual language.

Your material has a clear point of view: good cooking starts with paying attention to what other people discard. That gives the site a reason to be memorable beyond its effects. It should feel like stepping into your kitchen, with the care and curiosity still visible.

I’ve reviewed both files in `material`. The photographs and scans are described there, but aren’t included, so the image choices below are proposed art direction. Nothing has been built.

**The idea**

Open with a dimensional view of the larder, then follow its contents into your cooking, teaching and community work. Time is the connection: fermentation takes time; so does learning to cook and building a kitchen culture.

The 3D element should be a small, tactile arrangement of jars inspired by your actual jar wall. Real photography carries the food and people. Handwritten details come from your actual labels and Brine, rather than a decorative handwriting font.

The site’s main job is to let someone understand your cooking, see evidence of your work, and find the right way to contact you. Saltmarsh bookings should be a clearly labelled secondary route.

**Colour and type**

Use the palette of a working larder, with final colours adjusted after seeing the photographs:

- **Warm paper `#F2EBDD`** for most backgrounds.
- **Ink `#252A24`** for text.
- **Deep seaweed `#34483C`** for the opening and occasional darker passages.
- **Brine yellow `#D8BE64`** for small markers and label details.
- **Oxide red `#9B4937`** sparingly, for issue numbers or annotations.

Keep text on solid surfaces so the food photography can stay rich and varied. Check all text and interactive colour combinations for accessible contrast.

Use **Newsreader** for large, expressive titles and longer quotations, paired with **IBM Plex Sans** for body copy, navigation and captions. A little **IBM Plex Mono** can evoke batch labels where there is real batch information to show. Avoid distressed textures everywhere; the photographs and scans will provide plenty of character.

The writing should sound direct and specific: “The koji room is a cupboard” is more distinctive than grand claims about culinary innovation.

**How the movement earns its place**

Reserve the most ambitious movement for the opening and the transition into the real larder. Perspective reveals the collection; the cooking stories reveal transformation; the zine stack introduces a body of published work.

Use ordinary browser scrolling, short transitions and restrained image movement. Keep navigation available: **Cooking, Kitchen, Brine, About, Contact**. Links should work independently of the animation, with no sound or custom cursor competing for attention.

**On a phone**

The sequence stays intact, but the composition changes:

- The opening becomes a strong still image or a lightweight dimensional scene, depending on device capability.
- Side-by-side stories become single-column image-and-caption sequences.
- The larder’s pinned desktop passage becomes three normal sections.
- Zines open as readable pages, with scans that can be enlarged.
- Navigation and contact links remain easy to reach and tap.

Reduced-motion settings should replace animated transitions with still compositions. If 3D cannot load, a prepared image takes its place without hiding any content. The first screen should load promptly; later images and 3D assets load only when needed.

**What else the site needs**

The homepage should be supported by individual pages for the selected cooking stories and each Brine issue, plus a practical workshop page if bookings or enquiries are part of your remit. Workshops need a confirmed location, time, cost, accessibility information and contact or booking route. “First Monday of the month” alone isn’t enough to publish a reliable event listing.

Before implementation, the content work is to:

- Select photographs and scans, confirm permission to reuse them, and agree credits for Priya and Olu.
- Write three real cooking stories and three larder entries, with accurate ingredients, processes and outcomes.
- Confirm the career dates: LinkedIn lists Head Chef from March 2022; your notes say you took over the kitchen in September 2022. Those may describe different milestones.
- Confirm contact details, external links, workshop arrangements and permissions for identifiable people in photographs.

The eventual build should include keyboard access, useful image descriptions, readable text versions of zine excerpts, search and social previews, and a simple way to update workshops and add work. Keep the weekly Saltmarsh menu linked to its maintained source.

The defining impression should be **a chef who sees possibility in what is already there**. The dimensional opening draws people in; your work gives them a reason to stay.
