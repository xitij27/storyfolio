The site should be called **“Before service”**: a journey through your kitchen in the order the work actually happens—ferment log, prep list, then menu—followed by the people you teach, the workshops you run and the influences behind it.

Its character comes from your real records, photographs and voice. The 3D makes the larder tangible; the substance is your cooking and the shared work behind it.

This is the full design plan. No building is included.

## 1. Overall structure

A substantial homepage tells the story. Dedicated pages let visitors go directly to practical information or read further.

The three main visitor routes are:

| Visitor | What they should find quickly | Main action |
|---|---|---|
| Prospective apprentices | What working and learning in your kitchen involves, and whether places are available | Apprentice enquiry or application |
| Workshop guests | Upcoming sessions, what they’ll learn and booking information | Book a workshop |
| Food writers | Your approach, specific examples, accurate biography and image information | Press enquiry |

The header contains **Ines Okafor**, **The kitchen**, **Apprentices**, **Workshops**, **Brine** and **About**. A distinct **Workshop dates** link leads directly to practical information. Contact stays available in the menu and footer.

Use normal browser scrolling, real links and shareable page addresses throughout.

## 2. The homepage, in order

### 01 — Before service

**What it shows**

Your name, **“Head Chef at Saltmarsh, Leeds”**, and this proposed opening:

> We start with the prep list and the ferment log. The menu comes last.

A short supporting sentence introduces fermentation, whole-vegetable cooking and teaching. Keep the first screen spacious enough to read immediately.

**What the 3D does**

A small arrangement of jars, based on your actual larder, sits beside the text. Glass, cloudy contents, paper labels and shelf shadows should feel handled and ordinary.

A modest perspective shift during the first scroll reveals the space between the jars. One jar comes forward slightly, bringing its label into focus. There is no endlessly spinning object or simulated laboratory.

**What visitors can click**

- **Inside the kitchen** scrolls to the ferment log.
- **Workshop dates** goes to the workshop page.
- Header links work immediately.

The jars are visual storytelling, not essential controls.

**Phone and fallback**

Text comes first, with a compact jar composition below. The default phone treatment is a prepared still from the same composition, keeping the introduction fast and visually consistent.

### 02 — The ferment log

**What it shows**

Introduce the larder and its **40+ ferments, vinegars and garums on rotation**. Use one approved real entry to explain the fields you track:

**Date started · Salt · Check days · Ready**

Pair it with your own working photographs and brief explanations of what you observe. Two further entries provide breadth without turning the homepage into a database.

Do not fabricate dates, salt quantities or readiness notes for visual effect.

**What the 3D does**

The opening jar remains briefly on one side of the desktop layout while successive log fields appear beside it. Small markers connect the label to the relevant text.

The jar then gives way to a real larder photograph. Its contents do not visibly “ferment” as the user scrolls: the animation follows a record of the work.

This is the end of the main 3D sequence.

**What visitors can click**

- **Read this larder story** opens a detailed account.
- Selected photographs can open at a larger size, with captions and credits.

All key information is readable without selecting anything.

**Phone and fallback**

Each entry becomes an image followed by its log and explanation. No sticky panel, horizontal scrolling or hidden hover content. The same sequence works with a still jar image.

### 03 — The prep list

**What it shows**

Explain how you decide what needs attention before service. Show approved excerpts of real prep lists alongside photographs of ingredients, containers and work surfaces.

Use transcribed excerpts to protect private information and keep the content readable. Supplier prices never appear.

Place the team’s waste result here:

> Over one month in 2023, we weighed the whole kitchen’s prep and plate waste. We brought it down from 14 kg to 5 kg a week. Bones weren’t included; they go to stock.

Present the numbers with that explanation, not as a context-free counter or a claim about current performance.

Include Callum’s approved quotation:

> “Ines turned our bins into a larder. The best thing on the menu most weeks started as something we used to throw away.”

**What the 3D does**

None. The scene resolves into a flat, editorial layout, echoing a prep sheet. Short text and image transitions are enough.

**What visitors can click**

- Links from selected prep entries to the relevant cooking stories.
- An accessible image enlargement where handwriting is worth examining.

There are no checkboxes that imply visitors are operating the kitchen’s live prep list.

**Phone and fallback**

One excerpt and photograph at a time. The waste explanation stays next to the figures at every screen size.

### 04 — Then, the menu

**What it shows**

Three selected cooking stories, each following:

**What we started with → What we did → What reached the table**

Pair working photographs with finished-dish photography. Each story should explain a real decision: why an ingredient was kept, how preservation changed its use, or how readiness affected the eventual dish.

Choose the dishes after reviewing the actual photographs and records. The supplied material does not identify them yet.

**What the 3D does**

None. Use still photography at generous scale, with restrained changes in crop or placement as it enters the viewport. Food should remain easy to study.

**What visitors can click**

Each story opens a dedicated page containing:

- The finished dish and its context.
- Ingredients and process at an appropriate level of detail.
- Relevant larder or prep records.
- Credits for the people and photography involved.

A secondary **Visit Saltmarsh** link leads to the restaurant’s current website. Keep its changing menu at its maintained source.

**Phone and fallback**

Stack the image pairs in narrative order, with captions directly underneath. Do not make comparison depend on dragging a slider.

## 3. The look

**Colour**

| Colour | Role |
|---|---|
| Warm paper `#F2EBDD` | Main reading background |
| Ink `#252A24` | Body text and navigation |
| Deep seaweed `#34483C` | Opening background and selected dark passages |
| Brine yellow `#D8BE64` | Small markers and emphasis |
| Oxide red `#9B4937` | Occasional annotations and issue numbers |

These are starting colours; adjust them against the actual photography. Keep text on stable, solid backgrounds and verify contrast for every functional pairing.

**Type**

- **Newsreader:** expressive titles, introductions and quotations.
- **IBM Plex Sans:** body text, navigation and practical information.
- **IBM Plex Mono:** limited use for genuine log fields and dates.

Use comfortable body sizes and relatively narrow reading columns. Real handwriting can appear in photographs and scans, with a readable transcription nearby.

**Composition and imagery**

Use the alignment and rules of kitchen records as the organising device, with generous photographic interruptions. Alternate your working photographs with Priya’s finished-dish images deliberately: each shows a different part of the same work.

Avoid generic food imagery, invented stains and decorative labels containing fictitious information. Warmth comes from your material.

## 4. Motion, phones and resilience

The site needs only one principal 3D scene, spanning the opening and ferment log. Load it after the essential text and prepared image. Reuse it across those sections, stop rendering when it leaves view, and release it when no longer needed.

The proposed performance target is a usable first screen within roughly 2.5 seconds on a representative mobile connection, validated during implementation. The 3D must never delay navigation or content.

Across the site:

- Preserve native scrolling and browser back behaviour.
- Use no audio, scroll hijacking or compulsory intro.
- Keep essential information out of hover-only interactions.
- Honour reduced-motion preferences with still compositions and immediate state changes.
- Provide a prepared image if 3D is unavailable, disabled or fails.
- Keep all content and links available independently of the canvas.
- Ensure menus, image viewers and controls work by keyboard, with visible focus.
- Avoid fixed-height sections that clip text when someone enlarges it.

On phones, default to the static opening, single-column stories and ordinary vertical scrolling. The distinctive design survives through photography, typography and sequence.

## 5. Everything else the site needs

### Supporting pages

- Three initial cooking stories.
- Selected larder stories.
- Apprenticeship information, including current status.
- Workshop listing and individual workshop pages.
- Brine index and four issue pages.
- Press information with an approved short biography, contact details and photography terms.
- Privacy, accessibility and credits information.
- A useful not-found page.

### Content and assets

- An inventory of available photographs and scans, with ownership and permission status.
- Approved restaurant photography with Priya’s credit.
- Your selected phone photographs and pot photograph.
- Olu’s permission before using his drawings, including scans that contain them.
- Staff clearance before publishing identifiable faces.
- Three real cooking stories and selected log and prep excerpts.
- Your own account of your grandmother’s influence.
- Confirmed apprentice terms, availability and enquiry process.
- Confirmed workshop logistics and booking details.
- Verified contact addresses and external links.

Review source images as well as captions for supplier prices, private notes and identifying details. Public downloads need the same review.

### Publishing and maintenance

A small content editor should let you update workshops, apprentice availability, stories and Brine without changing the design. Records should include image credits, alternative text and permission status.

Workshop listings need a clear past-event state and a straightforward way to mark sessions sold out or cancelled. Apprentice availability should be easy to update independently of the main page.

### Technical foundations

- Domain, hosting, HTTPS and an agreed owner for renewals.
- Responsive image sizes, modern image formats and lazy loading below the opening.
- Optimised, self-hosted fonts with readable fallbacks.
- Search titles, descriptions, canonical addresses and sitemap.
- Social sharing images and accurate event information.
- Accessible text equivalents for scanned material.
- Backups, preview-before-publish and a simple recovery process.
- Checks across common phones, browsers, keyboard use, zoom and reduced motion.
- Contact delivery checks; if a form is added, minimal fields, spam protection and clear success/error messages.
- Privacy information matched to the services actually used.

My recommendation is to launch with email enquiries and an existing workshop booking service. Add forms or tracking only when they meet a clear need.

## 6. Assumptions and decisions still to confirm

I’m assuming this is your personal site, with Saltmarsh clearly credited as your workplace; workshop payments remain with an existing booking service; and apprentice enquiries are welcome even when no vacancy is advertised.

I’m also assuming selected log entries can be published after review. They illustrate your practice rather than serving as complete fermentation instructions.

The photographs themselves still need reviewing. The cooking examples, workshop details, apprentice terms, contact information and exact personal narrative remain content tasks. Restaurant image reuse and Olu’s permission remain publication dependencies; your own photographs support the direction in the meantime.

The finished experience should leave visitors understanding **how you work, what they could learn with you, and how to take the next step**.
