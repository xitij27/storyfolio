The recommended direction is **“Making the safe thing easier”**: a portfolio structured around an ISBAR handover, showing how you observe clinical work, identify problems, and help teams improve systems.

It should feel precise, humane, and grounded in nursing. The memorable visual is a dimensional handover sheet that gradually becomes a clear, readable document as the visitor scrolls. Your evidence remains the main event.

This is the full design and content plan. No building is included.

**1. Navigation and first impression**

A compact navigation stays available at the top:

**Aoife Brennan** · **Work** · **Approach** · **About** · **Contact**

A separate **View CV** link gives hiring managers an immediate route to your qualifications. Your name returns to the top; the other links jump to sections using normal page scrolling.

ISBAR provides the underlying sequence, with small section labels pairing clinical and plain-language terms:

**Identify / Introduction → Situation / Clinical perspective → Background / Selected work → Assessment / Approach → Recommendation / Next role**

The navigation should not require visitors to understand ISBAR.

There is no introductory loading screen. Name, headline, role, and links appear immediately while the optional visual loads.

**4. Background: the flagship scanning case study**

This is the largest section and the strongest evidence for your next role.

Proposed title:

> **Why nurses skip the scan**

Open with the shared result:

> **Ward medication scanning rose from 71% to 94% over nine months.**
>
> A shared improvement involving pharmacy, IT, night staff, and me.

Immediately distinguish your contribution:

> **My part:** forty hours of observation, identifying three workarounds, and getting the scanners moved to the medication room door.

The section unfolds in four short stages.

**The investigation.** Explain the forty hours of observation and the study’s interviews with 22 nurses. Do not imply that you personally conducted all interviews unless confirmed.

**The findings.** Show three concise observations:

- Wristbands became difficult to scan after a shower.
- Scanners were stored away from where they were needed.
- Printed barcode sheets were being carried in pockets.

Give **“None was laziness”** typographic emphasis, followed by the finding that these workarounds saved nurses time. This demonstrates your ability to understand behavior before proposing a fix.

**The changes.** Match each finding to the reported intervention: changed wristband stock, scanner relocation, and stopping the printed barcode sheets. Attribute the overall work to the team; identify scanner relocation as your specific contribution.

**The result.** Repeat the two measured figures with their timeframe. Use a simple comparison with a shared scale, starting at zero. Label the change as **23 percentage points**. Do not create intermediate data points or imply a measured reduction in harm.

**3D behavior:** The handover composition gives way to a restrained spatial illustration of the scanner’s position relative to the medication room door. Scrolling moves a simple scanner marker to the door. This is explicitly a schematic, not an accurate ward floor plan. The other findings use static illustrations; they do not need additional 3D scenes.

Once the result appears, all movement stops so the reader can inspect it.

**Visitor actions:**

- **Read the full case study** — opens a dedicated page with context, your contribution, findings, interventions, evidence, and limitations.
- **Read the conference abstract** — opens an accessible text version credited to the Irish Nursing Informatics Conference 2025.
- A poster download appears only if an appropriate file is available.

**Phone and fallback:** Observation, change, and result appear in a straightforward vertical sequence. A static before/after schematic explains the scanner relocation. Team attribution remains next to the headline result at every screen size.

**The look across the whole site**

The visual language comes from clinical documents, carefully typeset and given room to breathe. It should suggest practical clarity without mimicking a hospital software interface.

| Element | Direction |
|---|---|
| Background | Warm paper — `#F4F1E9` |
| Main text | Deep ink — `#172C32` |
| Links and primary accent | Dark teal — `#246B68` |
| Supporting surfaces | Pale gray-green — `#DCE4DE` |
| Occasional annotations | Muted rust — `#A44732` |
| Editorial headlines | Newsreader |
| Body, navigation, labels | Source Sans 3 |
| Figures | Large numerals, plain labels, consistent alignment |

Final color pairings need contrast checks. Rust should read as an editorial accent, not a simulated clinical warning.

Use generous margins, thin rules, and a clear hierarchy. Avoid excessive cards, decorative medical icons, stock hospital photography, and oversized empty sections. Actual documents retain their original visual identity.

**Rules for motion and 3D**

The site uses one coherent document-based visual system, with the scanner schematic as a focused supporting illustration.

- Native scrolling throughout; no scroll hijacking.
- Short transitions that follow the reader’s progress.
- No prolonged pinned sections.
- No essential content available only through hover, dragging, or animation.
- No auto-rotating objects or continuous background movement.
- Links and text remain outside the 3D canvas.
- Off-screen animation stops.
- Reduced-motion settings produce a fully still experience.

Without WebGL, with JavaScript unavailable, or on a device where the visual would perform poorly, static artwork replaces it. The full narrative, links, documents, and evidence still work. This fallback is part of the design, not an error state.

**Assumptions behind this plan**

- Recruitment is the primary purpose, with hospital and healthcare software employers given equal weight.
- The two documented projects provide enough evidence for launch; a blog and additional case studies are unnecessary initially.
- “From 2026” remains accurate and will be reviewed before publication.
- The scanning percentages are reported ward-level results, without additional raw data available for analysis.
- Six-ward adoption is the supported handover outcome; time savings and safety outcomes are not established.
- A portrait is optional; the design remains complete without one.
- The early warning score chart and care plan demonstrate your clinical context, not authorship of those tools.
- No patient stories or patient-level data are needed anywhere in the experience.

The site’s strongest impression should be that **you can look closely at a difficult clinical workflow, explain what is happening, and help a team improve it**. The 3D makes that approach memorable; the case studies make it credible.
