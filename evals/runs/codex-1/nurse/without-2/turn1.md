**How the work earns attention**

The medication-scanning case should lead. Its title could be **“Understanding why nurses skip the scan.”**

Build its narrative around the strongest line in the poster: **“None was laziness.”** Follow it with the evidence:

- Forty hours of observation and interviews with 22 nurses.
- Three recurring workarounds, each saving between one and four minutes per round.
- Changes to scanner placement, wristband stock and the use of printed barcodes.
- Scanning rising from **71% to 94% over nine months**.

Show that as a simple before-and-after comparison. There are no monthly measurements in the supplied material, so don’t invent a smooth trend chart. Describe the change as a **23 percentage-point increase in scanning**, without implying that a reduction in medication errors was measured.

Credit the 2025 conference poster and distinguish your contribution from the wider team’s implementation.

The handover project should provide a different kind of evidence: close reading of an actual tool. Once the old and new sheets are available, explain two or three specific design decisions. Six-ward adoption is already a useful result; claims about time saved or fewer omissions need additional evidence.

The audit dashboards currently have too little detail for full case studies. Give them concise supporting mentions until examples and context are available.

**Colour, type and visual character**

Use a warm, readable palette:

| Colour | Role |
|---|---|

| **Ink — `#172D32`** | Body text, navigation and strong contrast |
| **Deep teal — `#246A67`** | Links, active states and diagram emphasis |
| **Clay — `#B5684E`** | Small markers and decorative annotations |

Teal and clay should support labels and shapes, never carry meaning alone. Check contrast in the actual combinations before launch.

Pair **Source Serif 4** for large editorial headlines with **Source Sans 3** for body copy, captions and interface text. The serif gives your own words presence; the sans keeps the evidence straightforward to read. Use large margins, fine rules, numbered figure captions and selective oversized figures.

The imagery should come from your work: permission-cleared documents, recreated diagrams, and perhaps a portrait. Avoid generic hospital photography. Put the visual ambition into composition, depth and transitions.

**How it holds up on a phone**

The mobile version gets its own composition:

- Replace the live 3D scene with a carefully framed still or lightweight illustration.
- Keep the headline, role and first work link visible early.
- Stack the case-study stages in normal reading order.
- Present the handover sheets as labelled “Before” and “After” views with readable annotations; avoid a precision slider on a narrow screen.
- Keep all explanations available without hovering, dragging or watching animation.
- Use native scrolling throughout, with no scroll hijacking.

For reduced-motion preferences, show settled compositions and all content immediately. Keyboard navigation, visible focus, semantic headings, generous touch targets and accessible document downloads belong in the initial design.
