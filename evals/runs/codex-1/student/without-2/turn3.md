The site will be built around **Mina’s job cards and workshop logbook**: what needed attention, what you found, what you did, and what the evidence shows.

It should feel like your own working record, edited for an engineering placement reviewer with two minutes. Real photographs, precise captions, and one purposeful 3D model will give it character. The homepage will contain enough information to assess you without opening another page.

This is the complete plan; no building is included.

The project record contains five short fields:

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| **Task** | The actual mounting requirements and redesign objective, supplied from your project account. |

| **Design decisions** | Two or three changes, illustrated and explained in your words. |

**3D:** The actual bracket model appears initially at a useful inspection angle. As the section enters view, it makes one short, controlled turn, then stops. On desktop, the model can remain beside the text for a brief section of scrolling; it must not trap the visitor in a long animation.

Annotations point to documented design changes. No invented stress colours, dramatic deformation, or decorative engineering measurements.

**Clicks:** Explore model, model controls, and **Read the design log**.

**The visual system**

The page should resemble a carefully edited working record, without reproducing a literal paper form.

| Element | Direction |
|---|---|
| **Background** | Warm paper `#F2EFE7`. |
| **Primary text** | Graphite `#202522`. |
| **Secondary graphics** | Muted steel `#66716E`, subject to contrast checks wherever used for text. |
| **Accent** | Burnt orange `#B64926`, potentially replaced by a suitable colour sampled from your bike. |
| **Titles** | Barlow Condensed, bold and compact. |
| **Body** | IBM Plex Sans, comfortably sized with generous line spacing. |
| **Small labels** | IBM Plex Mono sparingly, for dates, figure numbers, and measurements. |
| **Composition** | Large photographs, restrained rules, short records, and plenty of space. Vary the layout between projects. |

Use consistent photographic colour correction while retaining grease, scratches, and wear. Those details belong to the work. Avoid stock workshop imagery, fake handwritten notes, decorative calculations, and unrelated 3D objects.

Movement has three purposes: reveal a relevant detail, connect related views, or help inspect geometry. No custom cursor, loading spectacle, automatic spinning, or sound.

**Phone and non-3D behavior**

The mobile version is designed as a complete reading experience.

- Introduction and primary links come before the opening photograph.
- Navigation stays compact, with a clearly labelled menu for secondary links if needed.
- Job-card fields stack vertically with their labels visible.
- All pinned behavior is removed.
- Photographs use deliberate crops; visitors can open the full image.
- Bracket annotations become numbered captions beside static views.
- **Explore in 3D** is optional and loads the viewer only when requested. It has a clear exit and does not take over ordinary page scrolling.
- Buttons and links have comfortable touch targets. Nothing depends on hover.

Without 3D, the bracket uses a prepared set of renders: overall view, mounting detail, and a comparison view if both versions exist. These deliver the same design explanation.

With reduced motion, the page uses still compositions and immediate state changes. If scripts or the model fail, the core text, images, navigation, CV, and contact remain available.

**Everything else the site needs**

Before design production:

- Original bike, component, and workshop photographs, with captions identifying what they show.
- Bracket CAD exports, useful static views, the mass-comparison baseline, and your actual simulation documentation.
- Your account of the bracket’s constraints, changes, and contribution.
- Charger images and a short account of the design and charging observation.
- An accurate CV consistent with the website.
- Verified contact and journal destinations.
- Confirmation of placement timing and the planned machining date.
- Permission for any team-owned material or identifiable people shown; customer details removed from job cards.

For implementation and launch:

- A domain, hosting, HTTPS, and a deployment process.
- Accessible HTML, logical headings, keyboard controls, visible focus, alt text, and checked contrast.
- Optimized responsive images, local/subset fonts where practical, and deferred 3D loading.
- Clear page titles, descriptions, a sitemap, favicon, and social sharing images.
- An accessible PDF CV plus readable education and skills information on the site.
- A useful missing-page screen and checked internal and external links.
- Verification on small phones, touch devices, desktop browsers, reduced-motion settings, and slower connections.
- A simple content-editing workflow for project status, captions, and new work.
- Basic maintenance for hosting, dependencies, links, and downloadable files.

A contact form, analytics, CMS, or cookie banner is not automatically required. At launch, I recommend direct email, lightweight content management through project files, and no tracking. Add more infrastructure only when it serves an actual need.
