The bike and bracket are the visual anchors. I would avoid inventing a mechanical connection between them or making the bracket appear to come off the bike. A clean change of scene respects the fact that they are separate projects.

For the visual direction, I’d use **warm paper, graphite, and workshop orange**:

- **Warm paper `#F2EFE7`** for most backgrounds.
- **Graphite `#202522`** for text and occasional dark visual panels.
- **Muted steel `#66716E`** for secondary graphics and rules.
- **Burnt orange `#B64926`** for links, selected states, and a few annotations.

Once your bike photographs are available, its actual paint colour could replace the orange. That would give the palette a personal source.

Typography would pair **Barlow Condensed** for large, compact titles with **IBM Plex Sans** for readable body text. Small measurements and figure labels could use the mono variant sparingly. Generous space, precise captions, and occasional figure numbers would suggest a thoughtfully documented workshop. Avoid decorative engineering diagrams, meaningless measurements, skill percentages, and fake terminal interfaces.

The overall composition should vary: a generous opening, a precise bracket study, a looser photographic restoration sequence, then a quiet ending. That rhythm is what keeps it from feeling like a repeated project-card template.

Motion needs a small, consistent vocabulary: **inspect, turn, reveal**. The bracket rotates to expose geometry; a photograph moves to reveal a useful detail; an annotation appears beside the feature it explains. No custom cursor, automatic spinning, background particles, sound, or scroll hijacking. The browser’s normal scrolling should always work, and visitors should be able to jump straight to projects or contact.
