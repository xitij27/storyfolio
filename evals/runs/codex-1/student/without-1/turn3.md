The direction is **Mina Park’s engineering logbook**: a portfolio organised around the job-card process you use at the co-op—what was reported, what you found, what you did, and how you checked it.

The visual signature is your garage-floor inventory of bike parts, paired with precise CAD views of the bracket. The site should feel carefully made and distinctly yours, while letting a placement reviewer understand your experience in two minutes.

This is the full plan. Nothing has been built.

## 1. The page, in order

### Navigation

A slim navigation bar stays available as the visitor moves through the page:

**Mina Park** · **Work** · **About** · **CV** · **Contact**

- Your name returns to the top.
- Work jumps to the bracket.
- About jumps to your background.
- CV opens an accessible PDF, with a separate download option.
- Contact jumps to the closing section.

Use ordinary links with visible keyboard focus. No introductory screen, sound, or required interaction.

### Opening: the person and the work

**Purpose:** establish your level, interests, and placement intent immediately.

Suggested copy:

> **Mina Park**  
> Mechanical engineering, learned hands-on.  
> Second-year BEng student at Hallam Institute of Technology, Sheffield. Saturday bike mechanic and Hallam Racing chassis sub-team member.  
> Seeking a placement in mechanical or product design.

Below this: **See my work** and **View CV**.

The main image is your actual garage-floor parts inventory. Its irregular arrangement gives the page a character that a generic grid of project cards would not.

A small caption identifies it:

> Parts from my 1984 steel road-bike restoration. Documented in Slow Miles.

**3D and movement:** no 3D here. A subtle change of framing can draw attention from the whole inventory toward a detail as the visitor scrolls. Text appears immediately, without animation delays.

**What visitors can click:** the two main links and a caption link to the restoration section. Individual parts do not become interactive until the dedicated inventory section, where the interaction can be explained properly.

### Project 01: the pedal box bracket

**Purpose:** show your strongest engineering example first, including its limits.

Heading:

> **A lighter pedal box mounting bracket**

An immediately visible summary gives the reviewer:

- **Context:** Hallam Racing, chassis sub-team; joined October 2025.
- **My contribution:** redesigned the mounting bracket.
- **Reported result:** 18% lighter in CAD and simulation.
- **Status:** not manufactured or physically tested; machining planned for November 2026, assuming that is the intended year.

The weight claim and the untested status sit together. Neither is hidden behind a click.

Below that, arrange the explanation as a job card:

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| **The requirement** | What the bracket must do and the constraints it must satisfy, taken from your project notes. |
| **What I changed** | Specific geometry or design changes, supported by the CAD views. |
| **My checks** | The available CAD comparison and simulation evidence, including their scope and limitations. |
| **Current status** | Digital design only; machining planned; physical verification still to come. |

**3D and movement:** this is the site’s main 3D sequence. On a capable desktop, a model stays beside the text for three short stages:

1. **Locate:** show the bracket in its immediate assembly context, if suitable assembly geometry is available.
2. **Inspect:** isolate the bracket and rotate to a useful engineering view.
3. **Compare:** show the earlier and revised designs at the same scale and camera angle, if both files are available.

Annotations identify documented changes only. No simulated loading animation or stress colouring unless it represents actual supplied results.

The sequence occupies roughly two desktop screen heights. It must not require repeated scrolling before the visitor can leave.

**What visitors can click:**

- **Original / Revised**, with clearly labelled states.
- **Inspect in 3D**, enabling manual rotation.
- **Reset view**, restoring the explanatory angle.
- **Read the design log**, opening the full case study.

Scroll-driven movement stops while manual inspection is active. Page scrolling remains normal outside the viewer.

**Without 3D:** the same three stages appear as annotated renders. All claims and explanations remain ordinary page text.

## 2. The look

The page should borrow the clarity of a job card and the specificity of a workshop logbook, without imitating a clipboard or adding artificial wear.

| Element | Direction |
|---|---|
| **Background** | Warm paper, `#F2EFE7` |
| **Main text** | Graphite, `#202522` |
| **Secondary surfaces** | Pale grey-green, `#DCE2DC` |
| **Links and key labels** | Deep green, `#244C43` |
| **Small emphasis marks** | Burnt orange, `#A94324` |
| **Headings and body** | Instrument Sans |
| **Dates, figure labels, technical captions** | IBM Plex Mono |

Check all final text and control combinations for accessible contrast. Status must be written in words, not communicated through colour alone.

Use large photographs, generous space, thin dividing rules, and compact annotations. Headings can be bold and oversized, but the reading text should remain comfortable. Keep longer text columns narrow enough to scan.

Photography should retain the real workshop and garage context. Crop distracting edges where useful, but do not remove the evidence of work. CAD views should share a consistent background, scale convention, and caption style.

Once the photographs are selected, the accent colour can be adjusted to suit the bike’s actual finish.

## 3. Motion rules

Every moving element must help locate, compare, or inspect something.

- Keep the bracket as the single substantial 3D feature.
- Use native scrolling throughout.
- Never delay readable content until an animation finishes.
- Avoid automatic continuous rotation.
- Pause rendering when the viewer is off-screen.
- Make transitions short and interruptible.
- Preserve the visitor’s place when they open and close an image.
- Honour reduced-motion preferences with static compositions.

There is no sound, custom cursor, particle background, or opening cinematic.

## 4. On a phone

The story stays in the same order, with simpler presentation.

- **Navigation:** your name, a direct CV link, and an accessible menu for the remaining sections.
- **Opening:** introduction and work link first, then the inventory photograph.
- **Bracket:** three annotated stills in normal document flow. An optional **Load 3D view** button lets the visitor choose the extra download and interaction.
- **Bike inventory:** overview photograph followed by a list of parts. Selecting an item expands its photos and notes immediately below the entry. No precision tapping required.
- **Charger and about:** single-column images and text.
- **Contact:** large, clearly labelled links.

Avoid pinned panels and horizontal scrolling. Use controls with comfortable touch targets, body text around 17–18 pixels, and image crops chosen for the smaller screen.

Manual 3D interaction is enabled only inside the activated viewer. It must not unexpectedly capture the visitor’s attempt to scroll down the page.

## 5. Without 3D—or with limited connectivity

The baseline site is a complete portfolio made of text, photographs, and CAD renders.

A missing model, unsupported browser, reduced-motion setting, or slow connection must never remove information. The bracket’s fallback images explain the same stages and remain available if the viewer fails.

With JavaScript disabled:

- Navigation and project links still work.
- All project summaries remain visible.
- The bike inventory becomes photographs and ordinary linked entries.
- Contact and the CV remain accessible.

Load the opening content first, responsive photographs as needed, and the 3D code and model later. A viewer failure should leave a useful image in place rather than an empty canvas.

## 6. Supporting pages

**Bracket design log**

The full requirement, your role, original and revised geometry, design decisions, weight-comparison method, simulation setup and limitations, current status, and planned physical checks. Add dated manufacturing and testing updates when those events actually happen.

**Bike restoration**

An overview and inventory, linking to individual repair stories. If Slow Miles already holds the full articles, use short introductions and link there rather than maintaining duplicate copies.

**Charger note**

Create this only if the photographs and notes support more than the homepage summary. Include team context, your contribution, observed performance, and documented reflection.

**CV**

An accessible PDF with selectable text and an up-to-date placement target. If the July 2025 team result remains, explicitly identify it as predating your membership. Prefer omitting it from personal achievement summaries.

Every supporting page needs a clear return to the portfolio, access to the CV and contact details, and its own shareable URL.

Run a two-minute reading check: can someone identify your level, your bracket contribution, its untested status, your practical experience, and how to contact you?
