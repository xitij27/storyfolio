The opening and bracket summary should work for someone who only gives you 30 seconds. The remaining sections reward the full two minutes. Detailed project pages are optional reading, not necessary to understand your suitability.

**The job-card structure**

Give each substantial project a consistent set of plain-language prompts:

- **The brief / reported fault**
- **What I found**
- **My part**
- **What I changed**
- **Checks and current status**

Only show fields supported by evidence. An unfinished project can end with a planned check; it does not need a fabricated conclusion. For engineering work, the logbook can hold supporting CAD views, calculations, design decisions, and dated updates.

This makes your thinking visible while keeping the homepage concise.

**How the claims should read**

For the bracket, use wording along these lines:

> Redesigned the pedal box mounting bracket as part of Hallam Racing’s chassis sub-team. The revised design is 18% lighter according to CAD and simulation results. It has not yet been manufactured or physically tested. Machining is planned for November.

Label the metric **“18% lighter — digital comparison”**, with the supporting method explained in the case study. Do not suggest that simulation establishes physical performance, and do not add strength, stiffness, or safety claims without evidence.

Omit the team’s July 2025 placing from the website’s achievement summaries. If it remains in the downloadable CV, explicitly label it as a team result before you joined in October 2025.

For the charger:

> Designed the gear train and housing in a four-person first-year project. The prototype worked, but took four minutes of cranking to add one percentage point of phone charge.

Present that as a reported prototype observation, not a controlled efficiency benchmark. It is a useful project because you can discuss a real outcome. Any explanation of *why* it performed badly must come from your notes, not an assumed diagnosis.

For the co-op:

> Saturday mechanic at Second Gear Bike Co-op, since 2023.

Describe the job-card process you gave me. Drop “hundreds of bikes” unless you later establish a count from records. There will be no testimonials, invented figures, or decorative credibility claims.

**Visual direction**

Keep the warm paper, graphite, deep green, and restrained burnt-orange palette from the first plan. Use **Instrument Sans** for reading and headings, and **IBM Plex Mono** for job-card labels, dates, and technical captions.

The distinctive visual asset is now the **actual garage-floor parts inventory**. Use its arrangement, shapes, and photography to establish the site’s identity. Avoid turning the interface into a literal clipboard: the job card should guide the information, with generous spacing and large, clear images.

The bracket remains the one substantial 3D experience. Its movement should explain location and design changes. If the available CAD cannot support a clear comparison, matched renders are the better presentation.

**Phone, accessibility, and speed**

On a phone, the inventory becomes an overview photograph followed by a tappable list of parts. The bracket becomes annotated stills with an optional **Inspect in 3D** button. Neither interaction should be required to read the evidence.

Use normal scrolling, readable captions, keyboard-accessible controls, and static equivalents for reduced-motion preferences. Load text and the opening image first; load 3D later. No introductory animation should consume any of the reviewer’s two minutes.

- Your placement dates need updating. I would use “Seeking a placement in mechanical or product design” provisionally, without inventing a year.
- Your photos and CAD can be published, subject to checking any Hallam Racing restrictions.

The site also needs an updated downloadable CV, shareable project pages, and simple contact access. A blog system, contact form, and analytics can wait unless there is a clear use for them.

The design should leave a reviewer able to say: **I know what Mina did, what she checked, and what she still needs to find out.**
