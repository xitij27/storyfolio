Your name, “Second-year mechanical engineering student,” placement interest, and immediate links to your work, CV, and contact. A photograph of the bike parts laid out on the garage floor becomes the main visual, with subtle depth as you scroll.

**3D and scroll direction**

The bike inventory becomes the signature interaction: scrolling gently spreads the photographed parts into an annotated layout. Selecting a part opens its repair story. Use real photographs arranged in depth, preserving their workshop character.

An actual bracket CAD model could provide a second, smaller interaction: rotate it and inspect annotated changes. Keep its **CAD / simulation only** status visible alongside it.

Neither interaction should delay access to project summaries. Avoid long pinned sequences, automatic camera rides, and essential information revealed only through animation. Mobile and reduced-motion versions use a clear static inventory.

**Visual language**

Paper-white backgrounds, dark ink, restrained workshop-orange accents, and logbook-style captions. Strong photography, clear dates, and generous spacing. Labels such as **Role**, **Finding**, **Action**, and **Verification** make the work quick to scan.

**Before implementation**

First, map the available photographs to these stories and sketch the first screen plus one complete job card. Then establish the remaining factual details: the placement season, live contact details, bracket comparison baseline and simulation scope, and your reflections on the charger.
