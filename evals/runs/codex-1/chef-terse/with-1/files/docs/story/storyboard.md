# The ferment log — full page plan

Status: concept selected; storyboard proposed for review. Planning only. No website, prototype, dependencies or final captions created. Fact IDs refer to facts.md, with user corrections governing all copy.

## The idea

One question carries the page: **What happens before a ferment is ready?**

One object carries the story: a logbook made of six paper leaves. The same leaves stay on screen, separating into a timeline, a record and evidence panels, then returning to a whole book. Their movement exposes information rather than running as a background animation.

The opening's proposed lead is Ines's own supplied wording: “The menu is the last thing written every week, not the first.” It is an existing source quotation, not newly drafted narrative copy. Public captions remain to be written and reviewed; below are working titles and factual briefs only. The close answers through the supplied log fields: start date, salt, check days and readiness, with the menu following the kitchen's prep work. No scientific mechanism or food-safety recipe is implied.

Six leaves and chapter headings are an editorial treatment, not a reproduction of an unseen logbook. Learning to wash and label jars at the stage is not presented as the origin of all her fermentation knowledge. The opening instead grounds the object in the prep list and ferment log she says she uses today.

## Thirty-second visit and navigation

First screen: name, current role, Leeds, subject of work, question, compact proof with measurement scope. A persistent six-link rail jumps to chapters with real fragment URLs; a contact link skips directly to Notes. On a phone, use a compact Chapters disclosure plus a visible Contact link. All links work with keyboard and without JavaScript. Visible focus, meaningful headings and sufficiently large tap targets; navigation never moves focus simply because the reader scrolls.

## Motion and interaction

Desktop: ordinary HTML content occupies roughly half the width; one sticky scene takes the remaining space. The camera stays almost fixed. Sheets move along short readable paths, settle at each beat and reverse smoothly when scrolling backwards. No spin, custom cursor, sound or automatic advancing.

Each leaf has a persistent identity, even when its face content changes at a settled transition. Changes show the shift from timeline to record to evidence. No extra jars, scales or plates arrive as replacement heroes. Selecting log fields uses HTML buttons and only highlights corresponding information; no content requires dragging a 3D surface. Optional image viewer supports keyboard close, focus return and meaningful alt text.

No scroll hijacking or mandatory snapping. Internal links reach the correct content immediately and settle the corresponding scene. Reduced motion and an optional motion-off control remove travel rather than merely slowing it.

## Future implementation and verification plan

Choose a static-first implementation: pre-render the full page and metadata, layer one lazy-loaded Three.js scene onto desktop, and derive its settled states from native scroll progress. Six simple paper meshes, small text textures, modest shadows and capped pixel ratio; no physics, video background or heavy environment map. Exact framework follows the existing project when a build is authorised; no framework or dependency changes now.

Performance targets for a future build: LCP at most 2.5 seconds, CLS at most 0.1 and INP at most 200 ms under representative mobile conditions; these are goals, not measured results. Load HTML and the first static figure before scene code, reserve scene space, stop rendering when idle or offscreen and use static fallback when rendering fails.

Future release checks: source-audit every public sentence; verify corrected dates, stage status and team-result scope; check cleared assets and credits; navigate all chapters and contact routes by keyboard; exercise phone, zoom, reduced motion, WebGL failure and JavaScript-off modes; confirm no clipping, content gaps or blank first screen; inspect preview metadata, 404 and real links; measure performance and contrast. No tests have been run because nothing is built.
