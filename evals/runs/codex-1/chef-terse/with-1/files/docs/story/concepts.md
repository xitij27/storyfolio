## 1. The ferment log — recommended

**Question:** What happens before a ferment is ready?

**Form:** Ferment log. Six chapters, ordered from identifying a batch to recording readiness:

1. Batch — opening and identity; introduce Ines and her actual log fields.
2. Started — background: training, roles and the accurately described stage.
3. Salt — method: what the kitchen records; no unsupplied quantities or implied recipe.
4. Checks — work: the log's check days, prep list and larder documentation; mention Brine and apprentices without fabricated lessons.
5. Ready — outcome: readiness is a log field; separately identify the kitchen-wide 2023 waste measurement and Callum's quote, without attributing the reduction to one ferment.
6. Notes — close: answer the question with the documented sequence of recording start, salt, checks and readiness; three visitor routes.

**Object:** A small logbook made of six thick paper leaves, built from flat planes. It stays visible throughout.

**Transformations:** In Started, leaves separate into a dated career sequence. In Checks, they spread into a readable record of the four supplied fields, beside cleared larder images when available. In Ready, two leaves align as weekly-waste evidence, with measurement scope on their faces and the quote on another leaf. End with the leaves reunited into the logbook.

**Opening and close:** The leaves assemble from the field names Ines supplied into the log she works from. Proposed opening: “The menu is the last thing written.” Opening provenance is her daily process, not an invented origin anecdote. Close returns to the complete log, with apprentice enquiries, workshops and press contact.

**Visual direction:** Warm paper, graphite, muted brine green; legible editorial typography and tabular dates. Movement is measured: sheets separate, align and settle as their information changes.

**Stands on:** F1–F4, F8–F10, F12–F13, F15, F19–F21. Apprentice programme mention from F6; no unconfirmed apprentice count.

**Needs:** A sanitised log sample would make the central exhibit concrete; until supplied, show only confirmed field names. Optional own larder photo, checked for prices and faces. Contact destinations. No dependency on Priya photos or Olu artwork.

**Why theirs:** A log recording salt and check days, with the menu written last, makes Ines's own working process the structure of the site.

## 3. The labelled larder

**Question:** What does a jar's label need to tell us?

**Form:** Ferment log read through its physical jars: Batch (opening/identity), Started (background), Salt (method), Check days (work), Ready (outcome), Record (close). These are proposed display chapters grounded in F21, not a claimed transcription of her labels.

**Object:** Six simple cylindrical jars with flat labels, grouped as one small larder. Six is illustrative, not the unconfirmed 40+ count. No invented contents or rendered bubbling to imply a real batch.

**Transformations:** Started arranges the jars as a career sequence; labels explicitly describe roles, not batch dates. Check days brings labels forward into a readable log grid carrying the confirmed field names. Ready arranges the labels as evidence panels for the whole kitchen's waste result and quote, explicitly separate from batch data. The same jars gather onto one shelf at the close.

**Opening and close:** Assemble the larder from Ines's stated work of washing and labelling jars during her ten-week stage. Do not claim she learned all her fermentation practice there. End with the labelled shelf and the answer: start date, salt, check days and readiness, followed by the three audience routes.

**Visual direction:** Quiet cupboard tones, pale labels and restrained amber glass. Jars move to make labels readable; avoid cinematic camera spins or decorative liquid simulation.

**Stands on:** F1–F4, F9, F13, F15, F19–F21. No claim that these six jars reproduce actual batches.

**Needs:** Own larder/label photo for visual fidelity, sanitised real batch example if one is to be shown, contact destinations. No dependence on restaurant photographs or Olu's drawings.

**Why theirs:** The jar labels connect the humble work she actually did at Fjaerland to the precise records she now says run her kitchen.
