I’ll read the material folder first, then shape a site concept and a full scroll-by-scroll plan around your work and personality. I won’t build anything.


The site should feel like **an architect opening her working drawings and showing why a building is worth keeping**. Your work has a clear point of view: retain what matters, improve how people live, and understand construction through making things yourself. That is a stronger foundation than visual spectacle alone.

I’d build the concept around **“Worth keeping.”** Large architectural photography, precise drawings and one carefully chosen 3D sequence would give it presence. The contrast between Calder Court’s 96 homes and the cabin you built with your father would make it unmistakably yours.

This plan draws on the CV, LinkedIn text and image inventory in `material`. The actual images and drawings aren’t included yet, so image choices and the proposed 3D treatment are provisional. Nothing has been built.

**The idea and visual character**

The site would move between three scales: **the building, the construction detail, and the hand that makes it**.

It should have the atmosphere of a working architecture publication: generous space, strong photographic compositions, measured typography, occasional annotations and visible material texture from your own photographs. Avoid simulated drafting software, floating decorative buildings and generic technical graphics.

Proposed opening copy:

> **Worth keeping.**  
> I work with existing buildings, and build small things in timber.  
> Marta Kowalczyk — Architect, ARB. Glasgow.

Your practice role would appear alongside this introduction or in the biography. LinkedIn records your promotion to Associate in August 2025, while the CV says Senior Architect; I’d provisionally use the newer title and confirm it before publication.

**The homepage, from top to bottom**

1. **Arrival — establish the person and the position.**

   Open with your name, the headline and one large completed photograph of Calder Court. Choose an image that shows the building as somewhere people live, if the available photographs support that.

   A compact navigation offers Work, About and Contact. The first screen should already reveal useful content, with no loading ceremony or mandatory animation. A small project caption names Calder Court and credits Field & Rowe and the photographer.

   As scrolling begins, the headline leaves naturally and the photograph gives way to the project’s earlier condition. A restrained image transition introduces the question behind your practice: what can be retained and made better?

2. **Calder Court — the main argument, supported by evidence.**

   This is the substantial opening project: a 1960s deck-access block, 96 flats, completed in 2025.

   Start with a clearly labelled before-and-after pair, aligned where the available views permit. Follow with a short explanation of external insulation and new winter gardens. Present **68% less heating demand** beside the supplied diagram, with the basis of the figure identified once verified.

   The signature scroll interaction comes here: a simplified **3D section of one facade bay**, derived from the actual drawings. Over a short desktop sequence, it moves from the existing condition to the retrofit, then resolves into the 1:5 wall detail. Components separate only enough to explain the construction, with a small number of useful labels.

   The effect should answer “what changed, and why?” Geometry and construction order would need checking against the drawings. If those drawings don’t support an accurate model, use layered section drawings instead.

   Finish with a completed photograph and a clear link to the full case study. The movement ends before the reader continues.

3. **Cabin at Kashuby — introduce the maker.**

   After the larger professional project, change pace and scale:

   > **28 square metres. Built with my father.**

   Let the personal photographs do most of the work. Curate perhaps six to eight images from the roughly 200 available: first post, frame, a joint or hand tool, enclosure, shingles and the finished cabin. Include an early hand drawing beside its CAD development.

   On desktop, one large image could remain in place briefly while three construction stages pass alongside it. Offer the frame-raising time-lapse through an explicit play control, with a still image shown first.

   This section is the emotional centre of the site. The visual interest comes from the real sequence of making, with captions written in your voice about choices, difficulties and discoveries.

4. **Kirk Street — demonstrate another kind of reuse.**

   A quieter, shorter chapter introduces the church hall converted into six flats, completed in 2023.

   Pair a photograph of the hall with the section drawing, then show the completed interior or exterior. A restrained highlight in the section could explain the conversion once the drawings have been reviewed. Keep the existing fabric legible.

   This chapter broadens the argument beyond Calder Court without repeating its full interactive treatment. Link to a dedicated case study for the complete story.

5. **A School That Grows — show the speculative work.**

   Label this prominently as **a shortlisted competition proposal, 2024**, so visitors can distinguish it from completed buildings.

   Extract one strong overview, one spatial drawing and the clearest explanation of the growth strategy from the six boards. The homepage should read as a composed story; the full boards belong in an optional viewer or downloadable document.

   If the proposal contains a genuine staged-growth diagram, reveal those stages as the reader scrolls. The animation should follow the submitted design rather than inventing a new narrative.

6. **About and teaching — connect the projects to your practice.**

   Introduce your position at Field & Rowe, your Year 2 teaching at Clyde School of Architecture and your experience in Glasgow and Gdańsk.

   Use a short first-person biography. Your existing statement about making old buildings worth keeping is the starting point, edited into a personal position rather than an unsupported general carbon claim.

   A photograph of you building, drawing or teaching would suit this section if one is available and usable. Teaching can be a concise paragraph initially; student work should only appear with permission and attribution.

   Include a downloadable, accessible CV and a compact career timeline.

7. **Contact — offer a clear next step.**

   Close with a simple invitation, provisionally:

   > **For conversations about existing buildings, timber and teaching.**

   Provide email, LinkedIn and Glasgow location. The wording should reflect whether the site is intended for collaborators, employers, speaking and teaching invitations, or independent commissions. It should also make your relationship with Field & Rowe clear.

**Colour and type**

Use a restrained palette that lets the photographs carry most of the colour:

| Role | Proposed colour | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Background | Warm chalk `#F3F0E8` | A soft setting for drawings and photography |
| Main text | Charcoal `#252923` | Strong, readable contrast |
| Secondary surfaces | Pale stone `#DEDCD3` | Captions, project facts and quiet transitions |
| Accent | Deep oxide `#854333` | Links, section markers and drawing annotations |

The palette should be checked against the actual photographs before it is fixed. Before/after states and drawing legends must use labels as well as colour.

Pair **Source Serif 4** for expressive headlines with **Inter** for navigation, captions and technical information. Both can be self-hosted. Large serif titles give the work an editorial presence; the clear sans serif keeps project information precise. Body text stays comfortably sized, with short line lengths and no tiny architectural labels.

**Movement and the phone experience**

The site should use native scrolling. Give Calder Court the one major choreographed sequence; elsewhere, rely on image changes, short reveals and mostly stationary content. Avoid cursor effects, scroll-controlled camera flights and continuous background motion.

On phones, turn the Calder Court sequence into three lightweight, labelled views: existing condition, intervention and finished detail. Stack before/after images vertically. Let the cabin photographs become a straightforward sequence with captions. Drawings get a tap-to-enlarge option; no essential information depends on hovering, dragging or animation.

For reduced-motion preferences, show the same story through static images and text. Navigation, image viewing and video controls must work with a keyboard, and all key information must remain available if 3D fails to load.

**What else the site needs**

- **Individual project pages.** Each gets a shareable URL, concise brief, project status, dates, your specific contribution, collaborators, credits and selected drawings. Professional projects must distinguish your contribution from the practice’s work.
- **An asset and permissions pass.** Confirm permission to publish Field & Rowe material and Tom Reid’s photographs, including credit wording. Select a small, purposeful set of cabin images. Check competition and teaching material separately.
- **Verified project claims.** Establish whether the heating-demand reduction is modelled or measured, its comparison basis, and your responsibilities on each practice project.
- **Fast delivery.** Load the opening photograph and text first; defer 3D until its section approaches. Use responsive images, lightweight models and video loaded on request. Aim for Core Web Vitals in the “good” range, verified on mobile.
- **A maintainable publishing setup.** Use a small project content system with editable captions, credits and image order. Keep the signature interaction independent so adding another project doesn’t require another animation.
- **Publication basics.** Page titles, social previews, descriptive image alternatives, accessible documents, a useful 404 page and a clear contact address. Add privacy information appropriate to whichever hosting, analytics or contact services are actually chosen.

Before design begins, the main decisions are the site’s primary audience, your preferred contact route and the availability of publishable images. The creative direction is already clear: **lead with the impact of retaining buildings, then let the cabin reveal the person who understands how they go together.**
