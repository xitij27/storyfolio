The recommended site is **“A place, carefully worked through.”** It follows the order of a drawing set—location, plans, sections, elevations, details and schedules—while telling a clear story to someone with an old house or a small plot.

The cabin leads because it brings together your design, drawings, photographs and experience of building with your father. Calder Court establishes your responsibility for technical design and delivery at a larger scale. Teaching and the school competition complete the picture.

The signature visual is **one accurate 3D model of the cabin**, used to explain the building. It appears in a bounded chapter rather than following visitors around the entire site.

This is the complete proposed plan. Nothing has been built.

## 1. Header and navigation

**What appears**

Your name on the left; Work, Approach, About and Contact on the right. A small “Architect · Glasgow” descriptor can sit beneath your name on larger screens.

The header is compact and remains available while scrolling. It has a solid background when overlapping content so it stays readable.

**What visitors can click**

- Your name returns to the top.
- Work goes to the project index.
- Approach goes to the drawing-and-detail chapter.
- About goes to your biography and teaching.
- Contact goes to the closing invitation.

**3D behaviour**

None. Navigation never depends on the model loading.

**Phone and fallback**

Use your name and an accessible Menu button. The opened menu contains the same four links, closes after selection and works with a keyboard. Include a skip-to-content link for keyboard users.

## 4. Sections — the signature cabin sequence

**What appears**

A chapter titled:

> **From drawing to frame.**

This contains the site’s principal interactive moment. An accurate, simplified cabin model sits beside three short passages. A small position indicator reads “1 of 3,” “2 of 3” or “3 of 3.”

**What the 3D does**

1. **The whole building.**  
   Show the cabin at a fixed, carefully chosen angle. Its relationship between roof, walls and openings is clear.

2. **The section.**  
   Remove or cut through a verified portion of the envelope to explain the interior and structure. Keep the camera largely steady so visitors can understand what changed.

3. **The frame.**  
   Reveal the timber frame, then transition to a real photograph from a matching construction stage.

The sequence occupies roughly two to three desktop screen heights. The visual stays in place while the accompanying passages scroll. Native scrolling continues normally; there is no scroll locking.

This is an explanatory sequence, not a claim about construction order. Any assembly animation would require evidence from your drawings and photographs.

**What visitors can click**

- “View as stills” switches to three labelled static views.
- “Skip to construction photographs” bypasses the sequence.
- “Open section drawing” shows the source drawing.

There is no default free-orbit control. The selected views make the important relationships clearer and avoid competing with page scrolling.

**Phone and fallback**

Phones receive the three static views in a vertical sequence by default. Reduced-motion settings also receive stills.

If WebGL is unavailable, slow or fails, the poster image and static views remain visible. All explanatory text exists outside the canvas. The model is supplementary; it never contains the only copy of essential information.

## The visual system

| Element | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Background | Warm chalk `#F3F0E8` |
| Main text | Charcoal `#252923` |
| Secondary surfaces | Pale stone `#DEDCD3` |
| Accent | Deep oxide `#854333` |
| Headlines | Source Serif 4 |
| Body, navigation and captions | Inter |
| Layout | Generous editorial grid; full-width photographs alternating with compact drawings and text |
| Graphic language | Fine rules, drawing numbers, aligned captions and restrained annotations |

Use the actual timber, landscape and construction photography to supply texture and colour. Avoid artificial paper grain, decorative crosshairs, custom cursors and floating architectural objects.

Headlines can be large, but body text should stay around 18–20 pixels on desktop and at least 16–18 on phones. Technical captions must remain comfortably readable. Check contrast in the final design and distinguish links through more than colour.

Motion is brief and purposeful outside the cabin sequence. No autoplay video, background audio or continuous decorative movement.

## The supporting pages

**Four project pages**, as material and permissions allow, each with:

1. Project summary, status and location
2. Practice or team attribution and your exact role
3. The brief and existing conditions
4. Selected decisions, explained through drawings and photographs
5. Construction or delivery evidence
6. Outcomes, limitations and lessons
7. Credits and links to another project or contact

Show RIBA stages only where the mapping is accurate. Calder Court provisionally covers Stages 4–6; do not imply responsibility for Stage 7 monitoring without confirmation. Avoid forcing the self-build cabin or competition into an unsupported stage history.

**A “How a project develops” page** translates Stages 0–7 into homeowner questions, from defining the need through use and evaluation. It explains the process without presenting unconfirmed services, fees or programme promises.

**An About/Teaching page** provides a shareable destination with the fuller biography, teaching information and CV.

**A credits/privacy page** records attribution and any necessary privacy information.

The snag-list mindset belongs in the construction and lessons sections: checking, resolving and following through. Actual snag records should only appear as approved, anonymised examples.

## Assumptions behind the plan

The site initially targets Glasgow and Scotland, welcomes early residential conversations and guest-critic enquiries, and prepares for an independent practice in roughly two years. It does not yet advertise confirmed independent appointments.

The visual design remains provisional until the actual photographs and drawings have been reviewed. The cabin model will explain only verified geometry. Practice material enters the published site only when cleared; the cabin-led version is a complete launch option in its own right.

The intended experience is a visitor moving from **understanding a place, to understanding your decisions, to trusting your ability to carry them into construction**.
