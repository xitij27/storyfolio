The direction is **“From location to detail.”** The site follows the order of an architectural drawing set, moving from the setting of a building to the joint that holds it together. Your cabin carries the story; your practice work and teaching show the wider experience behind it.

The defining visual is a **single, drawing-derived 3D model of the cabin**. Across selected moments, it moves from whole building to section to timber connection. Photography repeatedly brings visitors back to the built work.

This is the full design and content plan. No implementation is included.

**1. What the site should achieve**

The site serves two audiences:

- People with an old house or small plot, who may become clients when you establish your practice.
- Architecture schools looking for a guest critic or tutor with experience of retrofit, timber, technical design, and construction.

Within the first minute, visitors should understand your interests, see something you designed and built yourself, and know which parts of the practice projects were your responsibility.

The voice is first-person, clear, and specific. Explain decisions through drawings and photographs. Use terms such as “section” alongside a short explanation when needed.

Your professional identification is:

> **Marta Kowalczyk**  
> Associate at Field & Rowe Architects, Glasgow. ARB registered.

The site represents you personally. It should not suggest that an independent practice is already operating.

**Phone and fallback:** The photograph leads, followed by a static axonometric drawing. The same text and links remain visible. Nothing waits for 3D to load.

**02 / Plans: Making room**

This chapter explains the small footprint of the cabin through one legible plan and two or three photographs.

Lead with:

> **A small footprint, considered closely.**

The accompanying text should describe two or three actual decisions revealed by the plan—circulation, openings, or the arrangement of spaces—after reviewing the drawings. Avoid assigning intentions that the available evidence does not support.

**3D:** The cabin rotates gently into a top view. The roof lifts out of view to expose the plan beneath. The movement then stops, leaving a readable drawing.

On desktop, the plan can remain alongside a short run of photographs. A numbered marker identifies the viewpoint of each photograph.

**Clicks:** Visitors can select a numbered viewpoint to switch photographs, open the plan in a zoomable viewer, or continue to the cabin case study. The first photograph and its caption are visible without interaction.

**Phone and fallback:** Show the plan, followed by the selected photographs and their matching viewpoint numbers. Avoid a sticky panel or miniature controls. A static plan replaces the model.

**05 / Details: Where I get closest**

This is the main interactive moment and the most personal chapter.

Start with a close construction photograph, then a short passage connecting your father’s workshop, the timber-joint book you have kept since nineteen, and the experience of building the cabin.

Proposed lead:

> **I learned to look closely in my father’s workshop.**

The final prose should come from your own account. It should describe something specific you learned, rather than adding an invented anecdote.

Select one real cabin joint that has sufficient drawings and photographs to explain clearly.

**3D:** The view cuts to that connection. Its components separate along their actual assembly directions, pause with concise labels, then reassemble. Use an intentional change of scale between building and joint rather than a long cinematic flight.

The animation explains assembly order and the relationship between parts. It should not make unsupported claims about structural performance.

A matching construction photograph sits beside the model. The real joint remains the reference point.

If permission is granted, the Calder wall build-up follows as a separate example of your technical work, credited to the practice and identifying your contribution.

**Clicks:** “Previous step,” “Next step,” and “Replay assembly” allow visitors to control the sequence. “View drawing” opens the annotated detail. The cabin time-lapse has a poster image and an explicit play button.

**Phone and fallback:** Three or four labelled stills explain the assembly. Capable phones may offer “Play assembly,” but the static sequence is complete on its own. The time-lapse never autoplays.

For the book, use its title and an original photograph once identified. Reproducing pages would require a separate rights decision.

**5. Look and typography**

| Element | Direction |
|---|---|
| Background | Warm paper, `#F2EFE8` |
| Main text | Graphite, `#252824` |
| Links and active controls | Forest green, `#344B40` |
| Selected components | Timber ochre, `#B78A54` |
| Secondary panels | Pale mineral, `#D8DCD5` |
| Display type | Instrument Serif, used sparingly for opening and personal statements |
| Reading and navigation | Manrope, including captions and drawing labels |
| Composition | Generous margins, fine rules, numbered figures, alternating photographs and drawing spreads |

Use ochre for highlighting components, not small text on the paper background. Check final contrast in context.

The model should resemble a precise architectural study: quiet surfaces, readable edges, restrained shadows. Photography supplies material richness. Avoid artificial paper wear, decorative technical annotations, custom cursors, or perpetual motion.

Aim for roughly 18px desktop body text and at least 16px on phones, with comfortably narrow paragraphs. Caption text must remain readable.

**6. How scrolling and interaction work**

The page uses native scrolling. There is no scroll hijacking or requirement to complete an animation before continuing.

Desktop has at most two short pinned sequences: the plan-to-section transformation and the joint assembly. Each should release within approximately one additional viewport of travel. Other transitions happen within ordinary document flow.

Every animated state is also available as a labelled still or through a normal control. Links work independently of the 3D scene. Keyboard focus must never move just because someone scrolls.

Provide a persistent motion preference. Honour reduced-motion settings automatically, and remember a visitor’s choice to pause animation.

Image and drawing viewers need visible close and zoom controls, keyboard operation, Escape-to-close, captions, and correct focus return.

**7. Phone and no-3D experience**

The mobile design follows the same order with a simpler presentation:

- One column with comfortable margins and touch targets of at least 44px.
- Photography and text first; drawings sized to remain meaningful.
- No scroll-pinned scenes.
- No essential hover interactions.
- Plans, sections, and assembly steps presented as a readable sequence.
- Optional 3D loaded only after an explicit request on phones.
- Video played only on request.

If WebGL is unavailable, loading fails, or motion is reduced, substitute prepared stills in the same spaces. There should be no error panel or empty canvas interrupting the story.

The site’s text, project links, drawings, and contact routes must remain usable without the interactive layer. The static version receives the same editorial attention as the animated version.

I have assumed that Glasgow remains your base, English is the launch language, direct email is the best contact method, and you are not yet advertising an independent practice. I have also assumed that the cabin drawings and photographs can support an accurate model; the exact joint and visual pairings must be chosen after reviewing those assets.

No independent-practice name, availability claim, testimonial, client-service promise, or unverified project outcome is part of the plan.
