# Storyboard — The bring-up fixture

Status: proposed 13 October 2026; awaiting user's approval. Concept 1 selected by the user authorizing the recommendation. Appearance and final wording remain later choices. No new skill or remote asset is needed.

## Question, form and answer

Opening question: **How do you connect firmware to a physical observation?**

Premises: Ada wrote Mosslink firmware, scheduled power states and built its bring-up fixture (F2). Ada wants a firmware hiring manager to understand the connection between code and a measurable signal (F1/F11).

Form: a bench checklist. Purpose → Setup → Exercise → Observe → Record limits → Handover. This is a narrative order, not a claim about Mosslink's historical test chronology or a reproduction of Ada's actual checklist.

Object: one explanatory bring-up fixture made of six substantial contact modules, with bodies, metallic pins and labelled inset faces. The same six modules persist throughout and reunite at the close. They stand for conceptual relationships; six does not stand for six tested samples, actual contact count or recovered hardware construction. No paper, sheets or card collection.

Closing meaning: connect a firmware decision to a physical observation through bench access, then limit the claim to the evidence. Ada's firmware/fixture work establishes the personal contribution. The radio-awake note establishes an observation without a confirmed explanation. The projection and 72-hour run establish that different evidence supports different claims (F2/F6/F7/F11). The close does not imply successful diagnosis, a fix, or completed testing.
