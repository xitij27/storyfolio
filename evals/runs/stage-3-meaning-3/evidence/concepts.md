## 1. Bring-up fixture — recommended

- Question: **What connects a firmware decision to a physical observation?**
- Premises: firmware, scheduling and fixture are Ada's own confirmed contributions; bench observation is her stated lesson. No diagnosis or fix is required for this question.
- Form: a proposed bring-up checklist, five chapters: Setup (opening/name/question and own contribution); Access (how / fixture's role in checking basic board operation); Command (how / scheduling states, labelled teaching loop); Observe (work/outcomes / six bench-tested of forty, radio-awake observation with no asserted resolution); Record (close / scope of projection versus run, preliminary qualification, email and CV).
- Object: six substantial probe assemblies, with mounting blocks, spring-like contacts and cable terminals, connecting into one explanatory bring-up fixture. No PCB or analogue circuitry is reproduced. Six is a visual tally in the middle, not the actual probe count of Ada's fixture.
- Transformations: Setup joins the probes as one fixture; Observe separates exactly six assemblies into a two-by-three tally, one for each bench-tested sample; Record reconnects them. Later full storyboard may add arrangements for Access/Command without changing the object.
- Confirmed count: exactly six assemblies in the middle represent six bench-tested engineering samples, from forty assembled; text explicitly carries denominator. The fixture itself is invented explanatory geometry.
- Opening: name, firmware contribution and a teaching fixture assembling from its parts. Close: joined fixture and Ada's lesson, email and corrected CV.
- Stands on: facts.md, confirmed contributions, working practice, sample scope and lesson.
- Needs: no unavailable asset or additional permission. Supplied fixture SVG can be labelled explanation; photo unavailable and excluded.
- Why theirs: it connects the fixture Ada built to the firmware she wrote and to this project's six-of-forty evidence boundary.
- Swap: specific contribution split and sample count would have to change for a colleague.
- Backdrop: physical contact assemblies connect, then separate as six countable observations, then reconnect; captions are not the sole change.
- Depth: setup → bench evidence → recorded scope. Does not end with an invented fix.
- Emblem: object is derived from Ada's built fixture, not a generic microchip badge.
- Honesty: replica status, test attribution, issue resolution and all unknown hardware details excluded; sample mapping explicitly explanatory.
- Thirty seconds: opening gives Ada, firmware/scheduling/fixture, email and direct evidence anchor; middle gives confirmed count. CV will be present in the approved build, not claimed to exist in this concept sketch.
- Sketch: `/sketch/fixture` — opening, middle and close.

## 2. Power-state dial

- Question: **What can a power-state plan tell us—and what needs a bench observation?**
- Premises: Ada scheduled power states; supplied Sleep/Measure/Transmit loop is teaching-only; projections and observations differ.
- Form: proposed state-table reading, five chapters: States (opening/name/question); Inputs (who / Ada's firmware contribution and board-design boundary); Transitions (how / teaching Sleep→Measure→Transmit sequence); Outputs (what / bench observation, radio-awake note without inferred causality); Evidence (outcomes/close / projection versus 72-hour run, email/CV).
- Object: six solid radial sectors forming a mechanical teaching dial, two sectors per teaching state. An invented explanatory object for Ada's scheduling work, not actual project hardware or a user-interface control.
- Transformations: States forms a rotor; Transitions opens it into three ordered state pairs; Evidence closes it. No animated speed, sector angle or spacing claims a measured duration/current.
- Confirmed order: Sleep → Measure → Transmit follows supplied teaching drawing. The equal sectors are visual layout only, not firmware execution or duty-cycle proportions.
- Opening: Ada's power scheduling makes the teaching dial assemble. Close: full dial alongside distinction between two-year estimate and 72-hour run.
- Stands on: confirmed scheduling, SVG restrictions, battery distinction, lesson.
- Needs: no additional assets; labelled SVG available. No trace or actual firmware state table needed.
- Why theirs: teaching states lead to Mosslink's precise projection/run distinction and Ada's scheduling contribution.
- Swap: the project's evidence boundary and personal contribution are specific, though the dial is more general than the fixture.
- Backdrop: closed rotor separates into ordered state pairs; visibly shows sequencing but cannot show measured performance.
- Depth: plan → sequence → evidence required to substantiate it.
- Emblem: a teaching model, not a microchip emblem; less directly an object Ada made than concept 1.
- Honesty: equal sectors have no timing/current meaning; no actual machine, fix or resolution claimed.
- Thirty seconds: Ada, contribution and email available at opening; middle names teaching loop with explicit scope. Final CV deferred to approved build.
- Sketch: `/sketch/dial` — opening, middle and close.

## 3. Engineering-sample rack

- Question: **How much does a bench result let us claim?**
- Premises: forty engineering samples assembled, six bench tested; no customer deployment; qualification unfinished.
- Form: proposed bench-test record, five chapters: Population (opening/name/question); Setup (who/how / firmware and bring-up fixture); Coverage (what / six-of-forty count); Observations (outcomes / 72-hour run and preliminary emissions check); Limits (close / projection versus observation and unfinished qualification, email/CV).
- Object: eight thick carrier rails, five recessed sockets per rail. A physical sample rack invented as explanatory geometry. No claim Mosslink used this rack or that sockets represent actual device shape.
- Transformations: Population joins forty sockets in one rack; Coverage separates rails for counting with exactly six amber sockets and thirty-four dark sockets; Limits returns to a whole while retaining six amber sockets. Dark does not mean failed.
- Confirmed proportion: six of forty = 15% bench-test coverage, spatially represented by six equal sockets among forty. No unconfirmed production/testing order implied by marker placement.
- Opening: Ada and firmware/fixture contributions with full sample population. Close: rack returns to whole, limitations remain explicit.
- Stands on: confirmed sample numbers, deployment scope, battery and emissions boundaries.
- Needs: no external assets, no actual sample photos/geometry. Label as explanatory count.
- Why theirs: exactly forty assembled and six bench tested tell Mosslink's actual evidence boundary without exaggerating a pilot.
- Swap: counts, ownership and qualification scope tie it to Mosslink; another project requires different geometry/copy.
- Backdrop: equal, countable wells show total and tested subset with captions hidden.
- Depth: population → coverage → claim boundary.
- Emblem: a concrete evidence tally, not firmware's conventional emblem.
- Honesty: sample rack is an illustration, amber means tested not passed; dark means outside confirmed tested subset not failed.
- Thirty seconds: name/contributions/email at opening, coverage at middle. CV comes in approved build.
- Sketch: `/sketch/rack` — opening, middle and close.

## Recommendation

Choose 1, the bring-up fixture: it comes from a thing Ada made and physically links firmware decisions with bench access. The six separated assemblies show a confirmed count without relying on face captions. The rack communicates the coverage fraction more strongly; the dial communicates the teaching order more strongly, but neither is as closely tied to Ada's own built object.
