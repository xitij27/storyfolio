## Spine and scope

- **Question:** What does the bench let me claim?
- **Form:** A bring-up checklist: Set up → Command → Observe → Record → Handover. A narrative reading order, not a recovered Mosslink procedure or chronology.
- **Object:** Eight thick fixture bars with five socket motifs each, assembled as two nested frames. Geometry is explanatory, not Ada's fixture design, capacity or wiring. The same eight pieces remain from opening to close; no pages, cards or unrelated objects.
- **Visitor:** Firmware hiring manager. The opening explains firmware as code that controls a physical device, and bring-up as checking a new board's basic operation. It identifies Ada's actual contribution without implying PCB ownership.
- **Length exception proposed:** Nine meaningful beats, **ten viewport heights** total. The usual12–22 beats would stretch one project and repeat its limited confirmed evidence. Approval of this storyboard includes agreement to this shorter story. All beats have stable anchors, allowing direct jumps.
- **Claim boundary:** Employer/current title/dates excluded. No project launch, customers, field pilot, two-year demonstrated endurance, finished qualification, or resolved radio fault. No source trace or actual fixture photograph available.

## Meaning of the ending

The answer to "What does the bench let me claim?" is that claims must retain the scope of their supporting observation and assumptions. Six tested samples do not establish testing of forty; a72-hour run does not establish two-year endurance; a preliminary emissions check does not finish qualification; a radio-awake observation does not establish a cause or a fix. These are the user's confirmed distinctions, not a claim that Ada resolved every issue. The object returns whole to connect the lesson to the fixture Ada built. Final wording is chosen later; the email/CV links are actions after the answer, not a substitute for it.
