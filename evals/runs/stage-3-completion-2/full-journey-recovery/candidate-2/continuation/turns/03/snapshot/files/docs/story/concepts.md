# Three concepts — A selected

Premise: Ada wrote firmware, scheduled power states and built the Mosslink bring-up fixture. Her lesson connects decisions to physical observations and distinguishes projections from observed runs. No concept assumes a radio cause/fix, field deployment or completed qualification.

Trade forms considered: state table, timing diagram, bench checklist, bring-up procedure, measurement log, qualification checklist. The first three are grounded in Ada's working documents/notes. Local geometry is original explanation, never a reconstruction or measured evidence.

## A. At the fixture (recommended)

- Question: How do you connect a firmware decision to a physical observation?
- Form: bench checklist. Chapters: Purpose (opening/name), Firmware scope (who), Bring-up (how), Power states (Mosslink work), Observation (radio-awake problem), Evidence limits (outcomes with qualifications), Handover (answer/contact).
- Object: seven chunky socket/contact modules forming a generic bring-up fixture with brass pins. Not actual PCB or wiring.
- Transformations: opening modules join as fixture; bring-up opens into command-to-observation path; evidence separates model/run groups; ending reconnects fixture.
- Opening draws from Ada's stated lesson and fixture authorship, not an invented origin anecdote. Close: a decision needs observation, and evidence limits remain explicit; email/CV/project links.
- Stands on confirmed firmware, fixture, daily documents, radio observation and corrected model/run/sample/qualification facts.
- Needs no additional asset/permission. Supplied bring-up teaching SVG may explain the relationship, clearly labelled.
- Why theirs: Ada built the fixture and wants hiring managers to see the connection between her firmware and bench observations.
- Runnable sketch: /sketch/fixture (Purpose, Observation, Handover).

## B. Power-state selector

- Question: What can a power-state schedule tell us—and what must we observe?
- Form: state table. Chapters: Scope (opening), Responsibilities (who), States (how), Transition (radio observation), Evidence (projection/run distinction), Next action (lesson/contact).
- Object: seven solid radial switch modules form an original teaching selector. It is not a device Ada built.
- Transformations: opening circular selector; states become an opened teaching sequence; evidence groups distinguish estimate and observed run; close selector returns whole.
- Opening asks schedule question; close states that model and physical observation have different evidential roles.
- Stands on Ada's power scheduling, use of state tables, confirmed radio observation and estimate/run lesson. No recovered firmware state sequence claimed.
- Needs nothing additional; supplied Sleep/Measure/Transmit diagram can be a teaching explanation only.
- Why theirs: makes the limits of Mosslink's projected endurance central to her power scheduling story.
- Runnable sketch: /sketch/states (Scope, Transition, Next action).

## C. Observation lead

- Question: Where does a firmware claim meet its evidence?
- Form: timing-diagram reading. Chapters: Signal question (opening), Firmware decision (who/how), State sequence (illustrative), Bench observation (radio issue), Observation window (projection versus run), Reading (answer/contact).
- Object: seven thick cylindrical sleeves form a generic bench observation lead. Metaphorical, not actual equipment/wiring.
- Transformations: opening connected lead; middle separates command and observation along reading axis; evidence divides model/run portions without plotted values; ending reconnects lead.
- Opening connects claim to physical observation. Close states the evidence window rather than an invented debugging resolution.
- Stands on timing diagrams/current traces as working documents, firmware ownership, 72-hour run and user lesson.
- Needs no additional asset. No actual trace available; no synthetic trace presented as evidence.
- Why theirs: the distinction between Mosslink's observed run and projected life determines how the lead opens and what the ending says.
- Runnable sketch: /sketch/lead (Signal question, Observation window, Reading).

## Assessment and scope

All three use one continuing tangible object, meaningful transformations and complete HTML with name, firmware work, contact and evidence limits. A best passes the personal-object test because it is drawn from something Ada actually built. B and C are explicitly teaching metaphors and weaker on personal specificity. Shared sketch colors are provisional, not the later look decision.

Final site also needs ordinary navigation, Mosslink project route, corrected downloadable printable HTML CV, share preview, icon and useful missing-page route. No extra projects, employer/dates, testimonials, external fonts/assets or legal-compliance claims. Publishing is user's decision.
