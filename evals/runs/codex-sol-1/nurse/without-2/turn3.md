## 5. Assessment and Recommendation — Medication scanning

Heading:

> **Why nurses skip the scan**

Present the result prominently:

**Ward scanning compliance: 71% → 94% over nine months**

Directly beside it, state your contribution:

> I carried out forty hours of observation, identified three workarounds, and got scanners moved to the medication room door.

The section unfolds in three parts.

### A. Observe the work

Show the study’s scope:

- Forty hours observing day and night shifts.
- Short interviews with 22 nurses.
- One cardiac ward.

Use the abstract’s line as the editorial anchor:

> **“None was laziness.”**

Explain that the workarounds saved nurses between one and four minutes per round.

### B. Understand the three workarounds

Present three annotated findings:

1. Wristbands that would not scan after showers.
2. Scanners kept on a trolley away from the medication room.
3. Scanning from printed barcode sheets kept in a pocket.

**3D:** A simplified spatial diagram explains the scanner-placement finding. It shows a generic medication-room doorway and a scanner position, without reproducing the hospital’s floor plan. As the visitor reaches the intervention, the scanner moves beside the door.

The wristband and printed-sheet findings use flat diagrams. One substantial spatial explanation is enough.

Each finding remains readable before its animation plays. Desktop may briefly hold the diagram alongside the text, but scrolling remains natural and reversible.

**My contribution**

Observation, identification of the workarounds, and getting the scanners relocated.

**Ward team changes**

Scanner placement, wristband stock, and stopping the printed sheets.

**Recorded result**

Scanning compliance reached 94% in nine months.

Include a short evidence note: this is a result from one ward. Do not imply a demonstrated reduction in medication errors or patient harm.

## The look

The site should feel like a carefully composed clinical working surface: warm paper, dark ink, document rules, generous space, and precise annotations.

| Role | Colour |
|---|---|
| Main background | Ivory `#F4F1E9` |
| Main text | Deep ink `#182B30` |
| Links and emphasis | Dark teal `#17635E` |
| Supporting surfaces | Pale grey-green `#DEE7E2` |
| Sparse annotation marks | Amber `#C58B36` |

Amber is a highlighting colour; small text uses a darker accessible colour. Results use clear labels rather than celebratory effects.

Use **Source Serif 4** for large statements and reflective passages, and **Source Sans 3** for body text, navigation, evidence, and form annotations.

Desktop layouts alternate spacious statements with denser evidence sections. Body text stays at a comfortable reading width. On mobile, headings scale down naturally and documents receive enough width to remain useful.

## Rules for motion, phones, and fallback

The two main animated moments are the opening ISBAR alignment and the scanner-placement explanation. Supporting movement is brief and restrained.

- Scrolling remains under the visitor’s control.
- No required dragging, hover, or animation completion.
- No custom cursor or automatic audio.
- Essential text and links load before visual effects.
- Mobile defaults to flat illustrations and simple transitions.
- Reduced-motion settings remove depth movement and scroll-linked animation.
- A failed or unavailable 3D scene displays a prepared static illustration.
- Browser zoom, keyboard navigation, and screen readers retain the complete story.
- Returning from a case study should preserve the visitor’s homepage position where practical.
