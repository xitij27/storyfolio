The direction is **“Mina Park — a record of work.”** The site follows your job-card and logbook habits: identify the problem, inspect the parts, record the work, and distinguish what has been checked from what remains to do.

There is no 3D in the navigation and no opening animation that delays access.

**The overall look**

The page should resemble a carefully arranged working record, with room for large evidence images.

| Element | Direction |
|---|---|
| Background | Warm paper `#F3EFE6` |
| Main text | Charcoal `#222624` |
| Selected panels and accents | Deep green `#23483E` |
| Links and important annotations | Rust `#A4432B`, used sparingly |
| Dividers and figure backgrounds | Pale grey `#D8DCD8` |
| Headings and body | Instrument Sans |
| Dates, entry numbers, status and technical labels | IBM Plex Mono |

Confirm contrast for actual colour pairings. Links have visible treatment beyond colour.

Use large but readable headings, generous margins, and a consistent grid. Keep paragraphs short and project status prominent. Original photographs retain their texture; avoid heavy filters.

The job-card influence comes through in labels and information order. Avoid distressed paper effects, fake handwriting, decorative stamps, or invented measurements.

**Motion and the two-minute visit**

The bracket is the main moving element. Elsewhere, use brief image transitions and subtle entrances only where they clarify the story.

Keep scrolling native. No scroll hijacking, custom cursor, autoplay sound, or mandatory loading screen. A reviewer can use the navigation to jump directly to work, CV, or contact.

The homepage contains the essential summaries. Longer technical explanation belongs on the project pages. The first screen establishes who you are; the bracket section establishes what you contributed and what remains unverified.

The bracket page includes original and revised views where available, the basis of the weight comparison, relevant simulation information, and a clear manufacturing/testing status. The charger page includes the gear train and housing, your contribution, the observed charging outcome, and your reflection.

Each page provides **Back to selected work**, **Next project**, **CV**, and **Email**. Important figures can be enlarged with accessible controls. Any downloadable technical files require a deliberate choice about what you want to share.

These pages remain useful with static images. A 3D viewer can supplement the bracket record, but the text and figures contain the evidence.

**Phone, reduced motion, and unavailable 3D**

The mobile layout preserves all content in the same order. It removes pinned sequences, avoids horizontal scrolling, and gives photographs and controls enough space.

Load text and a bracket preview before the 3D asset. Provide a static fallback for unsupported browsers, loading failures, or constrained devices. Optional 3D can be closed to return to the image.

Reduced-motion preferences receive static camera views and immediate state changes. Visitors never need to rotate an object to discover a claim, navigate the site, or understand a result.

Use semantic page structure, visible keyboard focus, descriptive links, sufficient contrast, and appropriate image descriptions. Image viewers and menus need keyboard controls and sensible focus behaviour.
