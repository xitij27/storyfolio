## Idea

Question: **How do you know a part is ready to work?**

Form: Mina's job card, using the five fields in the order she described. The page exposes the difference between finding a fault, designing a change and checking a result. The end answers the question by distinguishing her reported physical prototype observation from a bracket still awaiting manufacture. It never states that her CAD bracket is ready for service.

One continuous object: **six newly designed portfolio job-card leaves**, two for each of the bike, bracket and charger. Their faces hold work images if cleared, otherwise ordinary records of the confirmed facts. They remain recognisable together as a shallow paper stack. They are not fictional customer job cards and do not contain customer names, manufactured service histories or signatures.

The cards serve as the evidence layout, not a background sculpture. Separating them communicates symptom versus finding; pairing them communicates project versus contribution; their final status arrangement communicates what is and is not checked. Every relation also has an HTML equivalent.

## Thirty-second and two-minute paths

- First screen: Mina's name, provisional second-year mechanical-engineering description, three work links, a qualified bracket proof preview and contact shortcut. A recruiter need not scroll to identify the work or reach her.
- Persistent small rail: Reported fault / Findings / Parts / Work carried out / Test ride; plain-text compact version on phones. Direct project anchors bypass the animation sequence.
- 'Skip to contact' and 'Skip to content' are keyboard accessible. Addressable anchors support sharing and browser back/forward. Direct anchor entry renders the correct settled scene without replaying previous chapters.
- The full read takes approximately two minutes. Additional project detail uses native inline disclosures and optional images, so the main narrative stays short.

## Motion and future implementation

- Desktop scene: six simple thin rectangular meshes, one camera and restrained lighting. Single object persists throughout; no physics engine or product-model reconstruction required.
- Scroll maps continuously to settled arrangements; card travel happens between reading positions. Cards rest while their evidence is read. Camera changes are small; no fly-through or forced perspective disorientation.
- Native document scroll remains in control. No scroll hijacking, compulsory intro, autoplay sound or forced carousel.
- Suggested future implementation: statically rendered HTML with a small Three.js module for the optional scene; standard CSS for document layout. Add scroll progress only after the accessible baseline works. Framework choice remains open to the host repository.
- Load HTML and opening proof before 3D. Lazy-load the scene, cap render resolution, pause when offscreen or tab is hidden, use compressed responsive images and avoid loading unseen full-size textures. Fall back on context loss or poor frame performance without losing the current chapter.
- Future acceptance targets, not measured results: LCP at most 2.5s, CLS at most 0.1, INP at most 200ms under representative mobile testing. Core content must remain functional if the scene never loads.
