**The idea**

The page answers one question:

**How do you take a building from a drawing to something people can use?**

It follows the order you work in: **Location → Plans → Sections → Elevations → Details → Schedules**.

The cabin supplies the principal visual evidence. Calder Court supplies a clearly attributed account of your responsibility from technical design to handover. Teaching and the school competition show further dimensions of your work without stretching your contribution.

The six sheets form one recognizable drawing set. Each carries genuine drawings, photographs or project records. Their arrangements change to explain the work: a plan opens out, a section aligns with construction photographs, a detail comes forward for inspection. At the end, they return to a complete set.

The sheets are an editorial presentation of your material; they do not pretend to be six existing project documents.

**The page, from top to bottom**

**1. Location — who you are and where the work begins**

The first screen contains your name, Associate at Field & Rowe, your focus on existing buildings and timber, and one clearly labelled cabin photograph or drawing. A visitor immediately gets identity, work and evidence.

The question appears alongside these facts. Your father’s workshop and the Swiss timber-joint book are introduced as influences in text.

The 3D begins as a shallow stack with a genuine cabin detail visible on top. As the visitor scrolls, the sheets gather into an ordered set, then fan out around a location/index sheet.

A second settled view introduces Glasgow and the Kashuby region, with brief sourced education context. The location graphic is an editorial index rather than a travel route; it does not reveal the cabin’s precise address.

Visitors can:

- Jump directly to any chapter.
- Skip to contact.
- Open the featured cabin drawing or photograph.

Approximate desktop length: two screens.

**2. Plans — design made readable**

This chapter presents the cabin through a genuine plan. Your hand drawing and CAD work can sit alongside it if the supplied files support a meaningful comparison.

The main sheet lowers into a broad, readable position. The other five remain visible as an indexed fan. The drawing is large enough to understand its organization; detailed annotations remain available through the full-size viewer.

The accompanying content establishes your ownership of the cabin’s design and drawings. Room functions, dimensions and design explanations will come from the actual evidence rather than assumptions.

Visitors can:

- Open the plan full-size.
- Select hand-drawn or CAD evidence where corresponding files exist.
- Read the cabin’s project record.

Approximate length: one screen.

**3. Sections — drawing, construction and responsibility**

The first beat aligns a real cabin section with photographs of the corresponding construction. The visitor can see a relationship between a drawn decision and something you built.

The sheets stand parallel, like a small inspection display. The section remains visible beside the photographs; the scene does not attempt a speculative reconstruction of the building.

The second beat introduces Calder Court. The sheets close into an ordered technical record, carrying a text-based account of your role:

- Field & Rowe’s project.
- A partner’s concept.
- Your responsibility as project architect from technical design to handover.
- Detailing, wall build-up and site work.

Cabin photographs remain explicitly labelled as cabin evidence. Practice imagery can be added only after permission.

Visitors can:

- Inspect the section and matching photographs.
- Select another documented comparison.
- Expand the Calder Court contribution record.

Approximate length: two screens.

**4. Elevations — what was built, and the wider work**

The first beat pairs a genuine cabin elevation with a completed exterior photograph. This gives the visitor a direct comparison between drawing and built appearance.

The sheets turn face-on into a composed display. The motion settles before close inspection, keeping the work readable.

The second beat introduces the broader project context:

Approximate length: one and three-quarter screens.

**5. Details — close inspection**

One genuine cabin timber detail becomes the focus, accompanied by photographs that actually correspond to it.

A single sheet advances toward the visitor while the related photographic sheets remain alongside it. The movement brings the drawing into view; inspection happens through deliberate controls.

Your workshop and timber-book influences return here, connected to your interest in construction. The book is mentioned in words. Its pages are not reproduced.

Visitors can:

- Open and zoom the detail.
- Reset the view.
- Move between related photographs or another supported detail.

Joint names, drawing scales and technical explanations appear only where the material supports them.

Approximate length: one and a quarter screens.

**6. Schedules — clear scope and the next conversation**

The sheets return to a complete, aligned set. The visible top sheet carries a project-and-contribution schedule.

This is the concluding view: what you own, what you contributed to, and where each project belongs. Credits sit beside the relevant work, with a consolidated credit list below.

The close answers the opening question through the evidence already shown: your own design and construction work, and your professional responsibility for detailing, site work and handover.

Two contact routes serve your intended audience:

- An old-house or small-plot conversation.
- A guest-critic invitation.

Both can use the same supplied email address with different suggested subjects. The address is also visible and selectable. An updated CV is available here once supplied.

The object holds quietly at the end; reaching contact does not require completing another animation.

Approximate length: one and a half screens.

**Navigation and interaction**

The desktop experience is roughly nine-and-a-half viewport heights, adjusted once the real material is available. Visitors control its pace through ordinary scrolling.

A persistent chapter rail shows the six drawing-set names and the current chapter. Links jump directly to a readable state. Scrolling backwards reverses the scene naturally.

Every inspectable image has a visible HTML button such as “View drawing.” Visitors never need to discover a clickable surface in 3D.

Image viewers provide close, zoom/reset and previous/next controls where useful. They support keyboard operation and return focus to the opening control. Project records expand inline without taking visitors away from the page.

The first screen and persistent contact link serve someone with thirty seconds. No visitor needs to complete the story to find your work or reach you.

**The look**

The selected palette comes from drawing paper and ink:

| Element | Selected treatment |
|---|---|
| Ground | Warm ivory `#F2EEE5` |
| Main ink | Charcoal `#242824` |
| Accent | Oxide `#8D432D` |
| 3D sheets | Matte warm paper, crisp lines, restrained shadows |
| Photography | Large, clearly labelled images with visible credits |

The composition uses generous margins, fine rules and a consistent drawing grid. Timber’s texture comes primarily from your photographs.

**Source Sans 3** handles headings and body text. **IBM Plex Mono** handles drawing numbers, dates, scales and schedule labels. Body text starts around 18–20px on desktop and at least 16px on phones.

Two alternatives remain documented: cool tracing paper with a muted green accent, and pale sheets against a dark workshop-board ground. Warm paper is the selected direction; the site does not need a theme chooser.

**On a phone**

The same six chapters become a normal vertical document:

- One readable drawing or photograph at a time.
- Text beneath the evidence.
- Paired comparisons stacked with clear labels.
- A compact chapter menu and accessible contact link.
- Full-size drawing inspection through touch and keyboard controls.

There is no long pinned scene. A brief, shallow sheet treatment can be included if device performance supports it, but the complete experience works through the images and their relationships.

Controls have generous touch targets. Essential information never depends on hover, sideways scrolling or reading tiny annotations inside an animation.

**Without the 3D**

| Situation | Experience |
|---|---|
| Reduced motion | Static chapter arrangements; no automatic rotation, parallax or zoom. Visitors can still deliberately inspect images. |
| No WebGL or weak device | Responsive drawings and photographs, with explicit comparison labels and a static drawing-set composition. |
| No JavaScript | Complete HTML chapters, images, project records, credits, anchor navigation and email links. Full-size drawings open as ordinary files. |

All essential text and controls exist outside the scene. The animation enhances the relationships between evidence; it does not contain the only copy of the evidence.
