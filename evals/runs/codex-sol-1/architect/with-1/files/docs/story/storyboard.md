## Central idea

Question: **How do you take a building from a drawing to something people can use?**

One long page follows Marta's drawing-set order: Location, Plans, Sections, Elevations, Details, Schedules. Six thin 3D sheets stay present throughout. They carry real drawings, photographs and contribution records. Their movement explains relationships: plan to section, section to built work, detail to construction. The sheets return to a complete set at the close.

The cabin is the principal visual case because its design, drawings, photographs and build are hers. Calder Court supplies a different kind of evidence: her confirmed responsibility from technical design to handover, attributed to the practice and partner's concept. The page distinguishes these through labels placed beside each project, rather than expecting the visitor to discover attribution in a footer.

## Look and type

Selected look: **Warm drawing paper**. Alternatives are documented as options, not three skins to build or a palette chooser.

| Look | Ground | Ink | Accent | Sheets |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Warm drawing paper — selected | #F2EEE5 | #242824 | #8D432D oxide | Slightly warm matte paper, restrained shadows, crisp drawing lines; timber appears in real photos rather than synthetic grain everywhere |
| Cool tracing paper | #EDF0EC | #202D2A | #386A61 | Pale translucent-looking sheets; HTML text remains opaque for contrast |
| Workshop drawing board | #222923 | #F4F0E6 | #D5AC78 | Warm pale sheets against a dark board, readable opaque surface; lower tonal emphasis on shadows |

Use **Source Sans 3** for headings and body, and **IBM Plex Mono** for drawing numbers, scales, project roles, dates and schedule labels. Self-host openly licensed fonts. Large but restrained headings; comfortable 18–20px desktop body and at least 16px on phones. Colour does not encode ownership/status alone. Check contrast and keyboard focus; accent is primarily for rules/controls, not small unchecked text.

Composition: generous paper margins, a coherent drawing grid, fine rules, large genuine drawing crops, photographs with visible credits. No oversized generic slogan, orbiting cursor or animated statistics counter. Visual hierarchy makes technical information inviting to a homeowner while preserving precision for an architectural colleague.

## Interaction contract

- Chapter navigation uses real anchor links and shareable section addresses. Active chapter indication is supplemental to clear link labels. Skip links work before the scene loads.
- Ordinary scrolling controls scene transitions; reversing scroll reverses the scene. Chapter jumps go directly to a readable state. A pinned desktop canvas may stay beside flowing HTML, but never captures the wheel, requires dragging or blocks contact.
- A clear HTML “View drawing” or “View photograph” button accompanies every inspectable image; visitors need not discover a 3D hit target. Image viewers provide close, zoom/reset and previous/next controls where appropriate, keyboard equivalents, focus return and descriptive text. Zoom is user-triggered, including in reduced-motion mode.
- Hand-drawn/CAD selection changes the evidence image only if corresponding files actually exist. An image does not morph into an invented intermediate drawing. Section/photo comparisons always show both labelled items and have equivalent stacked content.
- Project records expand inline as accessible disclosures, preserving scroll position and remaining available to screen readers. Cabin records contain owned evidence; practice records contain confirmed scope and approved images only. Third-party material is not a launch dependency.
- The contact routes use one genuine supplied address with different subject suggestions, one for an old house/small plot and one for guest-critic invitations. The address is also visible/selectable for people without an email app. CV link appears only after an accurate PDF is supplied.
- On-screen navigation and controls have at least 44px touch targets, visible focus and clear names; no essential content depends on hover, sound, colour alone or free-form 3D manipulation.
