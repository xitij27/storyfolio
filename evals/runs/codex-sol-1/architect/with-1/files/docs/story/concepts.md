# Three concepts

Selected by delegated judgement, 10 October 2026. User subsequently confirmed the recommended direction: “Go with the direction you recommend.” The expanded storyboard is delivered for review; final captions and implementation remain outside this planning work.

## Forms considered

Architectural drawing set; RIBA Plan of Work; snag list; specification; site diary; drawing register. The first three are explicitly named by Marta (F21); the others are trade forms considered by the planner, not claims about her practice. The drawing set supplies six useful chapters in her stated order without needing invented project chronology, defects or site records.

## 1. The drawing set — selected

- **Question:** How do you take a building from a drawing to something people can use?
- **Form:** Location (opening, identity, origins); Plans (work and approach); Sections (construction and professional scope); Elevations (built evidence and project context); Details (hands-on skill and influences); Schedules (attributions, status, contact and answer). Six chapters in Marta's actual order. Outcomes stay beside the project they describe; no testimonial chapter with nothing to hold.
- **Object:** Six thin drawing sheets, one per chapter. Recognizable as a stacked architectural drawing set at rest. Faces carry Marta's cabin drawings, photographs and factual project entries. The sheets are an editorial arrangement, not an assertion that these six exact documents already exist.
- **Transformations:** Plans lays one sheet flat and fans the others into a drawing index; Sections stands the sheets parallel to compare a section and construction photos; Details brings one real drawing crop forward, with adjacent photographs on the remaining sheets.
- **Opening:** Her workshop and Swiss-joint-book influences are named in ordinary text while a cabin joint/detail drawing appears on the top sheet. No borrowed book reproduction or invented workshop image. The sheets gather into the set.
- **Close:** The same six sheets rest as one complete set; the schedule connects evidence, contribution and next conversation. The question is answered by the progression from drawings through construction to the completed cabin, and her confirmed technical-to-handover role on Calder Court.
- **Stands on:** F01–F03, F10, F13–F14, F17, F20–F23. Dated education/teaching references use F07–F09 with source status preserved.
- **Needs:** Actual cabin drawings/photos; contact destination. No third-party approval required to support the main visual story. Work assets remain later additions requiring rights.
- **Why hers:** The set follows her own working order and moves between a cabin she owns and her specifically bounded technical role on a practice retrofit, anchored in the workshop and timber-joint book she named.

## 2. The project stages

- **Question:** What does it take to turn a building idea into a finished place?
- **Form:** RIBA stages 0 Strategic Definition, 1 Preparation and Briefing, 2 Concept Design, 3 Spatial Coordination, 4 Technical Design, 5 Manufacturing and Construction, 6 Handover, 7 Use. Eight chapters: intent; identity/context; cabin design; drawings; technical role; build; completed evidence; future conversation. These are editorial lenses, not a claim that the cabin followed a documented RIBA appointment.
- **Object:** Eight stage-file cards held together as one project dossier, carrying cabin drawings, construction photographs and contribution records.
- **Transformations:** Stage 2 spreads the cards into design evidence; Stage 4 aligns drawings and technical responsibilities; Stage 6 pairs evidence of construction and completion. One dossier returns at Stage 7.
- **Opening:** A timber-detail drawing occupies the first card, with her influences identified in text. The name and question sit beside the whole dossier.
- **Close:** Stage 7 offers a conversation about an old house/small plot or a guest-critic invitation, with no invented in-use result.
- **Stands on:** F10, F14, F17, F20–F23.
- **Needs:** Cabin files spanning design and construction; no stage-specific claim without evidence. A meaningful Use chapter needs information not currently supplied; keep its role to future inquiry, not measured performance.
- **Why hers:** Makes visible the precise boundary of her Calder Court role and contrasts it with ownership of the cabin's design and build.
- **Assessment:** More explanatory, but eight stages require visitors to learn an industry process and risk implying a comprehensive documented project history. Less direct than the drawing set for a homeowner.

## 3. A timber drawing set

- **Question:** How does knowing how something is built change the way you draw it?
- **Form:** Location, Plans, Sections, Elevations, Details, Schedules, in the same real drawing-set order. Opening/origins; work; construction; visible results; close inspection; contributions and contact.
- **Object:** Six rectangular timber panels assembled as an abstract folded cabin study. Each carries an owned drawing or photograph on a face; it is explicitly an editorial model, not a dimensionally accurate cabin or a buildable joint.
- **Transformations:** Plans lowers the panels into a layout with a real plan printed across their faces; Sections stands them into a cutaway arrangement; Details separates them enough to expose drawing crops and matching photographs. The close brings all six together.
- **Opening:** Six timber panels gather from a flat arrangement, with Marta's cabin drawings and named workshop influence, avoiding a fabricated replica of her father's workshop.
- **Close:** The abstract model returns whole; a schedule on its faces records the cabin's ownership and her different scope on practice work.
- **Stands on:** F10, F14, F17, F20–F23.
- **Needs:** Actual drawings to avoid misleading geometry; cabin photographs; no book scans or practice images needed.
- **Why hers:** Directly joins her workshop influence, her own timber build and her technical detailing work.
- **Assessment:** Stronger physical presence, but the invented panel model could draw attention away from the actual drawings and imply construction geometry. The drawing sheets keep evidence easier to inspect.

## Choice and checks

Choose concept 1 over 2 and 3: it makes the strongest use of the confirmed workflow, gives six meaningful transformations and needs the fewest unsupported details. Palette, opening treatment and order chosen under F23; subject to storyboard review.

All three retain the same object from beginning to end, show evidence on its faces, have a return-to-whole ending, and offer name/work/proof/contact without scrolling. Removing 3D would remove the visual relation between drawings and built evidence, but the accessible HTML preserves that relation explicitly. No generic architectural emblem, fabricated defect list, testimonial or borrowed scene is required.
