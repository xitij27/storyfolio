## The idea: “Worth keeping”

Your portfolio should feel like an architect opening a building up to explain it: what was there, what you changed, and why it matters.

The thread comes directly from your material: existing buildings, lower heating demand, thoughtful housing, timber, teaching, and making things with your hands. Calder Court establishes the scale of your professional work. The cabin you built with your father makes that philosophy personal.

The distinctive element would be **a scroll-controlled architectural cutaway**, drawn from a real project detail. It lets visitors understand your work through depth, materials, and construction. Photography, drawings, and your words carry the rest of the story.

The site’s main job is to help a potential collaborator, client, or employer understand your judgment—and find evidence of it quickly.

## Colours and typography

The visual language should borrow from drawing paper, graphite, timber, and the colours present in the selected photographs.

| Use | Proposed colour |
|---|---|
| Main background | Warm paper — `#F3F0E8` |
| Text and drawing lines | Graphite — `#242826` |
| Secondary surfaces | Stone — `#D8D5CC` |
| Links, diagram emphasis, active states | Deep green — `#315B4C` |
| Occasional material accent | Timber brown — `#946747` |

Use green sparingly to connect navigation and technical annotations. Keep brown decorative unless its contrast is verified. Final colour tuning should happen after the photography is selected.

For type, use **a restrained serif for major statements** and **a clear sans serif for reading and drawing labels**. A practical starting pair is **Newsreader + IBM Plex Sans**, self-hosted.

Headings should feel spacious and assured. Body copy stays comfortably readable; captions remain large enough to use. Avoid tiny technical text, oversized blocks of uppercase, and decorative typography over important images.

## Motion and 3D rules

The signature experience is the Calder Court cutaway. Give it the production attention needed to feel accurate and intentional.

- Tie scroll progress to a short, understandable sequence: existing condition, intervention, result.
- Keep the camera mostly steady so the building remains legible.
- Use subtle transitions elsewhere: drawing lines, image reveals, and changes of scale.
- Preserve normal scrolling, visible navigation, and browser back behavior.
- Provide the same explanation through static diagrams when 3D is unavailable or motion is reduced.

An endless orbiting model would add little to the story. A precise construction reveal gives the movement a reason to exist.

## How it works on a phone

The phone version should have its own composition.

The opening becomes a strong image, statement, and project link. The Calder Court explanation becomes three stacked steps with static section views; a lightweight 3D enhancement can be considered only after performance testing.

All projects use a single column. Before-and-after photographs stack with labels. Drawings open into a zoomable view with an accessible close control. The cabin sequence becomes a browsable gallery, and video loads after the visitor chooses to play it.

Keep essential content visible without hovering, dragging, or waiting for animation. Respect reduced-motion settings, maintain readable contrast and touch targets, and prevent decorative layers from blocking links.

## What else the site needs

**A homepage plus four project pages.** The homepage tells the connected story; individual pages give each project a shareable address and room for drawings, process, credits, and your contribution.

**A consistent project structure.** Each page should establish project status, location, date, practice, your role, collaborators, challenge, decisions, and outcome. Competition work should remain clearly identified.

**An asset and attribution pass.** The folder contains descriptions of available imagery, not the files themselves. Select assets from those collections, confirm web-use permissions for practice work and Tom Reid’s photographs, and record credits. Derive any 3D geometry from approved drawings.

**Content verification.** Resolve your current title, confirm the heating-demand claim, document your role in each practice project, and obtain your contact details and short personal accounts.

**Accessibility and performance from the outset.** Use semantic text, keyboard navigation, useful image descriptions, captions, responsive images, and a complete static fallback. Load the 3D scene progressively so visitors can read and navigate immediately.

**Practical publishing support.** Include search metadata, social-sharing images, an accessible CV, a useful error page, and an easy way to update projects. Choose analytics and any privacy requirements based on the services actually used.
