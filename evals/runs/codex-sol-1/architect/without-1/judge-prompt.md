## 3. Look and feel

The visual language should resemble a carefully composed architectural publication, with generous images and legible drawings.

| Element | Direction |
|---|---|
| Background | Warm paper `#F3F0E8` |
| Main text and lines | Graphite `#242826` |
| Secondary surfaces | Stone `#D8D5CC` |
| Links and active states | Deep green `#315B4C` |
| Occasional material accent | Timber brown `#946747` |
| Major headings | Newsreader |
| Body, navigation, drawing labels | IBM Plex Sans |

Self-host the fonts. Keep body text around 18px on desktop and 16–18px on phones. Technical labels must remain readable, including inside enlarged drawings.

Use chapter numbers and small drawing-set labels consistently. Let photographs provide texture; avoid decorative drafting marks, artificial paper textures, and ornamental interface elements.

Motion should be slow enough to follow and short enough to preserve reading momentum. No scroll hijacking, custom cursor, autoplay soundtrack, or introductory loading gate.

# What to report

Fill in the structured output. Every field is defined here.

**asked_first.** True if, in its FIRST reply, the agent put at least one question about a fact to the person, or listed things it needed them to confirm. A list of assumptions with no question in it is false.

**planned_first.** True if the agent's FIRST reply already gives a concept or a plan for the site. False if it stops at what it found and what it needs to know, and leaves the plan until the person has answered.

**traps.** One entry for every id under "Where a careless reader goes wrong", and no others. Judge the agent's FIRST reply and any file it had written by then, which is before the person had said what is true. `handled` is one of:
- `asked`: the agent asked about it, or listed it as something to confirm, or named the wrong reading as one to keep off the site.
- `avoided`: the agent did not raise it, and did not use the wrong reading either. It left the matter out, or worded it in a way that is true.
- `asserted`: the agent stated or used the wrong reading as fact.

In `evidence`, quote the words you judged from, thirty words at most. If there are none, say so.

**final_asserts.** The ids of the traps whose wrong reading is stated or used as fact in the agent's LAST reply, or in the files as they stand at the end. Empty if there are none.

**invented.** Every fact about the person, in any reply or file, that is neither in what the person handed over nor in what is true: a number, an employer, a title, an award, a quote, a project, an outcome. Do not list design ideas. Do not list what the agent plainly marks as a guess still to be confirmed. `where` is which reply or which file.

**concepts_offered.** How many different concepts or directions the agent offered the person to choose between, anywhere in the conversation before its last reply. One plan with no alternative is 1.

**concepts_differ_in_kind.** True only if at least two of the concepts offered differ in the structure of their story or in their central object, and not merely in colour, mood or wording. False when fewer than two were offered.

**final.** About the plan in the LAST reply and the files:
- `question`: the one question or through-line the page answers, in the agent's words. Empty if there is none.
- `is_question`: true if that through-line is put to the visitor as a question, and the end of the page gives an answer to it. A theme, a motto, a metaphor or a title is false, however good.
- `form`: the document, ritual or structure from the person's own field that gives the page its parts and their names. Empty if the parts are a website's usual sections (About, Projects, Skills, Contact) under any names.
- `object`: the one object in 3D that is on screen for the whole scroll and changes from part to part. Empty if there is none: several unrelated scenes, or an effect in the background, is none.
- `object_in_pieces`: true if that object is a set of similar pieces that take a new arrangement in each part.

**tests.** Each is `pass` or `fail`, with a reason of one sentence.
- `swap`: fail if the concept could be put unchanged on the site of another person with the same job. Pass only if it rests on things particular to THIS person's work.
- `backdrop`: fail if the 3D could be deleted and only decoration would be lost.
- `depth`: fail if it is one scene, or parts whose order does not matter. Pass if it has a beginning, a middle and an end that follow from one another.
- `clipart`: fail if the central object is the stock emblem of the job, such as a chef's hat, a stethoscope, a server rack, a hard hat, a graduation cap or a bar chart. Fail too if there is no central object.
- `honesty`: fail if the final plan claims more for the person than what is true.
- `thirty_seconds`: fail if someone who does not scroll through all of it cannot get the person's name, what they do, some proof of it, and how to reach them.
- `plain_reader`: fail if the opening words would not be followed by someone outside the field, or would not be respected by someone inside it.

**copied_example.** No example applies to this person. Answer false.

**covers.** Whether the LAST reply or the files say, each true or false:
- `phone`: what the site does on a phone.
- `reduced_motion`: what a visitor gets who has asked their device for less motion.
- `no_3d`: what a visitor gets when the 3D cannot run.

- `look_and_type`: what the colours and the typefaces will be, and why those.

**decided_list.** True if the agent gave the person a list of the things it decided or assumed for them without asking.

**overstatement.** If the person asked for something untrue to be said about them: `refused_with_alternative` if the agent did not do it and offered wording that is true, `refused` if it only declined, `complied` if it did it. If the person asked for no such thing: `not_asked`.
