I recommend **“From place to joint”**: a portfolio organised around the way you understand architecture, moving from a building’s setting to its spaces, construction and care.

The cabin leads because its design, drawings, photographs and construction are yours. Calder Court then demonstrates your experience delivering technically demanding work within a practice. The site should help someone with an old house or a small plot understand your judgement, and help a school understand what you bring to teaching.

This is the complete design plan. Nothing will be built at this stage.

**1. The page structure**

The homepage is one continuous story with nine parts. Visitors can also open individual project pages without following the whole scroll journey.

A quiet header contains your name and four links: **Work, Approach, Teaching, Contact**. It stays available as a compact bar after the opening. Your name returns to the top. No loading screen precedes the content.

**Part 1 — Location: the opening**

A large photograph places the cabin in its landscape. Your name and introduction sit beside or beneath it, with enough plain background for comfortable reading.

Proposed copy:

> **Marta Kowalczyk**  
> Architecture, from place to joint.  
> Associate at Field & Rowe Architects, Glasgow.  
> Existing buildings, small spaces and timber.

Below it:

> My work begins with what is there: a place, a building, a material. This portfolio follows that work from the first drawing to the things that need checking on site.

A small caption identifies **Cabin at Kashuby, Poland · 28 m² · 2021–22**.

- **3D:** None at arrival. The real photograph establishes the work immediately. Further down, a location drawing introduces the footprint and setting.
- **Clicks:** “Explore the work” scrolls to the plans section. “Get in touch” goes to contact. The cabin caption links to its project page.
- **Phone:** Photograph, name and introduction stack vertically. The opening does not depend on fitting everything into one screen.
- **Without 3D:** Identical experience.

**Part 2 — Plans: how a small building works**

Introduce the cabin through its plan and a small selection of interior photographs. The drawing is large enough to read, with a few annotations explaining actual design decisions.

The writing answers questions a prospective client might have: how the space is organised, how it relates to the site, and what makes 28 square metres useful. Those explanations must come from your drawings and account of the project.

- **3D:** None. A clear plan is the right tool here.
- **Clicks:** Selecting a numbered plan marker changes the adjacent photograph and caption. All photographs are also available in an ordinary gallery. “View the plan” opens an enlarged drawing; “Read the cabin project” opens the full case study.
- **Phone:** The plan appears first, followed by the photographs and explanations. Marker controls are large and optional.
- **Without 3D:** Identical experience. If interactive controls fail, the images and captions remain in document order.

**Part 3 — Sections: the main spatial sequence**

This is the site’s principal 3D moment.

A simplified model of the cabin, reconstructed from your drawings, occupies one side of the desktop layout. Three short explanations pass beside it:

1. **The whole:** the complete cabin, with a camera angle aligned to a real photograph.
2. **The section:** part of the enclosure moves aside to expose the interior and timber frame.
3. **The assembly:** selected construction layers separate slightly, with labels explaining how they meet.

The model returns to its assembled state before the next section. Nothing spins continuously. Visitors never need to learn camera controls.

The sequence should occupy roughly two to three desktop screen heights, with a visible way to skip ahead.

- **3D:** One controlled cutaway and assembly sequence. It shows verified construction, with no invented concealed details.
- **Clicks:** “Whole,” “Section” and “Assembly” buttons select the same states directly. “Skip animation” advances to the construction photographs. A play button opens your frame-raising time-lapse.
- **Phone:** Three annotated still drawings replace the pinned sequence. The time-lapse remains available on request.
- **Without 3D:** The same three drawings and explanations appear. Reduced-motion settings receive this version automatically.

**Part 4 — Elevations: making existing buildings useful**

The page moves from your cabin to work within Field & Rowe.

Calder Court receives a substantial feature: before and after photographs, a concise explanation of the retrofit, your contribution and a clearly qualified performance figure. Kirk Street follows as a smaller feature about converting a church hall into six flats.

- **Clicks:** Before/after buttons switch between aligned images, with both images also accessible separately. Each project has a “Read project” link.
- **Phone:** Before and after appear as labelled, stacked photographs. Avoid a comparison slider that makes inspection difficult.
- **Without 3D:** Identical experience.
- **Publication condition:** Practice photography and drawings appear only after the necessary permissions. Until then, use accurate text summaries and project facts; do not substitute invented renders.

Kirk Street’s role description remains a content item to confirm before publication.

**Part 5 — Details: where your judgement becomes visible**

A short personal passage connects this attention to your father’s workshop and the Swiss timber-joint book you have kept since nineteen. The book belongs in the story as an influence; scans are unnecessary.

If permission is obtained, Calder’s wall build-up appears alongside the cabin detail, explicitly identifying your technical contribution within the practice project.

- **3D:** Reuse the cabin model for one close view of a documented joint. A button can separate its components slightly. No second elaborate scene is needed. If the available drawings do not establish the joint precisely, use the drawing and photograph instead.
- **Clicks:** “See the assembly,” “View drawing” and labelled image enlargement.
- **Phone:** A large detail photograph, drawing and explanation stack vertically.
- **Without 3D:** A static assembled view and an annotated assembly diagram convey the same information.

**2. The look**

The site should resemble a carefully edited architectural publication, with movement revealing the construction.

| Element | Direction |
|---|---|
| Background | Warm paper, `#F2EFE7` |
| Main text | Charcoal, `#252824` |
| Occasional dark surface | Deep pine, `#34483E` |
| Drawing highlights and selected accents | Oxide, `#A34832` |
| Headlines and personal passages | Newsreader |
| Body text, navigation and labels | Instrument Sans |
| Images | Large, deliberate crops; preserve the material character of the originals |
| Drawings | Clean backgrounds, legible linework, consistent annotations |

Use generous margins and an asymmetric desktop grid: large images balanced by shorter explanatory columns. Body text should be approximately 18–20 pixels; captions remain comfortably readable. Headlines scale down fluidly on smaller screens.

Colour must never carry drawing meaning alone. Labels, line styles and a clear legend accompany it. Check contrast for every actual colour pairing before implementation.

**3. Interaction rules**

All navigation uses ordinary links. Project pages have stable URLs, so visitors can bookmark and share them.

Image enlargement opens an accessible viewer with captions, credits, previous/next controls, keyboard support and a clear close button. Video plays only when requested. No custom cursor, sound, scroll hijacking or obligatory animation.

A small drawing-set progress indicator may accompany the desktop page. It identifies the current section and acts as navigation. On phones, the compact menu provides those links without taking over the screen.

**4. Project pages and RIBA stages**

Each project page contains:

- Opening image and factual summary.
- Your role, collaborators and attribution.
- The problem, decisions and resulting work.
- Selected drawings and photographs with captions.
- Construction or performance information where supported.
- Credits and a link to the next project.

For Calder, highlight your involvement from **Stage 4: Technical Design** through **Stage 5: Manufacturing and Construction** to **Stage 6: Handover**. Identify planned monitoring under **Stage 7: Use**, without implying that your involvement there is established.

The complete stage vocabulary is 0 Strategic Definition, 1 Preparation and Briefing, 2 Concept Design, 3 Spatial Coordination, 4 Technical Design, 5 Manufacturing and Construction, 6 Handover, and 7 Use. Only show a project’s stage history where the facts support it.

**Design and production assets**

- A checked cabin model derived from your drawings.
- Static equivalents for every 3D state.
- Consistent drawing exports, annotations and legends.
- Image crops, alternative text, captions and credits.

**Site infrastructure**

- Domain, hosting and professional email.
- A simple content editor for project text, images, credits and updates.
- Draft preview and a publication checklist for rights and attribution.
- Responsive images, deferred 3D loading and video loaded on request.
- Accessible navigation, viewers, controls and reduced-motion behaviour.
- Search metadata, sitemap, project URLs and social previews.
- Useful missing-page and loading-failure states.
- HTTPS, backups and a maintenance process.
- Minimal analytics only if you want them, with the appropriate privacy arrangements.

**Validation before launch**

Check the whole site on real phones, slower connections, keyboard navigation and with motion disabled. Confirm that every important explanation survives without WebGL or JavaScript. Verify credits, role descriptions, dates and performance wording against the final content.

**6. Assumptions**

I’m assuming Glasgow and its surrounding area are the initial audience; your independent practice is a two-year ambition; and no third-party publication permissions have yet been secured.

The visual selections remain dependent on the actual assets. The Swiss book can inform your personal story without reproducing its pages. The initial contact route is email, and the launch can lead confidently with owned cabin material while practice imagery awaits clearance.
