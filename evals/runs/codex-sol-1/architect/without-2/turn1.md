**The idea and visual character**

An architectural publication brought into motion: generous photographs, crisp drawings, large but restrained typography, and occasional spatial sequences that explain the work.

The distinctive element would be a building section that opens as you scroll. Existing fabric, new insulation and winter gardens separate just enough to make the intervention understandable, then settle back into the completed building. The movement has a purpose: showing what you retained, what you added, and why.

Avoid generic floating objects, cinematic fly-throughs and an elaborate opening loader. Your name, architectural position and first project should be visible immediately.

**How the project pages work**

Each project needs its own pacing, while sharing a clear information structure:

- A strong opening image, project summary, location, dates and status.
- Your precise role and contribution.
- The architectural problem, key decisions and resulting spaces.
- Selected photographs and drawings with useful captions.
- Practice, collaborators and photography credits.
- A link to the next project and back to selected work.

Calder Court deserves the deepest technical explanation. Kirk Street can be shorter and spatial. The cabin should read like a construction journal. The school entry should clearly identify itself as a shortlisted competition proposal.

Field & Rowe projects must carry explicit practice attribution. The site should distinguish your contribution from the work of the wider team.

Build planning should include:

- **Search and sharing:** descriptive project URLs, page titles, social preview images and indexable project text.
- **Contact:** a direct email link initially; a form only if you want to maintain one.
