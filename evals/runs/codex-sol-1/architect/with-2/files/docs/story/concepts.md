### 1. The Drawing Set — selected

**Question:** How do you turn an architectural idea into something that can be built?

**Form and chapter roles, in Marta's order:**

1. Location — opening, identity, where she works and what informed her.
2. Plans — the cabin as authored work; space and intended visitor interests.
3. Sections — structure, school competition contribution and teaching.
4. Elevations — the cabin as built, then an honest index of projects and roles.
5. Details — Calder Court technical design-to-handover contribution and her approach to making.
6. Schedules — a clear evidence/role register, qualifications/source-backed background, contact and the answer.

**Object:** One set of six architectural drawing sheets, one per category. Simple thin rectangular meshes; at rest recognisable as an indexed stack of drawings. Faces carry actual Marta-owned cabin material where suitable. If a matching drawing category is absent, that sheet carries an accurately captioned owned image or editorial index, not a fabricated technical drawing.

**Three transformations:** Plans fans the sheets into a plan/image relationship; Sections stands the same sheets as parallel sectional planes; Details spaces them into a clearly labelled sequence of drawing, construction image and finished condition. Schedules gathers them into the complete indexed set. These show relationships and provenance, not an unsupported simulation of material layers.

**Opening:** A crop of one of Marta's own hand-drawn cabin sheets comes into view; five other sheets settle beneath it. Text names her father's workshop and the Swiss timber-joint book as influences, without showing an invented workshop or the book's pages. Name, Associate role, work focus, owned cabin proof and contact are available immediately.

**Close:** The set is complete. Proposed answer: “An idea becomes buildable through drawings, details and attention on site.” Two contact routes address future projects and guest criticism. Present role remains visible.

**Grounding:** F01–F05, F07–F10, F15, F18; A01, A02, A06–A10. Conditional project details/figures remain omitted as in facts.md.

**Needs:** Actual cabin assets. Optional cleared Calder detail and school section would deepen individual chapters but are not required. No employer or book reproduction assumed.

**Why it is Marta's:** Her exact working order carries her own cabin drawings alongside her specific technical and structural contributions, opening with the influences she named.

### 2. From Brief to Use — alternative

**Question:** What does it take to carry a building idea through to use?

**Form and chapter roles:** RIBA stages in order: 0 Strategic Definition (identity and intentions), 1 Preparation and Briefing (who the site is for), 2 Concept Design (owned cabin design), 3 Spatial Coordination (drawing relationships and school sections), 4 Technical Design (Calder detailing responsibility), 5 Manufacturing and Construction (owned cabin construction photos), 6 Handover (Calder role through handover), 7 Use (distinguish modelled performance from unmeasured use; close/contact). These are an editorial organising framework, not a claim that Marta led all stages of every project.

**Object:** Eight thin document folios, each holding an owned image/drawing or explicitly editorial role note. They stay as one stage-indexed project file throughout the page.

**Three transformations:** At stage 2, the folios reveal authored cabin design; at stage 4, a sheet aligns drawing and responsibility captions; at stage 6, folios assemble an evidence record with authorship beside each item. At stage 7 they return to the full file, with no fictional monitoring chart.

**Opening:** The file opens on Marta's own cabin drawing, acknowledging the workshop/book influence in text. **Close:** “Design needs attention through construction and into use” as proposed editorial answer, with contact.

**Grounding:** F04, F15, F18; A02, A03, A06–A10.

**Needs:** Owned cabin files; practice rights only for optional additions. No in-use results are available. Avoid suggesting a new independent practice already exists.

**Why it is Marta's:** Authored timber construction and her defined technical-to-handover practice role make the stages tangible rather than a generic process graphic.

**Tradeoff:** Eight chapters require more explanation for house owners, and evidence for stages 0, 1 and 7 is thin. The drawing set gives the same understanding with less padding.

### 3. The Site Record — alternative

**Question:** How do you make clear what still needs attention before handover?

**Form and chapter roles:** The snag list, using conventional columns as reading chapters: Location (opening/context), Item (work index), Required action (how drawings and construction relate), Responsibility (exact authorship and project role), Status (documented outcomes and contact). These column names are a proposed format, not an assertion about Marta's firm's template. No actual defect or resolved snag is invented.

**Object:** Five leaves of one site checklist, carrying real owned cabin construction photographs and accurately marked drawing excerpts. They remain visible as one paper record; any annotation must describe a visible feature, never an invented fault.

**Three transformations:** Location places the leaves beside the relevant owned image; Required action pairs drawing and construction evidence without claiming a defect; Responsibility arranges leaves into an attribution register. Status closes the completed editorial record; no fabricated ticked-off site work.

**Opening:** A real cabin construction image is revealed on the first leaf, with workshop influence acknowledged in text. **Close:** “Clear drawings and clear responsibilities keep the next step understandable” as proposed editorial answer, with contact.

**Grounding:** A02, A06, A08–A10; F18.

**Needs:** Actual owned files; a real cleared snag record would materially strengthen this option. None has been provided.

**Why it is Marta's:** It foregrounds her stated site work and concern with technical completion, supported by construction she authored herself.

**Tradeoff:** A snag list is strongest with real site items. Without them it risks imposing the language of defects on work we have no evidence was defective; it also gives design authorship and teaching less room.

## Choice and checks

Chosen: **The Drawing Set**, initially under A11's explicit delegation of unanswered choices, then explicitly confirmed by Marta: “Go with the direction you recommend.” Chosen over RIBA stages for a shorter, more visual story and over the snag list for fuller evidence and better fit to both audiences.

- Swap: the chapter order is Marta's stated order; contents depend on her cabin, workshop/book influences and exact project contributions.
- Backdrop: sheet transformations expose relationships among drawings, construction and attribution. Static equivalents preserve those relationships when 3D is unavailable.
- Depth: move from location and space through structure, built work and technical detail to a complete record and an invitation.
- Emblem: actual drawing sheets from her work, rather than a generic floating house or architect's tools.
- Honesty: no partner concept credited to Marta; no measured performance, uncleared images, fictional snags or invented workshop scene.
- Thirty seconds: opening contains name, current role, work focus, owned cabin proof and a contact link; chapter jumps expose work and roles without full scrolling.

The selection is recorded for continuity, not an approval to build. Next: complete storyboard for Marta's review.
