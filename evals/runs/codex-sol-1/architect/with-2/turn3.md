**The page, from top to bottom**

**1. Location — your introduction and starting point**

The first screen immediately gives visitors your name, your role as Associate at Field & Rowe, your focus on existing buildings and timber, and one clear example of your own cabin work. Contact is visible immediately.

A short introduction acknowledges your father’s workshop and the Swiss timber-joint book. These are personal influences expressed through text and your own material; reproducing the book or reconstructing an imaginary workshop is unnecessary.

The opening 3D object is one of your original hand-drawn cabin sheets, nearly facing the viewer. Five sheets sit beneath it. During the first scroll, they gently gather into an indexed drawing set. All six remain recognisable throughout the page.

The next settled state introduces the drawing-set order and a compact professional background. It avoids turning your career into an elaborate timeline.

Visitors can click:

- Any chapter in the navigation.
- Contact, which jumps to the closing section.
- The cabin image or drawing to examine it at full size.

Approximately **one and a half desktop screens**. Identity and proof appear before any movement is required.

**2. Plans — your authored work**

The cabin becomes the first substantial project. Visitors see a real plan beside an owned photograph that helps them understand the space.

The emphasis is on your authorship: design, drawings, photographs and build. Specific design explanations will come from your drawings or your words once the files are examined. The page will not invent a brief, budget, occupant story or reason for a design decision.

The six sheets fan horizontally. The selected plan comes forward; a corresponding photograph sits alongside it. A second beat settles the pair into a readable comparison.

This makes the movement useful: visitors can understand the relationship between a drawing and what was built.

Visitors can click:

- The plan to open a readable, zoomable version.
- The corresponding photograph.
- A cabin project anchor that can be shared directly.

Approximately **one and a half desktop screens**.

**The overall movement**

The desktop experience occupies roughly **eight to nine screen lengths**, adjusted to the actual content. Short sticky passages let a comparison settle before the next chapter begins. Ordinary browser scrolling controls everything.

The same six sheets persist from beginning to end. Their faces contain real work or clearly identified editorial records. There is no required dragging, camera control or animation-watching before visitors can reach information.

Chapter links are real anchors. Shared links open the relevant content directly. A visitor with thirty seconds can get your identity, focus, proof and contact from the opening.

**The look**

The selected treatment is **Workshop paper**.

| Element | Direction |
|---|---|
| Page ground | Warm off-white `#F3F0E8` |
| Main text | Graphite `#242824` |
| Accent | Oxide `#914D35` |
| Sheets | Matte cream, fine edges and restrained shadows |
| Texture | Original drawing marks and timber photographs |

Oxide marks links, the active chapter and occasional annotations. The page stays quiet enough for the drawings and photographs to carry its character.

**IBM Plex Sans** handles headings and prose. **IBM Plex Mono** handles drawing identifiers, register labels and technical information. Original lettering remains visible within your drawings.

Body text is comfortably sized—approximately 18–20px on desktop and 16–18px on phones—with generous line spacing. Headings resize fluidly. Technical labels remain readable rather than becoming decorative microtype.

**Phone and fallback behavior**

| Situation | Experience |
|---|---|
| **Phone with 3D** | Single-column content, full-width images, compact sheet scenes and shallow transitions. Drawing/photo pairs stack vertically. No long pinned passages. |
| **Reduced motion** | Static sheet arrangements preserve each chapter’s relationships. No animated assembly, parallax or interpolation. |
| **No WebGL** | Static compositions of the drawing set replace the scene. All text, drawings, photographs, credits and contact remain available. |
| **No JavaScript** | Complete HTML page with chapter anchors, real images and email links. Image links open full-size files. |

On phones, navigation becomes a simple chapter menu. Contact stays readily accessible without covering content. Every action works by touch; nothing depends on hover.

The image viewer supports zoom, closing, keyboard operation and returning focus to the original link. Next/previous controls appear only where there is a real image sequence.

All meaningful content exists outside the canvas. Screen readers receive ordinary headings, text, image descriptions and links. Drawings receive adjacent explanations where an image description alone would be insufficient.
