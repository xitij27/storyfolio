**The idea and the first impression**

The site should feel like entering your kitchen before anyone arrives: shelves, handwritten labels, glass, paper, and evidence of work in progress. Beautiful, but lived in.

The opening copy could be:

> **Good food starts long before service.**  
> I’m Ines Okafor, head chef at Saltmarsh in Leeds. I cook with the whole vegetable, build a larder, and teach what I learn.

Beside it, a carefully art-directed 3D fragment of the jar wall: a few convincing jars, with natural imperfections, photographed contents, and readable labels. Your real larder determines their appearance.

A small mouse movement changes the viewing angle. Scrolling carries us closer to one jar and into the story behind it. On touchscreens, the scroll alone drives the movement.

Your name, role, and links must be visible immediately. The visitor can enjoy the scene or go directly to the work.

**Colours, type, and photographic treatment**

The palette should come from the material: warm paper, dark ink, amber liquid, green glass, and faded labels. A starting set:

| Colour | Proposed value | Role |

| Warm paper | `#F3EBDD` | Main background |

| Bottle green | `#35483D` | Dark sections and structural details |
| Ferment amber | `#A65B27` | Small accents and selected states |

Use **Newsreader** for expressive headlines, **IBM Plex Sans** for clear body text, and a little **IBM Plex Mono** for dates and jar labels. These are available as open-source fonts. Oversized headlines and small practical annotations create contrast without making the page difficult to read.

Keep Priya’s photographs consistent in their colour treatment. Let your phone photographs retain their immediacy: they show the working kitchen. Olu’s drawings should remain recognisably their own contribution, with credit. Avoid adding generic botanical illustrations when you already have original material.

**How the movement earns its place**

The core motion tells a specific story: shelf → ingredient → process → plate → shared knowledge. Use one carefully made 3D scene and a few transitions that support that sequence.

Scrolling should remain native, with no forced speed or compulsory animation. Links work immediately; text never requires motion to become readable. A still image appears first while the 3D scene loads, so the first impression is fast and complete.

With reduced motion enabled, the same story becomes a sequence of still photographs and ordinary sections.

**How it holds up on a phone**

Mobile gets its own composition. The introduction comes first, followed by a close crop of the jar scene. Longer desktop transitions become short reveals; ingredient stories stack vertically with their captions beside the relevant image.

Dish photographs remain large. Brine uses readable previews with optional full-screen viewing. Navigation and contact controls have comfortable touch targets. Nothing depends on hovering, dragging, or precise gestures.

On devices that struggle with 3D, use the photographic version of the same composition. The content and visual identity remain complete.
