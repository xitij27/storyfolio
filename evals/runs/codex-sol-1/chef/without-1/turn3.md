**I recommend “The menu comes last”: a portfolio built around the order in which you actually work—prep list, ferment log, what’s ready, then the menu.** The jar wall supplies the visual identity; your cooking, teaching, and records give the movement its purpose.

The site is primarily for prospective apprentices, workshop visitors, and food writers. It should make those routes obvious from the first screen, while giving people a distinctive story to explore.

Everything below is a plan. Nothing has been built. Suggested copy is marked by quotation formatting; dish stories and log examples will come from your real material.

**The page, in order**

**1. Header and navigation**

Your name appears at the top left, with “Chef · Leeds” beneath it. Navigation links lead to Cooking, Learning, Brine, and About. A prominent “See workshops” link leads directly to the workshop section.

The header is slim, with generous spacing and a warm-paper background. It remains available as people scroll, becoming more compact after the introduction.

There is no 3D in the header. It provides a steady point of reference while the scene below changes.

Visitors can click your name to return to the top, use the section links, or jump to workshops. A keyboard-accessible “Skip to content” link comes first.

On a phone, show your name, the workshop link, and a clearly labelled menu button. The menu opens a simple list of section links.

**2. Opening: “The menu comes last”**

Proposed copy:

> **The menu comes last.**  
> First, the prep list. Then the jars, their dates, their checks. Then what’s ready.  
>   
> I’m Ines Okafor, head chef at Saltmarsh in Leeds. I cook, preserve, and teach.

Below it, three direct routes:

- **Ask about an apprenticeship**
- **See workshops**
- **For food writers**

The visual is a close, carefully composed fragment of your jar wall. It should use a few convincing jars, recognisable contents, actual label styles, and the proportions of your real shelf. Soft side lighting catches the glass; the background stays quiet.

**3D:** The scene begins still. A small pointer movement gently changes the viewing angle on desktop. As the visitor scrolls, the camera moves toward one selected jar while the introduction leaves the screen naturally. Nothing requires dragging or interacting with the jars.

**Clicks:** The three audience links jump to their relevant sections. A small “Explore the kitchen” link moves to the prep list.

**Phone:** Introductory text and links come first, followed by a close crop of the jars. Use a shorter camera movement driven only by scrolling.

**Without 3D:** A strong photograph or pre-rendered still occupies the same space. The text and routes work identically.

**3. The prep list: where the week begins**

This section introduces the kitchen’s working order through an approved excerpt of a real prep list.

An oversized title—**“First, the work.”**—sits beside the list. Three selected tasks connect to photographs of ingredients or preparation. Short text explains why these tasks matter to the eventual cooking.

Use real tasks once chosen. Don’t invent ingredients or manufacture a handwritten list for atmosphere.

**3D:** The jar scene settles into the background and leaves the frame as the prep list takes over. There is no new 3D object. Movement now comes from a selected list entry being highlighted as its corresponding photograph appears.

**Clicks:** Three clearly labelled task buttons let visitors choose the accompanying story. One is open by default, and scrolling reveals the main sequence without requiring clicks.

**Phone:** Each selected task appears with its photograph and explanation underneath. Avoid pinning a tall list beside a small image.

**Without 3D:** The transition is an ordinary change of section. The task stories remain intact.

Public copies of these records must exclude supplier prices and private information before they become website assets.

**4. The ferment log: making time visible**

The next title is **“Then, the checks.”**

One real jar becomes the central case study. Show its start date, recorded salt information, scheduled checks, observations, and readiness. Use the terminology and measurements from the actual record.

The visitor should understand that preserving involves attention and repeated decisions. A compact introduction establishes that the larder has **40+ ferments, vinegars, and garums in rotation**.

Include the koji cupboard as a small photograph and caption within this section. Describe it as a cupboard.

**3D:** The selected jar returns in a close view. It rotates slightly to bring its label into sight, then stays steady while log entries appear alongside it. The movement does not simulate fermentation or invent changes in the contents.

Only use changing contents or photographs if you have genuine records of those stages. Otherwise, the log carries the passage of time.

**Clicks:** Visitors can select recorded check dates to read observations. An optional “See another jar” control opens up to two additional documented examples. The main example is already readable without interaction.

**Phone:** Use a jar photograph followed by a vertical sequence of checks. Date buttons can jump between entries, but every entry remains available in the page.

**Without 3D:** A labelled jar photograph replaces the model. All dates, observations, and explanations remain accessible.

This is a curated historical example, not a live feed from the kitchen.

**5. What’s ready: cooking and finished dishes**

The log’s readiness entry leads to the title **“Then, the plate.”**

Present three substantial ingredient-to-dish stories. Each contains:

- The ingredient or starting material.
- The preparation or preservation involved.
- How you decided it was ready.
- Its role in the finished dish.
- A finished-food photograph and concise caption.

Use Priya’s photography for the finished plates once restaurant permission is confirmed, with her credit. Your phone photographs can show the preparation.

These examples represent your practice over time. They do not pretend to be this week’s menu.

**3D:** The jar turns slightly and settles near the edge of the composition. The first finished-dish photograph takes over the main visual area. The 3D scene ends here; later sections get their character from photographs, type, and paper.

**Clicks:** Each story offers “Read the process,” expanding further detail inline. Photographs can open in an accessible image viewer, with captions and credits.

**Phone:** Stack each story as a complete unit. Keep the relevant process text beside its photograph in the reading order.

**Without 3D:** Use a direct photographic transition from jar to dish.

Follow the dishes with a short evidence block:

> We reduced the whole kitchen’s weekly prep and plate waste from 14 kg to 5 kg, measured over one month in 2023. Bones were excluded because they went to stock.

Present this as a clearly contextualised observation. Don’t animate the numbers or extrapolate them into annual savings.

**6. Learning: apprenticeships and workshops**

The title is **“Pass it on.”**

This is a major destination, with two distinct blocks rather than a general paragraph about teaching.

The **apprenticeship block** explains what someone learns, how you supervise, and what working in the kitchen involves. State that you have taken two apprentices a year since 2023. Include staff meal where it helps explain the learning environment.

Before publication, confirm current availability, pay, hours, eligibility, application arrangements, and what information an enquiry should contain.

The **workshop block** explains the format and subject, the usual first-Monday rhythm, and confirmed upcoming dates. Each bookable workshop needs its location, duration, price, accessibility information, what is included, and any experience requirements.

**3D:** None. This section needs immediate clarity. A restrained reveal of photographs or text is enough.

**Clicks:** “Ask about an apprenticeship” opens a prepared email or a short enquiry form. Each confirmed workshop has “View details” and a booking link. If dates are unavailable, show an honest availability message and a contact route.

**Phone:** Apprenticeships and workshops stack vertically, each with its own action. Booking details remain easy to scan.

**Without 3D:** No change.

Photographs may show hands, tools, ingredients, and spaces. Identifiable staff appear only after you have asked them.

**7. Brine: preserving knowledge**

Introduce Brine as your zine about preserving, with four issues published between 2022 and 2025.

Give each issue its date, a concise description, and a selected readable excerpt. Use the actual issue titles when supplied.

The composition changes from kitchen records to an editorial spread: warm paper, generous margins, and a clear reading hierarchy.

**3D:** None. A brief, flat opening movement can reveal a spread, then stop. Avoid a simulated book that visitors must manipulate to read.

**Clicks:** Each issue offers “Read an excerpt.” Offer “Download issue” only where distribution permission and a suitable file are confirmed. Larger page previews can open in an accessible viewer.

**Phone:** Issues appear one at a time. Text excerpts remain readable HTML; scans are optional enlargements.

**Without 3D:** No change.

Olu’s drawings remain out until permission is confirmed. The layout must work with text and other approved material while that decision is pending. Don’t reproduce scans containing his artwork before permission.

**8. About: where this practice comes from**

Use your photograph of your grandmother’s ogiri pot as the anchor.

A short personal account connects what she taught you with your present work. Her approach to measuring, and your encounters with Sandor Katz’s books, belong here in your own words. Avoid making assumptions about family history or cultural meaning beyond what you choose to share.

Follow with a compact career record, not a large CV:

- **Head Chef, Saltmarsh — September 2022–present.**
- Acting head chef from March to September 2022, where the longer biography needs that distinction.
- Earlier kitchen roles in Leeds and Manchester.
- **Fjaerland Fermentation Lab — ten-week unpaid stage, 2018:** washed and labelled jars and helped with two batches of garum.

Give Sunday Pot a distinct paragraph: co-founded in 2021, providing a hot meal every Sunday at St Aidan’s from restaurant surplus.

**3D:** None. The pot appears as a photograph, without speculative reconstruction or decorative rotation.

**Clicks:** Visitors can expand the fuller career record. Add a Sunday Pot link only if an appropriate public destination exists.

**Phone and without 3D:** A straightforward sequence of photograph, personal account, career details, and Sunday Pot.

**9. Food writers and contact**

This section provides a practical destination for writers while keeping the other routes visible.

Include a short approved biography, selected facts, interview contact, and press images only where usage is authorised. Every downloadable image carries its credit and usage information.

Place Callum’s approved quotation here:

> “Ines turned our bins into a larder. The best thing on the menu most weeks started as something we used to throw away.”

**3D:** None. A small still detail from the jar wall can quietly connect this ending to the opening.

**Clicks:** “Email Ines,” “Read biography,” approved press downloads, and links back to workshops and apprenticeship enquiries. Restaurant enquiries go to Saltmarsh.

**Phone and without 3D:** The same content, with large, clearly labelled links.

The biography and downloads must preserve the correct waste context, dates, and description of the stage.

**10. Footer**

Include your name, Leeds location, contact link, relevant social links, Saltmarsh link, photography and illustration credits, privacy information, and a motion setting.

**3D:** None.

**Clicks:** All practical links, “Back to top,” and “Reduce motion.” Respect the visitor’s device preference automatically.

**The look**

The site should feel carefully composed and worked in: shelves, labels, records, glass, and paper. Use the original material for its character.

| Element | Direction |
|---|---|
| Background | Warm paper, `#F3EBDD` |
| Main text | Dark ink, `#23251F` |
| Dark sections | Bottle green, `#35483D` |
| Small accents | Ferment amber, `#A65B27` |
| Light text | Chalk, `#FFFDF7` |
| Headlines | Newsreader |
| Body and controls | IBM Plex Sans |
| Dates and log annotations | IBM Plex Mono, used sparingly |

Check contrast for each actual colour pairing; amber should mainly serve as an accent.

Use large headlines, spacious photography, and small precise annotations. Your phone photographs retain their working-kitchen quality. Priya’s images receive consistent treatment. Olu’s drawings retain their own identity and credit if approved.

Avoid decorative handwriting, invented stains, custom cursors, sound effects, and motion that competes with reading.

**How the experience behaves**

Scrolling stays native. No compulsory introduction, forced scroll speed, or drag-to-enter interaction.

The 3D occupies only the opening through the cooking sequence. Text is ordinary accessible page content, independent of the scene. A complete still image appears first; the model loads afterwards.

On phones, shorten or remove pinned sequences and give each story a natural vertical reading order. On slower devices or when 3D fails, keep the still version automatically. Reduced-motion mode removes camera travel and animated transitions while preserving the whole story.

Links should support direct entry to apprenticeships, workshops, and writer information. Someone arriving from a workshop announcement should not have to traverse the visual introduction.

**Everything else the site needs**

- **Content:** Three documented cooking stories; one complete log example; optional additional jars; approved prep-list excerpts; apprenticeship information; workshop details; Brine descriptions and excerpts; your personal account; approved biography; contact details.
- **Assets:** Selected dish and jar-wall photographs, phone photographs, the pot photograph, relevant scans, and reference images for the 3D shelf and jars.
- **Permissions and credits:** Restaurant approval for its photographs, Priya’s credit, Olu’s permission before artwork use, and your approval process for identifiable staff.
- **Public document copies:** Prices and private details removed from all records, scans, photographs, and downloads before publication.
- **Workshop booking:** Use the existing booking service if available. Otherwise choose a booking route covering capacity, payment, confirmation, cancellations, and accessibility enquiries.
- **Apprenticeship enquiries:** A working contact route, clear availability, and confirmed application information.
- **Editing:** A simple way to update workshops, apprenticeship availability, publications, and selected work without changing the visual system.
- **Accessibility:** Keyboard navigation, visible focus, semantic headings, descriptive image text, readable records, accessible image viewers, reduced motion, and sufficient contrast.
- **Performance:** Compressed responsive photographs, lightweight 3D assets, deferred loading, and testing on an ordinary phone and a slower connection.
- **Search and sharing:** Accurate page titles and descriptions, social preview images, indexing information, and stable links to key sections.
- **Contact delivery:** Tested email or form delivery, appropriate spam protection if a form is used, and clear confirmation after submission.
- **Privacy:** Information matching the actual booking, contact, and analytics tools. My default recommendation is no analytics at launch unless you have a specific question to measure.
- **Launch checks:** Confirm facts, permissions, credits, links, booking behaviour, mobile layouts, keyboard use, motion preferences, and the complete experience with 3D unavailable.
- **Maintenance:** Domain and hosting ownership, HTTPS, backups, and a named person responsible for dates and enquiries.

**Assumptions**

This remains your personal portfolio, with restaurant bookings handled by Saltmarsh. The site uses selected historical kitchen records. Workshop dates are published only when confirmed. Apprenticeship enquiries can remain open between intakes if availability is clearly stated. No job-seeking language is included.

The distinctive experience comes from letting visitors follow your actual working order. They encounter the jars as objects, understand them through records, see their place in the cooking, and find a clear way to learn from you.
