## The experience as a whole

One long page, with six chapters. On desktop, ordinary text scrolls beside one persistent 3D scene. The same six jars remain recognisable throughout; their arrangements and readable labels explain each chapter.

Six jars are a visual composition, not a claim about your inventory. They represent the chapters, rather than pretending to document six particular recipes. Real ingredients, batch dates, salt ratios and checking records appear only when you supply approved examples.

The scene begins and ends as a complete larder. Between those states, it becomes an inspection row, a close-up of a log label, and a shelf supporting the evidence of your work.

The desktop journey is roughly eight viewport heights. Scrolling remains native: no forced pauses, wheel interception or requirement to complete an animation before reading.

## The look

The selected direction is **warm paper and olive**.

| Element | Direction |
|---|---|
| Background | Warm paper — `#F2EBDD` |
| Text | Dark ink — `#252820` |
| Interface accent | Deep olive — `#52613D` |
| Jars | Clear, lightly tinted glass |
| Labels | Pale paper with readable dark text |
| Lids | Restrained dark or muted metal surfaces |
| Lighting | Soft directional light and modest shadows |

The materials come from the larder and its records. Glass provides depth, but labels remain the clearest part of every jar.

Use **Source Sans 3** for page text and headings, and **IBM Plex Mono** for log fields, dates and labels. Both should be self-hosted with a limited set of weights.

Photographed handwriting can remain visible, accompanied by legible transcription. Avoid a handwriting font pretending to be yours, distressed textures behind text, or small decorative labels that cannot be read.

Desktop text has a comfortable reading width and generous spacing. Accent colour identifies links and active controls, with underlines or other cues so colour is never the sole signal.

## Navigation and interaction

The page has a discreet chapter rail on desktop and a simple **Chapters** disclosure on phones. Both use normal section anchors.

A persistent contact link lets visitors bypass the story. Direct links to chapters land at the relevant content without replaying earlier transitions.

Scrolling backwards reverses the arrangement of the scene; it does not pretend to reverse a fermentation process.

There is no autoplay sound, cursor replacement, hidden drag interaction or loading sequence that withholds the page.

Photo detail views need keyboard operation, a visible close button, and correct focus handling.

## On a phone

The phone version is a normal vertical document.

It opens with a static shelf composition, then uses smaller static arrangements of the same jars beside the chapter content. Labels and photographs provide detail without competing with the reading space.

There is no pinned full-screen canvas, horizontal scrolling or requirement to swipe through a 3D object.

The phone version preserves:

- Every chapter and factual explanation.
- The waste result and its scope.
- Callum’s quotation.
- Photograph enlargement.
- All three contact routes.
- Chapter navigation.

Controls have at least 44-pixel touch targets. Images reserve their space to prevent layout jumps. Labels remain readable without zooming the whole page.

## Without motion or 3D

| Situation | Experience |
|---|---|
| Reduced motion | Static chapter compositions; no parallax, rotation, scrubbing or animated assembly. Anchor jumps are immediate. |
| WebGL unavailable or lost | Static shelf images or photo-and-label layouts replace the scene. Text and links continue normally. |
| JavaScript disabled | Complete HTML page with every section, image and contact link. Log-field explanations remain visible or use native disclosures. |
| Slow connection | Text and essential images arrive first. The desktop 3D enhancement loads later. |
| Keyboard or screen reader | Logical headings, visible focus, skip link and ordinary controls. Canvas labels never hold the only copy of information. |

The substitute layouts should be designed alongside the desktop scene, so the page remains visually intentional in every mode.
