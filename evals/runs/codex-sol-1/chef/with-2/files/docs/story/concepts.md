# Three concepts

## Selection

Chosen by the assistant under user delegation A11, then explicitly accepted by Ines (“Go with the direction you recommend”): **The Ferment Log**. It uses the user's named daily document, has a real progression from start to ready, and gives the 3D a job: carrying visible records of preparation, checking and use. The menu arrives last, grounded in A10. This is a design interpretation of a log, not a claim about how Ines's actual log is formatted.

Trade forms considered: prep list, ferment log, recipe sheet, weekly menu, ordering sheet, service handover. Only prep list and ferment log are confirmed as the documents Ines runs her kitchen on. Menu is confirmed as written last. Other forms are trade possibilities, not personal facts.

## 1. The Ferment Log — selected

- **Question:** What happens before the menu is written?
- **Form:** Ferment log. Six proposed chapters in operational order: Started (opening/who/where from), Salt (how), Checks (work shown), Ready (what came of it), Use (public work/testimony), Next batch (close). These are proposed editorial field names, not a transcription of an unseen document.
- **Object:** One small larder of six glass jars with large paper log bands. Simple cylinders, lids and flat label surfaces. Labels are the physical connection to the document, supported by the user's labelled larder photographs. Six is a compositional choice, not a claim about larder size. They remain visible throughout the desktop journey.
- **Transformations:** Started: six jars grouped on a shallow shelf, beside the photograph of the ogiri pot and a source-based account of influences. Checks: six jars become a single inspection row, one log band facing forward, with the fields started/salt/checks/ready. Ready: jars align beside a clearly labelled kitchen-wide waste measurement, with the jar bands carrying context rather than encoding a false statistical chart. Use: the same six jars form a shelf beside Brine/workshops/apprentices; a plain menu sheet appears only after these working stages. Close: jars return to a complete larder, labels readable, alongside three contact routes.
- **Opening/close:** Name, role, Leeds, log question, cleared testimonial and contact skip on opening. Ending answers the question through the visible sequence—prep list and ferment log before menu—and offers apprentice, writer and workshop contact.
- **Sources:** F02–F05, F16–F19, F21/F23; A01–A03, A06–A10. F08 is used only with A02's exact scope.
- **Needs:** Owned pot/larder photos and approved excerpts of real labels/log entries. Restaurant photos and Olu's art are optional, not required. No invented jar ingredients, dates, salt ratios or time-lapse of a fictional batch.
- **Why theirs:** The log fields, menu written last, labelled larder, unpaid jar-washing stage, grandmother's pot, and Brine together root it in Ines's supplied material.

## 2. The Prep List

- **Question:** What work makes it onto the menu?
- **Form:** Prep list. Six editorial chapters: Before service (opening/who), Gather (where from), Prepare (how), Check (work), Finish (outcomes/testimony), Handover (close). Names need not imitate the actual unseen list.
- **Object:** Six long paper slips, a designed exhibition version of a prep list, each holding a sourced piece of work. They stay together on screen and at rest read as a clipped kitchen list. No fabricated completed task or actual kitchen instruction is printed.
- **Transformations:** Gather: slips fan into source photographs and influences. Prepare: slips align in a work sequence with prep/log fields. Finish: slips become one completed editorial list, with the team waste result in its own context and a menu sheet arriving after preparation.
- **Opening/close:** A list headed with Ines's identity, the question and proof, then a finished list with the three contact choices.
- **Sources:** F04/F05/F16/F18/F19/F21/F23; A02/A03/A06–A10.
- **Needs:** Owned photos and an approved, price-free prep-list extract; otherwise clearly designed, factual editorial slips. No restaurant image dependency.
- **Why theirs:** It centres her stated working order and the prep list that is longer than the menu, rather than presenting courses as the structure.
- **Tradeoff:** Particularly clear and fast on phones, but the papers make the physical larder less immediate. Rejected in favour of the log's more recognisable object and stronger start-to-ready progression.

## 3. The Label Index

- **Question:** What does a jar's label tell you about the work behind it?
- **Form:** Ferment log, presented as a six-entry inspection index: Started, Salt, Check dates, Readiness, Use, Record the next batch. Roles: opening/who, how, work, outcomes, publication/testimony, close.
- **Object:** Six thick, gently curled paper label tiles, enlarged so actual handwritten marks can be read. At rest they form an index board; always six pieces, with no jar models. This is a display treatment, not an assertion that Ines keeps an index board.
- **Transformations:** Started: labels line up beside the pot photo and influences. Check dates: labels form a calendar-like sequence only when actual approved dates exist; otherwise a diagram of field order with no numerical dates. Use: labels turn into an index of the log, apprentice programme, Brine and workshop information; close returns to the complete board.
- **Opening/close:** Identity/question/contact skip beside one readable label; final complete index routes visitors to the three kinds of inquiry.
- **Sources:** F04/F05/F16/F18/F19/F21/F23; A02/A03/A06–A10.
- **Needs:** Owned, legible close-ups of labels and price-free fields, or newly typeset factual field headings. No artwork or third-party photos needed.
- **Why theirs:** Her log's specific fields and larder labels become the entire visual vocabulary.
- **Tradeoff:** Best detail-reading experience and cheapest rendering, but less physical presence than a shelf of jars. Rejected because the jars provide context without requiring a visitor to decipher an abstract index.

## Checks on all three

- No kitchen emblems, generic particle field or unrelated effects.
- Piece transformations express work order, record structure or grouping; no looping decoration.
- Each starts with identity, work, proof and contact and resolves its opening question at the end.
- Claims trace to the fact sheet; source photos do not silently create permission or batch facts.
- Proposed document chapter names and layouts are openly editorial, not presented as original kitchen documents.
