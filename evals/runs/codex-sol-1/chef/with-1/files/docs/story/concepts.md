## 3. The Larder

- **Question:** What does a jar hold besides its ingredients?
- **Form:** Ferment log, same supplied field order, with five chapters: Started (opening/who/influences), Salt (how), Checked (stage/checking/outcomes), Ready (work/teaching/quote), Start again (close). Final chapter is editorial, not a claimed practice.
- **Object:** Eight simple glass jars with paper labels, an editorial subset of the actual jar wall, not a claim about a specific eight-jar collection. Owned photographs and confirmed words appear on readable label panels.
- **Three transformations:** Started brings jars into a row around the pot photograph; Checked moves labels forward into a paired waste record; Ready aligns jars as a shelf of larder, Brine, apprentice and workshop entries. Contents never simulate real fermentation or a recipe.
- **Opening:** Real jar-wall phone photograph introduces the object; no generic laboratory or chef iconography.
- **Close:** All jars return to one shelf with three contact choices.
- **Stands on:** F01, F02, F06–F10, F12 and source jar-wall descriptions.
- **Needs:** Owned larder photographs and contact destinations; same optional artwork permissions as above.
- **Why hers:** The jar wall and practical cupboard-sized koji room belong to her stated work, and jar labels can reveal the logging she explicitly described.
- **Tradeoff:** Recognisable but translucent glass adds rendering cost and labels can become hard to read on phones; using the jar merely as an emblem would weaken the story.

## Review

All three have one recurring object, a beginning/process/result/close and visible HTML contact shortcuts. Work evidence lives on the pieces, not in an unrelated scene. Each uses confirmed personal material; unsupported claims are excluded. The selected log is strongest because the object can expose actual records clearly without relying on glass effects or unconfirmed prep-list headings.
