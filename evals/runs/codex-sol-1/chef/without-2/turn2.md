**The revised journey**

1. **An opening that states your approach.**  
   “Ines Okafor — Head Chef, Saltmarsh, Leeds” sits beside a 3D jar based on one of your real preserves.

   Proposed opening copy:  
   **“The menu is the last thing we write.”**  
   “First come the prep list, the jars, and the work of checking what’s ready.”

   Scrolling reveals the jar’s label and moves into the working larder. Navigation offers direct routes to **The kitchen, Apprentices, Workshops, and Brine**, with contact always accessible.

2. **The prep list: where the week begins.**  
   Use an edited, publishable extract from a real prep list alongside your phone photographs. Short annotations explain how you decide what needs using, preserving, or preparing.

   The composition can reveal tasks progressively as someone scrolls, but the text remains readable without animation. No supplier prices appear. Any operational documents used here are deliberately selected for publication.

3. **The ferment log: the central interactive piece.**  
   The opening jar becomes a way into the log. Visitors can follow a few selected jars through the fields you actually use: start date, salt, scheduled checks, and readiness.

   A wider photograph then reveals the jar wall and the scale of the larder. The koji cupboard belongs here too, described plainly.

4. **What becomes dinner.**  
   Only now do finished dishes appear. Two or three documented examples connect prep and preservation to something served at Saltmarsh. The final beat is the weekly menu: the result of the preceding work.
