The direction is **“Before the menu”**: a portfolio that follows the real order of your kitchen’s work. Visitors move from the prep list to the ferment log, through checking and readiness, into dishes, then meet the teaching, workshops, and writing that grow from that practice.

The central visual is one real jar, recreated in 3D and connected to its actual record. Its purpose is to draw people into your decisions. Your photographs, documents, and words carry the rest of the experience.

This is the complete proposed plan. Nothing will be built at this stage.

**The page, in order**

1. **Opening — “The menu is the last thing we write.”**

   The opening pairs your name and role with a large preserving jar on a warm paper background.

   Proposed copy:

   > **Ines Okafor**  
   > Head Chef at Saltmarsh, Leeds  
   >  
   > **The menu is the last thing we write.**  
   > First come the prep list, the jars, and the work of checking what’s ready.

   The jar should be selected from your actual larder: something with a useful log, photographs, and a clear connection to a finished dish.

   **3D:** The jar appears at a slight angle, with convincing glass, contents, and its real label. A small, bounded response to pointer movement makes it feel physical. On scroll, it turns enough to bring the label into view. Essential text appears immediately; loading the model never delays the introduction.

   **Visitors can click:** “Follow a jar” to enter the narrative, plus direct links to apprentices and workshops. A compact navigation provides Kitchen, Apprentices, Workshops, Brine, and Contact. Your name returns to the top.

   **Phone:** Text comes first, followed by a smaller jar. No pointer-dependent interaction. The apprentice and workshop links remain visible near the opening.

   **Without 3D:** A carefully lit photograph of the same jar occupies the composition. The label and introductory copy provide the same information.

2. **The prep list — where the week begins**

   An edited extract from a real prep list introduces the kitchen’s priorities. Pair it with your phone photographs of ingredients, labels, and the larder.

   Three or four annotations explain actual choices: what needs attention, what can be preserved, what is ready to use. These must come from your account of a real working week.

   **3D:** The opening jar moves into a smaller position beside the prep list, connecting the object to a task. It then settles; the document gets the attention.

   **Visitors can click:** Selected annotations to reveal short explanations, and “Read the prep list” to open an accessible transcription alongside the document image. Expanded content also needs a direct link so someone can share it.

   **Phone:** The document and annotations appear in reading order. Each explanation opens beneath its trigger.

   **Without 3D:** A jar thumbnail provides the connection. The prep list and explanations work independently.

   Only a selected public extract appears. Supplier prices and any other private operational information are removed before assets reach the site.

3. **The ferment log — the centre of the experience**

   Follow the chosen jar through its actual record:

   - Date started.
   - Salt information as recorded.
   - Scheduled checks.
   - Observations, where available.
   - When it was ready.

   Your captions explain the judgment behind the entries. If the record has gaps, the presentation reflects them.

   **3D:** A short desktop sequence keeps the jar in view while dated log entries advance beside it. The camera changes between a full view and a label detail. The jar’s appearance changes only where photographs or records support a visible change; time is expressed chiefly through the log.

   Scrolling controls the sequence naturally. There is no locked scroll or requirement to finish an animation.

   **Visitors can click:** Each dated entry to inspect its note and photograph; previous and next controls to move through entries; “Read the full record” for a linear, accessible version.

   **Phone:** A vertical timeline presents the entries with photographs. A small jar image establishes continuity, without occupying most of the screen.

   **Without 3D:** The same record uses still photographs. All dates, observations, controls, and links remain available.

4. **The larder — one jar among many**

   Pull back from the individual story to Priya’s jar-wall photography. This section shows the broader system: more than 40 ferments, vinegars, and garums on rotation, and the koji cupboard.

   Feature a small selection of additional jars with concise stories. Their names and descriptions come from your records.

   **3D:** The camera briefly pulls back from the featured jar, then the scene gives way to the real jar-wall photograph. The wall itself is photographic; its detail and irregularity are part of its character.

   **Visitors can click:** Clearly marked jars in the photograph, with the same selections also available as ordinary text links. Each opens a story containing its photograph, ingredients, process, and use. The koji cupboard has its own small entry.

   **Phone:** The full wall image establishes the space, followed by a readable list of featured jars. Small hotspots are optional shortcuts.

   **Without 3D:** The section begins directly with the wall photograph. No content changes.

5. **From prep to plate — the menu arrives**

   Show two or three real examples connecting an ingredient or preparation task to a finished dish. At least one completes the featured jar’s story.

   Each example explains:

   - What you started with.
   - What the kitchen did.
   - Why you made that decision.
   - How it was served.

   Finish with a dated menu extract or a short explanation of how that week’s menu came together.

   **3D:** The featured jar makes its final appearance next to the relevant dish, then leaves the page. Photographs carry the other transformations. Scrolling can briefly hold a caption while the corresponding image changes.

   **Visitors can click:** Each example to read its fuller account and enlarge photographs. A separate Saltmarsh link handles restaurant information and dining enquiries.

   **Phone:** Each story becomes a sequence of image-and-caption pairs. Visitors can read it without swiping or waiting.

   **Without 3D:** A still jar image completes the same connection to the dish.

   The waste result belongs here, with the agreed scope:

   > “Over one month in 2023, we weighed the kitchen’s prep and plate waste. Weekly waste fell from 14 kg to 5 kg. Bones weren’t included; they went to stock.”

   Place Callum’s approved quote beside it:

   > “Ines turned our bins into a larder. The best thing on the menu most weeks started as something we used to throw away.”  
   > — Callum Reyes, owner of Saltmarsh

6. **Apprentices — learning in this kitchen**

   This is a substantial destination for prospective applicants. Explain what you teach, how learning happens during service and preparation, and what someone entering the kitchen should expect.

   Include the programme’s history: two apprentices a year since 2023. Staff meal can illustrate the working culture through a specific account, once supplied.

   **3D:** None. The page becomes calmer and more direct. Documentary photographs, text, and a sample task provide the detail.

   **Visitors can click:** “What to expect,” a short FAQ, and the appropriate application or enquiry action. The section states the current intake status explicitly. It must not imply vacancies unless they exist.

   **Phone and without 3D:** The same straightforward layout, with practical information before supporting photography.

   Images may show equipment, food, documents, or consenting participants. Every selected staff photograph still receives a permission check, even when faces are absent.

7. **Workshops — learn with you**

   A practical section supports booking while conveying your teaching style. Use a real workshop example and a photograph of its materials or activity.

   Every bookable workshop needs its topic, intended audience, confirmed date, venue, duration, price, availability, access information, and what participants should bring or expect.

   **3D:** None. A restrained photographic transition introduces the section.

   **Visitors can click:** A confirmed session to view full details and book through the chosen booking service. When no session is available, show an honest status and a workshop enquiry route.

   **Phone and without 3D:** Dates, key details, and the booking action appear early. Any external booking destination is identified clearly.

   The first-Monday pattern can be mentioned once confirmed as current. It does not automatically generate future bookable dates.

8. **Brine — preserving in print**

   Present the four issues, dated 2022–2025, with a short introduction to why you make the zine. Each issue receives a summary and a clear access status.

   **3D:** None. If Olu agrees to the relevant use, scanned pages can have a small paper-lift transition. Ordinary reading controls remain primary.

   **Visitors can click:** An issue to see its description, readable sample pages, and the confirmed way to read, download, buy, or enquire about it.

   **Phone:** One issue per row. Reading opens at a useful width with page controls and zoom where needed.

   **Without 3D or motion:** Static covers and pages provide the entire experience.

   Olu’s illustrations and any scans containing them remain unpublished until his permission is secured. The initial design can use a typographic issue list.

9. **What brought you here — and where the work goes**

   Your photograph of your grandmother’s ogiri pot anchors a short personal passage in your voice. Connect it to her way of cooking without measurements and the influence of Sandor Katz’s books.

   Include a concise career account and Sunday Pot: the weekly meal at St Aidan’s hall, made from restaurants’ surplus.

   **3D:** None. The pot is presented through your photograph, with space and a carefully written caption.

   **Visitors can click:** A longer biography and a Sunday Pot destination, if an appropriate public link exists.

   **Phone and without 3D:** A simple sequence of photograph, text, and links.

   The biography uses **Head Chef, September 2022–present**. It may separately mention acting head chef from March 2022.

   Fjaerland is described precisely:

   > “A ten-week unpaid stage at Fjaerland Fermentation Lab in 2018, washing and labelling jars and helping with two batches of garum.”

10. **Contact — three clear routes**

    End with apprentice enquiries, workshop bookings or enquiries, and press enquiries. Each route has a short explanation and the relevant destination.

    Food writers can access a factual biography, approved facts, and downloadable press photographs with their captions and credits.

    **3D:** None. A quiet detail from a real label or the jar wall closes the visual story.

    **Visitors can click:** The three contact routes, press resources, Saltmarsh, and any confirmed public profiles. The footer contains image credits, privacy information, and an accessibility contact.

    **Phone and without 3D:** The routes stack vertically with large tap targets.

**The look**

The site should feel like a working larder documented with editorial care. Use generous space for dishes, denser compositions for records, and quieter layouts for teaching and booking.

| Element | Direction |
|---|---|
| Background | Warm paper, `#F3EBDD` |
| Main text | Dark ink, `#242820` |
| Large supporting panels | Larder green, `#465641` |
| Links and emphasis | Ferment rust, `#A44832` |
| Decorative detail | Brine gold, `#C5A35A` |
| Headlines and personal passages | Newsreader |
| Navigation, records, captions, forms | Inter |

Final colours should be checked against the actual photographs and adjusted for accessible contrast. Gold is decorative.

Use your real handwriting as photographed detail, with typed equivalents for anything visitors need to read. Document dates and image credits should be legible. Avoid simulated stains or distressed textures; the source material supplies the texture.

**Movement, navigation, and performance**

The main 3D sequence spans the opening, prep list, and ferment log. It concludes at the dish. Beyond that, small image transitions are enough.

Desktop compositions may briefly hold an image beside changing text. Phone layouts use normal vertical reading. The page keeps ordinary browser scrolling, supports section links, and preserves navigation history when visitors open deeper stories.

Three versions share the same content:

- **Full experience:** 3D and restrained scroll transitions on capable devices.
- **Light experience:** Still images and simpler transitions when rendering is unsuitable.
- **Reduced motion:** Static compositions and immediate content changes.

The introduction and navigation load first. The model loads afterward; lower-page images load as needed. A visible fallback handles loading failure. Test the experience on a representative older phone and a slower connection, as well as a desktop.
