# Three concepts — Before the menu selected

All are proposed presentation devices, not claims that Ines's documents physically look this way. No website has been built. Source IDs refer to facts.md; user confirmations supersede earlier source ambiguity.

## Forms considered

The trade has prep lists, ferment logs, menus, recipe cards, ordering sheets and service handovers. Only the prep list and ferment log are confirmed as Ines's working forms (F19). Menus cannot lead this story: Ines writes them last. Ordering sheets would also risk foregrounding restricted supplier information.

## 1. Before the menu — recommended

**Question:** What happens before the menu is written?

**Form:** The ferment log, presented through six jars and their attached log labels. Chapters follow its confirmed field order, with contextual opening and closing:

1. The log — opening and who: Ines, Saltmarsh, fermentation and apprentices.
2. Started — where from: grandmother's pot as a named influence; concise career dates and the ten-week stage, without an invented recipe lineage.
3. Salt — how: introduce the log's salt field and prep list; actual values only if provided. Do not imply salt is the sole determinant of safety or readiness.
4. Check days — work: the checking practice and apprentice programme, with no fabricated checks or results.
5. Ready — outcomes and testimony: kitchen waste measurement with its scope and “we”; Callum's exact approved quote.
6. The menu, written last — close: answer the opening question with the prep list, ferment log and teaching; workshop booking, apprentice enquiry and writer contact.

**Object:** Six simple jars with large attached labels. They are a curated representation of the jar wall, not six invented ferments. Faces carry source-supported facts, approved imagery or real cleared entries; no invented ingredients, dates or salt percentages.

**Three transformations:** At Started the same jars form a chronological line with career facts on labels. At Check days they align with the log's fields, showing what is recorded rather than simulating a biological process. At Ready their labels become evidence surfaces holding the scoped team result and quote. The jars remain visible throughout and return to a shelf at the close.

**Opening:** A flat label unfolds onto a jar, making the record-keeping origin legible immediately. The remaining jars gather beside it. Ines's own pot photo may appear beside the influence text after asset review; no claim that a particular ferment descends from it.

**Close:** The same shelf settles beside the menu-last statement and contact choices. Workshop booking and apprentice enquiry receive priority; no job-seeking language.

**Stands on:** F01–F05, F07, F09–F11, F14–F19; A02/A04 are optional reviewed assets. No unconfirmed supplier metric or apprentice completion claim.

**Needs:** Public-safe log entry or label photograph; workshop and enquiry destinations. Restaurant images require reuse clearance and Priya credit; unnecessary to the concept. Olu's drawings excluded pending permission.

**Why hers:** It joins her actual log fields, jar wall, kitchen-team measurement and insistence that the menu is written last.

## 2. The prep comes first

**Question:** What work belongs on the list before a dish reaches the menu?

**Form:** A prep list, arranged into six proposed groups. Group titles must be checked against Ines's actual list before storyboard copy is treated as her terminology.

1. Today's list — opening/who: name, role and question.
2. Larder — how: fermentation and the jar wall.
3. Ferment checks — how/work: the confirmed log fields alongside the prep list.
4. Apprentice work — work: Ines runs the programme; no assumed tasks or completion counts.
5. Review — outcomes/testimony: scoped waste result and Callum's quote; this is a proposed editorial grouping, not a claimed kitchen ritual.
6. Menu — close: written last, with workshop and apprentice contacts.

**Object:** Six paper slips assembled into one prep list. These are designed chapter slips, not reproductions of a purported real list. Actual cleared prep entries can replace generic field labels later.

**Three transformations:** At Larder the slips stand as a labelled inventory with approved larder images. At Ferment checks they align into a table of the four real fields. At Menu they stack into a completed list, with the menu statement visible only after the preparation chapters. No automatic tick marks imply real jobs completed.

**Opening:** Loose slips gather into a readable list headed with Ines's name; origin is her confirmed everyday working document.

**Close:** The list is assembled; the menu comes last. The visitor can book a workshop or ask about apprenticeships.

**Stands on:** F01–F05, F07, F14, F16–F19; A02 optional. Career can stay as a concise linked text detail, rather than forcing chronology into a task list.

**Needs:** A redacted prep list to verify task vocabulary and ordering, plus contact destinations. No supplier prices, staff faces or unapproved artwork.

**Why hers:** The story follows her explicit reversal of the usual restaurant presentation: preparation first, menu last.

## 3. Read the ferment log

**Question:** What do you record while a ferment develops?

**Form:** The ferment log as a teaching document: six leaves in a designed bound ledger, not a claim about the physical format of her actual log.

1. Open the log — opening/who: Ines, her work and question.
2. Start date — background: career dates and the accurately described stage; dates on career leaves are distinct from ferment start dates.
3. Salt — how: the actual recorded field, without invented units or a recipe.
4. Check days — work: log structure, apprentices and workshops. A cleared example could show actual entries, but no process timings are fabricated.
5. Ready — testimony: Callum's quote and Ines's wider work in preserving, including a text-only mention of Brine.
6. Pass it on — close: answer with the four confirmed log fields; choose a workshop or apprentice enquiry.

**Object:** Six thick paper leaves bound into a ledger. Unlike option 2's task slips, each leaf teaches one field and the leaves continuously open and close as a book.

**Three transformations:** At Start date leaves spread into dated career markers. At Check days they lie open as a legible field-by-field record, keeping example data absent until supplied. At Pass it on they rebind into the closed ledger beside the enquiry choices.

**Opening:** The four confirmed field names appear on the ledger as it opens. No claim that grandmother or Katz supplied this specific logging method.

**Close:** The leaves come together as a document someone could learn to read with Ines. Booking/enquiry actions sit beside it, with writer contact secondary.

**Stands on:** F01, F03–F04, F09–F11, F13–F14, F16–F19. No dependence on Brine scans or specific unconfirmed lessons.

**Needs:** Real redacted log material for a worked example; workshop and apprentice contact destinations. Typography can carry the concept without third-party imagery.

**Why hers:** It presents her exact recording fields through the apprentice and workshop audiences she named, rather than pitching her for employment.

## Checks and recommendation

- Swap: each depends on her menu-last practice or exact log fields and audience, with her actual evidence on the object faces.
- Meaning: the 3D pieces encode chronology, field relationships or preparation order. Remove their arrangement and that visual explanation is lost; accessible text must still preserve all information.
- Depth: each begins from a real working object, develops its use, and returns it whole at the close.
- Emblem: no chef hats, utensils or generic food spectacle.
- Honesty: no simulated real ferment chemistry, invented recipes, unapproved images, personal credit for team results or researcher title.
- Thirty seconds: every opening includes name, current work, a compact scoped proof line and visible contact choices. Navigation allows direct access without completing the scroll.

Choose **1, Before the menu**: the jars offer the strongest depth and movement, while their labels explain the actual work. The prep list is quieter and depends more on a real document; the ledger is best if teaching should dominate the first impression.
