# Three concepts — awaiting selection

Planning only. No site, prototype or assets built. All chapter labels below are proposed editorial labels drawn from confirmed working forms, not claims about exact headings on Ines’s documents.

Trade forms considered: prep list, ferment log, recipe card, ordering sheet, weekly menu, service handover. User confirms working from the first two, with the menu written last. Concepts use those forms, not a tasting-menu structure.

## 1. Before the menu — the ferment log

- Question: What happens before the menu is written?
- Form: ferment log; Entry (name/question), Started (career/context), Salt (working method), Checks (larder practice and apprentice programme), Ready (work and properly attributed kitchen waste result), Notes (Brine and Callum’s quote), Next entry (answer/contact).
- Object: seven thick log leaves, a single bound register at rest. Faces carry confirmed text and approved excerpts/images; no invented recipes, dates or salinity values.
- Transformations: Started unfolds the leaves into the confirmed career sequence, explicitly identifying Norway as a stage; Checks sets them side by side to explain the supplied log fields; Ready exposes a leaf with the scoped “we” waste result and its measurement note. Leaves remain visible throughout and rebind at the close.
- Opening: the register opens to the fields Ines says the kitchen works from; her name, Head Chef at Saltmarsh, question and audience links are immediately readable. No unsupported origin anecdote.
- Close: return to a whole register; answer that prep and checking precede the weekly menu. Offer apprenticeship enquiry, workshop booking/enquiry and writer contact, destinations pending.
- Evidence: confirmed log fields, prep-list/menu order, Head Chef date, stage duties, scoped waste measurement, audience, quote approval; source statements about Brine/apprentices remain provisional for detailed claims.
- Needs: an approved log excerpt or field-only editorial diagram; chosen phone images after content review; contact routes. Priya/Olu assets optional and excluded until permission.
- Why theirs: the page is governed by Ines’s ferment log and explicit rule that the menu is written last.

## 2. The prep list — work before service

- Question: What work comes before service at Saltmarsh?
- Form: prep list; Before service (name/question), Larder (current role and career context), Label (stage duties and log method), Check (larder work and apprentices), Staff meal (staff meal/Sunday Pot only to the extent confirmed), Menu (work/outcome/quote), Handover (answer/contact).
- Object: seven rectangular prep slips, assembled into one clipped list at rest; faces carry real text and approved material.
- Transformations: Label lays slips into the confirmed start/salt/check/ready field sequence; Check fans them into a readable work display; Menu brings them together with the attributed waste finding and Callum’s quote. No simulated tick marks claiming actual task completion.
- Opening: slips gather into the prep list, the daily document Ines named, without invented tasks.
- Close: the slips collect as a complete list; the answer names prep and the ferment log, with the menu last. Three audience-specific contact links remain available throughout.
- Evidence: confirmed prep list and log, source pre-service focus/staff meal, stage duties, audience, scoped waste figure and quote.
- Needs: actual safe prep-list excerpt; clarification of staff meal/Sunday Pot contributions before detailed coverage; approved photos and contact routes. If clarification does not arrive, omit unsupported stories rather than fill them in.
- Why theirs: labelling jars, checking ferments and writing the menu last supply the chapter sequence, rather than generic chef credentials.

## 3. A wall you can read — the ferment log on jars

- Question: What can the jar wall tell you about how Ines works?
- Form: ferment log; Register (name/question), Started (career/context), Salt (method), Checks (work and teaching), Ready (work/outcome), Notes (quote and Brine), Next batch (answer/contact).
- Object: seven simple cylindrical jars with flat readable labels, standing together as a small wall at rest. They represent log fields and evidence, not seven purported recipes or real batches. No invented contents or quantities.
- Transformations: Started arranges the label faces along the confirmed career sequence; Checks groups jars by the supplied log fields; Ready turns the labels toward the visitor as evidence panels, including the scoped team waste measurement. The same jars remain present and regroup into a wall at the close.
- Opening: the labels become readable on the jar wall already described in the source; no claim that this represents its exact physical layout.
- Close: wall reassembled, with an answer centred on dated, salted, checked work before the menu. Audience-specific contacts visible without requiring a full scroll.
- Evidence: source jar wall plus confirmed log fields, role date, stage duties, waste claim, audience and quote.
- Needs: chosen owned phone photographs and approved label content to make it specific; contact routes. Exclude supplier prices, unapproved faces, Priya photographs and Olu drawings pending permission.
- Why theirs: jars carry the actual fields of Ines’s ferment log, connecting the wall visitors ask about to how her kitchen runs.

## Recommendation and selection

Recommend 1, Before the menu. The register gives text, images and career facts usable surfaces in 3D, and its structure comes directly from Ines’s working document. The jar concept has the most immediate visual recognition but needs especially careful label design to keep information legible.

Selection: Concept 1, Before the menu. User: “Go with the one you recommend, and give me the whole plan. Still no building.” Selected because the ferment register carries the process, evidence and career in one persistent object. No opening copy, palette or storyboard approved.
