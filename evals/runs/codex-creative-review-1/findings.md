Offline review on 10 October 2026, following the author's accepted order of
privacy, unsupported premises and creative review. No conversations or
judgements were purchased for this directory. Original scores are preserved.

## What the six STEM plans actually explain

All six selected objects use document surfaces. That resemblance alone does
not show that all six concepts fail: the design deliberately specifies one
object in similar pieces, with a shared scroll grammar. The useful question
is what the pieces let a visitor understand about this person's work.

| Saved skill case in `../codex-stem-1/` | Object | Relationship in the plan | Original creative outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| backend/with-1 | Six event cards | Two explicitly invented requests carry the same key; arrival order and matching keys explain repeat recognition. A separate scoped benchmark uses proportional 200:120 lengths. | Pass |
| ml/with-1 | Six experiment leaves | Evaluation scope, partitions, ownership, different metrics and limits remain connected. | Pass |

## The strongest contrast

The backend storyboard specifies what the visitor compares: a later arrival
has the same arbitrary example key as the first. The illustration is labelled
as invented, and it stops short of inventing the person's actual handling
algorithm or successful test results. Its meaning comes from the comparison,
not from card thickness. Removing the scene requires another way to explain
that relationship, which the accessible version can provide.

The physicist's three alternatives are a protocol, uncertainty budget and
notebook. All have six flat leaves and near-equivalent identity, reference,
document roles, contribution, output records and contact progressions. Their
different names do not establish different story structures. The alternatives
themselves acknowledge that a permitted budget or notebook extract is absent.

The physics fixture gives a residual definition, a qualified tenfold claim,
authorship boundaries and permission to use public figures. It supplies no
actual schematic, procedure or uncertainty components. The plan correctly
avoids inventing those. It could use a more specific public example of the
person's calibration decision, but we cannot conclude from this run that
another generic instruction would supply that missing evidence.

The analyst cases have a related problem: memo leaves become a timeline,
tool grid and project index without explaining how a particular dashboard
decision works. In `../codex-sol-2/analyst-hurried/with-2/`, labels and grouping
even receive a backdrop pass while the swap and depth tests fail. This is a
reason to inspect the underlying relationship rather than treat any one
creative verdict as objective proof.

The hurried analyst deliberately withholds answers about responsibilities,
working artifacts and permissions. Its creative result is constrained by
that missing evidence. A more specific story must not be obtained by
reviving unconfirmed career claims; report factual restraint and creative
limitations separately.

## Closing meaning versus finished copy

The storyboard reference defers finished captions. The rubric still requires
the page's ending to answer its opening question. These can coexist: a plan
can specify the conclusion and its factual basis while leaving its final
wording for later.

- `../codex-sol-2/analyst/with-1/files/docs/story/storyboard.md` leaves an
  "answer placeholder based on facts". It does not resolve what the answer
  says, and its question criterion fails.
- `../codex-sol-2/analyst/with-2/files/docs/story/storyboard.md` specifies a
  proposed synthesis: orient the dashboard around a question, show evidence
  and limits, and lead to a next action. It attributes that synthesis to the
  person's confirmed memo structure, not an undocumented history. Its
  question criterion passes while polished copy remains deferred.
- The earlier identical-prompt repeat in
  `../codex-sol-1-diagnosis/analyst/with-1/` changes the question score. That
  remains evidence of judgement variability; it is not a replacement score.

Review interpretation: a resolved closing meaning with a supportable basis
is enough at the planning stage; an unspecified promise to write an answer
later is not. This documents how to review the existing requirement. It does
not modify the judge prompt, scoring code or any saved judgement.

## Decisions

1. Do not ban paper objects or force a new object class merely to produce
   variety. Compare story structures and explanatory relationships after
   removing the profession and project labels.
2. Preserve accessible HTML and static diagrams. Some failed backdrop
   reasons cite duplicated HTML, but equivalent accessible content is not
   itself evidence of decoration. Review what the spatial arrangement
   explains. This is already clarified in `evals/README.md`.
3. For a further creative experiment, first obtain or add as a separately
   versioned fixture one concrete shareable work example. For physics that
   could be the actual approved schematic plus an account of a calibration
   decision. Do not fabricate a procedure or overwrite the historical
   fixture to improve the score. This is a proposed follow-up, not a fixture
   change made here.
4. Keep the embedded opening's unexplained terminology and the earlier
   nurse's CNM1 as a separate plain-language problem. A future correction
   should test those two saved misses and student coverage.
5. Add no creative skill instructions or rubric changes in this review.
   The current references already require differing concepts, explanatory
   arrangements and a closing answer. The evidence supports a concrete
   concept review and clearer scoring examples before another wording loop.

The author's assessment of which concept is worth building remains part of
the design's review process. No engine, prototype or website was built.
