# Diagrams

Open this when a chapter's scene explains something: where, when, how a thing works, what changed, how much.

The site this skill came from draws its diagrams; it does not model them. Its pages carry flat, precise figures in the look's ink and its one accent, each fitted to its page, with the words beside it, and 3D moves the pages between them. Each is built from real data: the globe from the world's real country outlines, the papers from the front pages the person learned from, the maze solved by an actual search, one real photograph split at a seam. That is why each means something.

## Choose by the question

A diagram answers one question about the person. Choose its kind by the question, then check that the fact sheet holds what it needs. A question the facts cannot answer gets no diagram.

| The question | The diagram | What it needs | How it is drawn |
|---|---|---|---|
| Where? | A route on a real map | Each place, its years, the order | The map from the places' coordinates; the route in the accent, drawn by the scroll; a second map at a larger scale where places are too close |
| When? | A timeline | A real start and end for every span | A bar for each span on a time axis, the current one in the accent |
| How does it flow? | A pipeline | What goes in, each step, what comes out | Boxes joined by arrows in one direction; what moves now in the accent |
| In what order, with what checks? | A gated process | The steps by name, the rule between them | A track through gates; a thing travels it, held at each gate until its check passes |
| What changed? | A before and after | Both states, from one source | One picture split at a seam the scroll moves |
| What is inside? | A cutaway or layers | The parts and how they sit | Layers in order, the one that matters in the accent |
| How much? | A comparison at true scale | The numbers, what they measure, whose they are | Lengths, areas or counted dots in true proportion, each number beside it |
| How does it behave? | A trace over time | Measured values at their times | A line on axes through the measured points only |
| What did they solve? | A working model | The problem's real rules | The model runs as the reader scrolls: the maze is solved, the cutover steps through |

## How a diagram is drawn

- **One idea a diagram.** A figure that needs a legend holds two.
- **From the person's data, never a symbol.** Their places at their coordinates, their stages by name, their numbers at true scale. A cylinder for a database, a tube for a route and a box for a service are symbols, and in a scene symbols look crude. So does a coastline drawn by hand.
- **Drawn flat, on a surface:** a page, a screen, a board in the world. Draw it with the engine's pen, on a face or a `Figure`, as `engine/README.md` says under Drawing figures. Do not build a diagram from meshes. 3D moves the surfaces between figures.
- **Ink for the structure, the accent for the subject, everything else faint.**
- **Few words, readable at the size shown on a phone,** or set beside the figure in the caption.
- **Fewer diagrams, finished.** One picture is not used for two chapters; where two want the same, give one of them another question.
