# Asking

## Facts

At the first stop your questions go in one message, numbered. `reading-a-profile.md` says what they are.

## A choice

Where you offer a choice, give the ones you would not choose as fully as the one you would. Then say which you would choose, and why.

If the person tells you to choose, choose. Write what you chose, and what you chose it over, into `docs/story/decided-for-you.md`.

## If the person will not answer

Some people will not answer your questions, or will say: decide everything. Then:

- **What is a matter of taste, you decide:** the concept, the look, the order. Say which you chose and what you chose it over.
- **A fact, you do not decide.** What you could not confirm stays off the page. A figure whose meaning you do not know, a project you cannot say ever ran, a paper you cannot say they wrote, a title you cannot say is theirs: leave it out, or carry it in the script marked "to confirm", and stand nothing on it. Build the concept on the facts that needed no confirming. The person's own resume is no exception: if you asked whether a project ever ran and they did not say, "real-time system" is still not confirmed, and it is not printed.
- **Say what is waiting on them,** as a numbered list at the top of what you hand over.

A wrong guess about someone's taste costs them an afternoon. A wrong claim about their work is read by the people they most want to convince, and it is their name on the site, not yours.
