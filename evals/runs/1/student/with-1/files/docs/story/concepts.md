## Concept 1 — The Job Card

**The question.** How do you know what's actually wrong?

**The form.** Her own job card, from the co-op — her words: "what the customer says is wrong, what I find, the parts, what I did, the test ride." Used in its own order:

| Chapter (the card's own field) | Role it holds |
|---|---|
| Complaint | The opening — her name, and the question |
| What I find | Who / Where from — Hallam, the co-op since 2023, how she works |
| Parts | What — the work laid out: bracket, charger, the bike |
| What I did | What — the work shown working: the redesign, the build, the repairs |
| Test ride | What came of it — outcomes, stated plainly, including the ones not yet in |
| (close) | The close — the answer, and how to reach her |

No "what others say" chapter — there are no testimonials to put in one.

**The object.** Eight job cards, simple rounded tickets like a service counter's, standing fanned at rest like a spike of tickets waiting to be collected: co-op (2023–), buying the bike, the inventory, stripping the paint, truing the wheel, the bottom bracket, the hand-crank charger, the pedal box bracket. Each card's face carries that job's own photo and text.

**What it becomes.**
- *Opening:* the cards stand closed and fanned, like tickets on a spike. Her name and the question sit on the top card.
- *Parts:* the fan opens into a grid, each card flipped to its "what's wrong" face with a real photo (the bent wheel, the heavy bracket, the dead charger module).
- *Test ride:* the same cards flip again to their outcome face — the bracket's card reads 18%, lighter in CAD and simulation, and is marked plainly as not yet machined; the charger's card reads four minutes for one percent, stated as it is.
- *Close:* the cards restack into one pile, in order, like a finished day's tickets. Her contact sits on the top one.

**The opening and the close.** It opens on a spike of tickets, as at the start of her Saturday at the co-op. It closes the same way, the pile whole again, the day's jobs done.

**What it stands on.** Her description of the job card (message, 2026-10-10); the five Slow Miles posts, each already shaped as a complaint-to-test-ride story; the bracket and charger facts, including the ones not yet finished.

**What it needs from her.** Nothing essential — the photos she says she has of the bike and the CAD screenshots cover most cards. The co-op card can run on text alone if there's no photo of the workshop itself.

**Why it's hers.** It is the literal document she names when asked what she works from. It could not sit on a colleague's site unless that colleague also wrote job cards for a living.

## Concept 2 — The Logbook

**The question.** What does a logbook say that a CV leaves out?

**The form.** The workshop logbook she also named, read in date order: 2023 (joining the co-op) → 2025, spring (the hand-crank charger) → 2025, buying the bike → October 2025 (joining the Chassis sub-team) → ongoing (the restoration posts) → close (what's next).

**The object.** A single bound logbook of six to eight pages, lying open; pages turn one at a time, each carrying a dated entry with its photo.

**What it becomes.**
- *Opening:* the closed logbook, her name on the cover, the question beneath it.
- *Middle chapters:* pages turn one by one, each a dated entry.
- *Close:* the pages fan out flat into a single timeline strip, the whole run of entries visible at once, with her contact at the last page.

**The opening and the close.** Opens shut; closes fanned flat, the whole record laid bare.

**What it stands on.** The dated facts on the CV, and the logbook she named.

**What it needs from her.** Nothing beyond what's already given.

**Why it's hers.** It uses a document she actually named — but a logbook kept in date order is closer to a diary than to a trade's reasoning, and the chapters here are really just "then, then, then." It would not take much to retitle this for a lab notebook or a pilot's log; it leans toward the emblem of record-keeping in general rather than toward her and what she does with what she finds.

## Concept 3 — The Teardown

**The question.** What's worth keeping, and what needs to be remade?

**The form.** Her own ritual, from the second Slow Miles post: lay everything out, then sort it — keep, fix, or replace — then put it back together.

| Chapter | Role it holds |
|---|---|
| Teardown | The opening — everything out on the floor, her name, the question |
| Where from | Who / Where from |
| Keep | How — what already works: the skills, the years at the co-op |
| Fix | What — what's in progress and not yet proven: the bracket, pending its November machining |
| Replace | What came of it — honestly, what a placement is for, not a confession of failing |
| Reassembled | The close — the whole put back together, and how to reach her |

**The object.** Nine simple mechanical pieces — gear, bolt, bracket, hub, wheel rim, pedal, housing, a length of chain, a card tag — scattered at the opening as on her garage floor, carrying real photos and labels on their faces.

**What it becomes.**
- *Opening:* the pieces lie scattered, as in her own inventory photos.
- *Keep / Fix / Replace:* the same pieces gather into three piles as the page scrolls, each piece carrying its own tag and photo.
- *Reassembled:* the piles draw together into one whole shape — the bike, or a machine built of the same parts — at the close.

**The opening and the close.** Opens in pieces; closes whole.

**What it stands on.** The "An inventory" post; the keep/fix/replace instinct it describes; the bracket's unfinished state; the charger's honest result.

**What it needs from her.** The garage-floor photos she says she has, ideally — generic part shapes would work, but her own photos would make the sorting real rather than illustrative.

**Why it's hers.** It's a ritual she already keeps and already wrote about, not a borrowed metaphor for "taking stock." The risk is tonal: unless "Replace" is written as what a placement is for rather than what's wrong with her, it can read like a job-interview cliché about weaknesses, which cuts against her own instruction not to undersell or oversell herself.

## Which I'd choose

**Concept 1, the Job Card.** It's the one document she named first and without prompting, and it already fits everything in the material without bending it: her bike posts are already complaint-to-test-ride stories, the bracket's unfinished state sits naturally on a "test ride" face marked not yet run, and the charger's unflattering result reads as one more honest card rather than an exception. It gives a two-minute reader the exact thing an engineering placement reader is looking for — how she finds what's wrong and whether she checks that a fix worked — without any chapter that has to be padded or any figure that has to be softened.

The Logbook (2) is safe but closer to a diary than to a trade's way of reasoning. The Teardown (3) is a close second and could be built instead if she'd rather the page end on "what's next" than on a day's work done — worth asking if the Job Card's ending feels right, or if Teardown's reassembly fits her better.
