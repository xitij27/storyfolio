# Storyboard — The Job Card

Concept: [[concepts]], Concept 1. Facts: [[facts]]. Judgement calls: [[decided-for-you]].

The object: eight job cards — simple rounded ticket shapes, like the ones at a bike co-op's counter. One each for: the co-op (2023–present), buying the bike, the inventory, stripping the paint, truing the wheel, the bottom bracket, the hand-crank charger, the pedal box bracket. ## Someone with thirty seconds

The first screen (1.1) carries her name, her field and institute, and the question — that's who she is and what she's after. The fanned eight cards behind the text are the proof at a glance: a visible, countable body of finished and in-progress work, before anyone reads a word of it. A thin side rail, present throughout, jumps straight to any chapter by name; a "Closed out ↓" link in the same rail skips straight to chapter 6, where the email and CV sit. A visitor who never scrolls past screen one still sees the name, the question, and the stack of proof; one click reaches contact.

## The look and the type

The object is a job card — a workshop ticket, not a brochure. Three looks, each a plausible one for that object:

1. **Service counter.** Ground: warm kraft/off-white, like ticket card stock (`#F1EAE0`-ish). Ink: near-black charcoal (`#262220`-ish). Accent: a worn safety-orange, like a repair tag's string. Pieces: manila-card surface, a punched hole top-left, a faint perforated edge.
2. **Workshop steel.** Ground: cool brushed-steel grey-blue. Ink: dark graphite. Accent: safety yellow or chain-grease amber. Pieces: look stamped, like a zinc-plated tool tag — slight bevel, no paper grain.
3. **Night ride.** Ground: near-black, like a lane after dark. Ink: warm off-white. Accent: reflective amber, like a bike reflector catching a headlight. Pieces: faintly glow at their edge, as if taped.

Two typefaces: a plain humanist sans for all running text (captions, chapter names, the side rail) — something in the Inter / IBM Plex Sans family, chosen for being read quickly by someone with two minutes, not for personality — and a monospace or typewriter face for everything printed on the cards themselves (the job-card fields, the diagnosis lines, the outcomes), since a real job card is typed or hand-filled, not typeset. IBM Plex Mono or similar.

She picks a look by scrolling through built samples, once this is built — not from the swatches above alone.
