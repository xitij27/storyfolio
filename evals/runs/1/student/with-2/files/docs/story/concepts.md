# Three concepts

## A — The job card (recommended)

**The question.** How do you find out what's actually wrong?

**The form** — the job card she fills out at the co-op (her own words: "what the customer says is wrong, what I find, the parts, what I did, the test ride"):

| Chapter | Role |
|---|---|
| Header (name, date) | The opening — her name, and the question |
| Customer says | Who is asking — the thing as first reported, wrong or incomplete |
| What I find | How she works — the diagnosis, where the real fault turns out to differ from the reported one |
| Parts | The materials and tools of the trade — SolidWorks, MATLAB, the lathe and mill |
| What I did | What — the work itself, shown working: bracket redesign, the charger, the bike |
| Test ride | What came of it — outcomes, stated as plainly as she gave them to me, including the ones that didn't fully work |
| Close | The answer, and how to reach her |

("What others say" is left out — there is nothing to put in it, and that's stated plainly rather than padded.)

**The object** — the bike's own parts: hub, wheel, bottom bracket, pedal, frame tube, headset, chain, and the pedal-box mounting bracket. Eight pieces, each a simple machined shape, each one she's actually worked on. The bracket is the only piece shown as a CAD wireframe rather than solid metal, because it hasn't been machined yet — that difference is true, not a style choice.

**What it becomes:**
- *Header:* the parts arrive scattered and rusted, settling into the same flat grid as her "every part on the garage floor" photo.
- *What I find:* one part — the hub — splits open as the page explains the cracked flange hiding under the grease. The bracket piece, still a wireframe, sits apart from the others.
- *Test ride:* the parts assemble into the shape of a whole bike. Where a fix is real and tested, the part is solid. The bracket stays a wireframe a moment longer, with a plain note that it's machined in November — then, at the very close, it's shown taking its place, solid, because by the time this site is read that will likely be true.

**The opening and the close.** Opens on "Thirty pounds and a flat tyre" — the parts in their first, broken state. Closes with the object whole and at rest, her name, and a way to reach her.

**What it stands on.** The job card line she gave me directly; the five blog posts, in their own order; the bracket's 18%-lighter-in-CAD figure, captioned exactly as she gave it (CAD and simulation, not yet tested); the charger's honest four-minutes-for-one-percent outcome; the co-op dates.

**What it needs from her.** The real photos of the parts on the garage floor (she has these), and the CAD view of the bracket (she has this). No one else's yes is needed — nothing here is anyone else's work or likeness.

**Why it's hers.** The job card isn't a device I'm imposing — it's the actual paper she already fills out every Saturday, and the object is the actual pile of parts she already photographed and labelled herself.

## B — The logbook

**The question.** What do you check before you believe the problem?

**The form** — the workshop logbook (her other named document), as dated entries: Entry 1 through Entry 6, one per blog post plus one for the bracket, run in date order.

**The object** — the garage floor itself, as a grid of labelled square tiles (echoing her "An inventory" post: keep, fix, replace), rather than individual parts. Scrolling tips the grid from a flat plan view into a 3D floor the visitor walks over.

**What it becomes:**
- *Opening:* tiles lie flat and bare, each labelled only with a part's name.
- *Entry 4 (the wheel):* the "fix" tile for the hub lifts and rotates to show the crack.
- *Close:* every tile flips to "fixed" or "kept," and the grid tilts back to flat, finished.

**Opening and close.** Opens on the bare inventory grid; closes on the same grid, all tiles turned over.

**What it stands on.** The same facts as A — the inventory post gives the keep/fix/replace structure directly.

**What it needs from her.** The same garage-floor photos as A.

**Why it's hers.** The keep/fix/replace sorting is her own post, not a borrowed device.

**Why I'd choose this less:** it sorts her work into a grid rather than carrying the visitor through one diagnosis to the next. A placement reader skimming for two minutes gets a tidy classification, not a worked example of how she thinks — and the question it answers is thinner than it first looks, because a grid doesn't naturally have a middle.

## C — The drawing

**The question.** What does the next version fix?

**The form** — an engineering drawing's title block and revision table (part name, material, mass, revision, change note), run as revisions A, B, and onward.

**The object** — the pedal-box bracket alone, exploded into its machined features: mounting face, boss, rib, bolt holes, fillet. Five or six pieces, all from one part.

**What it becomes:**
- *Rev A:* the heavier, original bracket shape, solid.
- *Rev B:* the features thin and reposition themselves into the 18%-lighter redesign, still a wireframe.
- *Close:* a blank Rev C title block, waiting for November's machining.

**Opening and close.** Opens on Rev A, solid and unremarkable; closes on an empty, dated Rev C, honestly waiting.

**What it stands on.** Only the bracket lines of the fact sheet.

**What it needs from her.** The CAD views, and ideally the original Rev A geometry for contrast, which she may not have kept.

**Why it's hers.** It's her actual part, through its actual revisions.

**Why I'd choose this least:** it puts nearly the whole site on the one piece of work that is, by her own account, still unbuilt and untested — and leaves out the bike, which is where most of her real, finished, provable work is. It risks the exact thing she asked me to avoid: looking like more than a second-year has done.

---

## Which I'd choose

**A, the job card.** It's the only one of the three with a real beginning, middle and end that follow from each other — broken, diagnosed, worked on, tested, whole — and it's carried by the document and the object she actually uses, not one I've picked for effect. It also does the most with her strongest, most complete material (five real blog posts of real diagnosis and repair) while still giving the unfinished bracket an honest place, instead of resting the whole site on it.
