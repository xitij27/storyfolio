# Storyboard — Mina Park

Concept A, the job card. The question: **How do you find out what's actually wrong?**

The object: eight pieces from her own work — hub, wheel, bottom bracket, pedal, frame tube, headset, chain, and the Formula Student pedal-box bracket. All but the bracket are real, physical, worked metal. The bracket alone renders as a thin CAD wireframe, because it hasn't been machined yet — that's true, not a style choice.

## Someone with thirty seconds

The first screen (1.1) carries her name, her course, and the question. The fixed top bar carries a "get in touch" link from the very first screen, which jumps straight to the Close. The rail jumps to any chapter without scrolling through the others. A visitor who taps straight to "Parts" or "Test ride" still gets one real proof (the bracket figure, or the charger's honest result) without reading anything else.

## The look and the type

**Workshop** (recommended) — ground: warm concrete grey, like the garage floor. Ink: near-black charcoal. One accent: a single oxide-orange, the colour of a job-card sticker or hazard tape, used only for tags ("fix") and the one "get in touch" link. The eight real pieces render as brushed steel and aluminium with worn, oily highlights; the bracket alone renders in thin cyan wireframe lines, so "built" and "not yet built" are never confused.

**Paper job card** — ground: cream, like a carbon-copy pad. Ink: ballpoint navy. Accent: a red-pen line, used only where a fault was found. Pieces render as pencil-line sketches rather than solid metal.

**Night workshop** — ground: near-black charcoal, one work lamp. Ink: off-white. Accent: warm incandescent yellow, where the lamp falls. Pieces are lit dramatically, in and out of shadow; the bracket glows cyan.

I'd choose **Workshop** — it reads as an actual garage in daylight, not staged, and keeps the orange tag colour doing real work (marking "fix," marking the one link that matters) instead of decorating.

Type: one plain, humanist sans for all reading text — captions, the question, the contact details. One separate face for anything printed on the pieces or the tags themselves — a condensed, stamped, stencil-leaning face, the kind an actual parts label or job-card stamp uses. The two are never mixed within one line.
