# Storyboard — Ines Okafor, "The Ferment Log"

Question: **What happens to what's left over?**
Object: nine jars, plain glass, labelled — the shape of her own jar wall. The same nine jars stand through the whole page, rearranged chapter to chapter, whole again at the close.

## Beats

### 1. Started

**Beat 1.1 — 1 screen.**
*Stand:* no jars yet — trim, salt grains and a scrap of glass drift in the dark, slowly gathering toward nine loose clusters.
*Faces:* nothing yet; one faint image surfaces among the drift — the photograph of her grandmother's ogiri pot, then fades back.
*Adds:* ambient drift, light parallax with scroll and pointer.
*Caption:* "Ines Okafor. Head Chef, Saltmarsh, Leeds." / "Cut kitchen food waste from 14 kg to 5 kg a week." / "What happens to what's left over? ↓"

**Beat 1.2 — 1 screen.**
*Stand:* the nine clusters resolve into plain glass jars; lids close one by one as you scroll.
*Faces:* still blank glass.
*Adds:* a quiet mechanical seal on each lid closing.
*Caption:* "My grandmother never measured anything. I learned the rest from two books — The Art of Fermentation, and Wild Fermentation."

### 2. Salted

**Beat 2.1 — 1 screen.**
*Stand:* the nine sealed jars settle into a loose, unlabelled row at eye level, turning slowly.
*Faces:* bare glass, salt crystallising faintly on a few.
*Adds:* nothing beyond the jars.
*Caption:* "Most of what I care about happens before service. What we do with the trim, the whey, the stems and the bones."

### 3. Checked

**Beat 3.1 — 2 screens, scroll-scrubbed.**
*Stand:* the row straightens into a single horizontal timeline. As you scroll, five jars step forward in order and print a label; the other four recede, softly blurred, behind the line.
*Faces (in order):*
- "Leeds Kitchen College — Diploma in Professional Cookery, 2014–2016."
- "Brassica, Manchester — Chef de Partie. Larder and garnish, then sauce. 2016–2019."
- "Fjaerland Fermentation Lab, Norway — A stage, ten weeks, unpaid. I washed jars, labelled them, and helped on two batches of garum. 2018."
- "The Corn Exchange Kitchen, Leeds — Sous Chef. Ran the pass on a 90-cover service. Took over ordering and brought the supplier list from 31 to 12. 2019–2022."
- "Saltmarsh, Leeds — Head Chef from September 2022, having run the kitchen as acting head chef from that March."
*Adds:* a thin drawn line connecting the five jars; whichever is centred lifts slightly and sharpens.
*Caption:* each line above, revealed as its jar centres.

### 4. Ready

**Beat 4.1 — 2 screens.**
*Stand:* all nine jars arrange into a 3×3 grid — the jar wall.
*Faces:* real photographs. Dish and jar-wall shots credited "Photograph: Priya Nair, for Saltmarsh"; larder and label shots credited "Photograph: Ines Okafor"; one jar carries the photograph of her grandmother's ogiri pot.
*Adds:* a soft shelf-light across the grid; hover or tap swaps a jar's face to a second photo where there is one.
*Caption, main:* "The jar wall. People ask about this more than anything else — along with the koji room. It's a cupboard."
*Caption, aside:* "The menu is the last thing written, each week. It follows the larder, not the other way round."

### 5. What came of it

**Beat 5.1 — 2 screens.**
*Stand:* four jars step forward from the wall and tip, pouring into four waiting cards laid in a row; the remaining five stay behind as a soft blur.
*Faces:* a number appears on each jar as it tips.
*Adds:* a simple pour of shapes, not a liquid simulation.
*Captions:*
- "14 kg to 5 kg. What the kitchen throws away each week — prep and plate, weighed over a month in 2023. Bones go to stock, not the bin."
- "40+ ferments, vinegars and garums on rotation in the larder."
- "Two apprentices a year, since 2023."
- "31 to 12. The supplier list, brought down since taking over ordering at the Corn Exchange."
Below the row, text-only, no photograph: "Sunday Pot. A hot meal every Sunday at St Aidan's hall, cooked from what restaurants in the city have left over. Co-founded, 2021."

### 6. What others say

**Beat 6.1 — 1 screen.**
*Stand:* eight jars blur back into a soft wall; one comes fully forward, centred, lit.
*Faces:* Callum's line, set like a label on the glass.
*Caption:* "Ines turned our bins into a larder. The best thing on the menu most weeks started as something we used to throw away." — Callum Reyes, owner of Saltmarsh.

## Someone with thirty seconds

The first screen (beat 1.1) carries her name, her role, and one proof — the waste figure — without scrolling. A rail, visible throughout, jumps straight to any chapter. One link on the first screen skips straight to Open (the close) for someone who wants the contact details and nothing else.

## Without the scene

The text and photographs are ordinary HTML over the scene in every case, so none of the content in the beats above is lost — only the motion and the 3D changes.

| Where | What the visitor gets |
|---|---|
| On a phone | The same seven chapters, same photos and captions. The jar wall becomes a single-column stack or a swipeable row instead of a 3×3 grid; the timeline in "Checked" becomes a vertical list you scroll rather than scrub sideways; hover-to-reveal becomes tap-to-reveal; the side rail becomes a small jump menu. Jar count on screen at once is reduced where needed to keep it smooth. |
| With reduced motion | Every chapter opens straight into its resting arrangement — the grid in "Ready," the row of cards in "What came of it," and so on — reached by a plain fade, not a scroll-scrubbed transition. No drifting particles, no parallax. |
| With no WebGL | The jars are replaced by flat photographs and styled cards in the same layout and order — a real jar-wall photo standing in for the grid, a simple timeline list standing in for the scrubbed line. Same captions, same credits. |
| With no JavaScript | A plain HTML page: heading, role, the seven chapters in order as sections with their photographs and captions, a simple in-page contents list instead of the rail, and ordinary links for every contact method. Nothing is withheld. |

## The look and the type

Three looks, each a ground, an ink, one accent, and the surface the jars are made of. Pick by scrolling once it's built.

1. **Linen day** — ground: warm linen/oatmeal paper. Ink: near-black. Accent: a deep brine green. Jars: clear glass, the real photograph visible through the glass like contents in a jar.
2. **Larder dark** — ground: deep charcoal, a kitchen at night. Ink: warm off-white. Accent: a turmeric-amber. Jars: dark glass with a warm rim-light, labels like pale paper tags.
3. **Steel and salt** — ground: cool stainless grey. Ink: soft white. Accent: a bright chilli red. Jars: brushed steel-and-glass, labels stamped like a label gun.

My own lean, to confirm once built: Linen day — it reads as a working daytime kitchen, and sits best against real food photography.

Two typefaces: a plain, warm working serif for the running text (the register of a recipe card, not a corporate sans), and a stamped or handwritten label face for whatever prints on a jar itself (the register of a kitchen label gun or a grease pencil) — since that's the form's own type, not the page's.
