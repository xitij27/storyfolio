# Concepts — Ines Okafor

Three concepts, differing in form and object. See `docs/story/facts.md` for the facts each stands on.

---

## Concept 1 — The Ferment Log

**The question:** What happens to what's left over?

Runs through almost every fact: the trim, whey, stems and bones that become the larder; the kitchen waste cut from 14 kg to 5 kg a week; the supplier list cut from 31 to 12; Sunday Pot, cooked from what restaurants have left over; Brine, a zine about preserving. It is a real question for her now, not a mood word, and the close can answer it plainly: it becomes the best thing on the menu.

**The form — the ferment log,** the document she named first as what the kitchen runs on: the date a jar was started, the salt, the days it's checked, when it's ready.

1. **Started** — opening. Her name, the question. Where this begins: her grandmother, who never measured anything; the two books.
2. **Salted** — how she works. Whole-vegetable, trim-first. The thing added that starts a transformation.
3. **Checked** — where from. The jobs as check-points on a timeline: Brassica, the Fjaerland stage, Corn Exchange, Saltmarsh, each with its years.
4. **Ready** — what. The larder and the jar wall at work: real dishes, real jars, credited photos. The menu, written last, not first.
5. **What came of it** — outcomes. The waste cut (the kitchen's, weighed over a month in 2023), the apprentices, the supplier cut, Sunday Pot.
6. **What others say** — Callum's line, on its own.
7. **Open** — the close. The answer, and how to reach her: apply to apprentice, book a workshop, write to her, read Brine.

**The object — jars,** nine or ten of them, plain and labelled, the shape of the ones on her own jar wall.

- *Started:* the jars begin as scraps and salt gathering in the dark, not yet sealed — the opening shows where the practice comes from, before it shows her.
- *Checked:* the jars arrange into a single line, one per job, each labelled with a role and its years, read left to right like labels down a shelf.
- *Ready:* the jars arrange into the grid of the actual jar wall, each face carrying a real photograph.
- *Close:* the jars settle back to rest as the wall, one jar carrying Callum's line, before the names and the ways to reach her.

**Opening and close:** opens on ingredients and salt in the dark, closes on the jar wall at rest, lit, whole.

**What it stands on:** nearly the whole fact sheet — the day-to-day tools she named, the jobs and years, the confirmed waste figure, Priya's credited photos, her own larder and pot photos, Callum's quote, Sunday Pot, and the audience she named.

**What it needs from her:** Priya's photo files and the exact credit line she wants used; a decision on whether Sunday Pot gets a photo at all, given the "no faces unless asked" rule; nothing from Olu, since Brine is named here, not shown through his drawings.

**Why it's hers:** the jar wall and the ferment log are the two things she said the kitchen runs on, in that order of her own choosing. No one else's site has this jar wall, filled from her own forty-plus ferments and a grandmother who never measured anything.

## Concept 2 — The Prep List

**The question:** How does a kitchen get ready before anyone orders?

Fits her own line that most of what she cares about happens before service. Threads through the stations she's worked, the ordering she took over, the larder built before the menu is written.

**The form — the prep list,** the other document she named: what a kitchen does before doors open.

1. **Mise en place** — opening. Her name, the question. What she sets up from: the books, her grandmother.
2. **Stations** — where from. Larder and garnish, then sauce (Brassica); the stage (Fjaerland); the pass (Corn Exchange); head chef (Saltmarsh) — each a station on a line, with years.
3. **Method** — how. Whole-vegetable, trim-first; the ferment log explained in passing.
4. **The board** — what. The larder and jar wall at work, real photographs.
5. **Service** — what came of it. The waste cut, the supplier cut, the apprentices; the menu, written last.
6. **Pass** — what others say. Callum's line, framed as the one who checks a dish before it leaves the kitchen.
7. **Open** — the close. The answer, and how to reach her.

**The object — tickets,** nine or ten index cards clipped along a rail, the kind a kitchen pass carries.

- *Opening:* blank tickets pin up, one line scrawled on each.
- *Stations:* the tickets arrange into a line, one per role, read like a kitchen line, left to right.
- *The board:* the tickets flip, becoming a wall whose faces carry real dish photographs.
- *Close:* the tickets clip back onto the rail at rest, in order, done.

**Opening and close:** opens on a bare rail being filled, closes on a full rail, orderly, service about to start.

**What it stands on:** the same job history and figures as Concept 1, read through the stations rather than the jars.

**What it needs from her:** the same photo and credit decisions as Concept 1.

**Why it's hers:** she ran the pass herself at Corn Exchange, and named the prep list as one of the two things the kitchen runs on. But it leans on the pass and the stations, and says less about the jar wall, the koji cupboard, and the staff meal — the three things she says people actually ask her about.

## Concept 3 — An Issue of Brine

**The question:** What's worth keeping?

Sits against the waste figures and the supplier cut: what she keeps, measured against what she lets go. Brine is itself a real object of hers — four issues, 2022 to 2025 — so the form doesn't have to be borrowed from the kitchen at all.

**The form — an issue of the zine,** read front to back.

1. **Cover** — opening. Her name, the question.
2. **Editor's note** — who. Her own words on what she cares about.
3. **A place** — where from. The jobs and years, laid out like a zine's "visited" page.
4. **A method** — how. The ferment log, explained in her own voice.
5. **A larder** — what. The jar wall at work, real photographs.
6. **A note from the kitchen** — what came of it. The figures.
7. **Praise** — what others say. Callum's line.
8. **Back page** — the close.

**The object — spreads,** nine or ten flat pages, laid out two-up like an open zine.

- *Cover:* a single spread lies alone, one photograph placed on it.
- *Where from:* the spreads lay out side by side like a contents page, one per job.
- *A larder:* the spreads arrange into a grid, each face a real photograph.

**What it needs from her:** this concept's whole premise is an issue of her own zine — and Olu draws for Brine. Even leaving his drawings out, a reader who knows Brine may expect his hand in it. It would need either his yes, or a clear decision with her to make the pages carry only her own photographs and words, with no illustration standing in for his.

**Why it's hers:** Brine is a real thing only she publishes. But of the three, it depends most on someone else's work she hasn't yet cleared, and says the least about the jar wall and the larder, which are what people actually ask her about.

---

## Decision

Ines chose Concept 1, the Ferment Log, on the recommendation above.

## Which I would choose

**Concept 1, the Ferment Log.** Its question holds the most of the fact sheet without strain — the waste cut, the supplier cut, Sunday Pot and Brine are all, honestly, the same question asked in different parts of her working life. Its form and object are both things she named herself, unprompted, as what she actually works from. And it needs nothing from Olu to stand up, where Concept 3 does. Concept 2 is close behind and shares most of its material, but spends its best space on the pass and the stations rather than the jar wall, the koji cupboard and the staff meal, which are what people actually stop her to ask about.
