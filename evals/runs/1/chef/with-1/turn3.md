Here's the whole script — **"The Ferment Log."** Question running through it: *what happens to what's left over?* The object is nine plain, labelled glass jars, the shape of your own jar wall — same nine jars the whole way down, rearranged chapter to chapter, whole again at the end.

## 1. Started — opening
Trim, salt and glass drift in the dark and gather into nine blank jars; lids seal shut one by one as you scroll. Your grandmother's ogiri pot surfaces faintly, then fades. First thing anyone sees, no scrolling required: your name, "Head Chef, Saltmarsh, Leeds," the waste figure as proof, and the question. A rail jumps to any chapter; one link skips straight to the close. Caption: *"My grandmother never measured anything. I learned the rest from two books — The Art of Fermentation, and Wild Fermentation."*

## 2. Salted — how you work
The nine sealed jars settle into a loose, turning row. Caption, your own words: *"Most of what I care about happens before service. What we do with the trim, the whey, the stems and the bones."*

## 3. Checked — where from
The row straightens into a timeline. Scrolling steps five jars forward in turn, each printing a label as it centres: Leeds Kitchen College → Brassica → the Fjaerland stage → Corn Exchange → Saltmarsh, each with its own line and years (the stage described plainly as ten unpaid weeks washing and labelling jars and helping on two garum batches — not research). You can click or tap any jar to open its line again.

## 4. Ready — what
All nine jars arrange into the actual 3×3 jar wall, each face a real photograph — credited to Priya Nair where they're hers, to you where they're yours, one jar carrying your grandmother's pot. Tap a jar to enlarge it. Captions name the jar wall and the koji cupboard ("it's a cupboard," your own line) and note the menu is written last, following the larder.

## 5. What came of it — outcomes
Four jars step out and tip into four cards: the waste cut (14→5 kg, the kitchen's, weighed over a month in 2023, bones excluded), the 40+ ferments on rotation, two apprentices a year since 2023, the supplier list (31→12). Sunday Pot sits below in text only, no photo.

## 6. What others say
Eight jars blur into the background; one comes forward, lit, Callum's line set on its glass like a label.

## 7. Open — close
The nine jars return to the wall, whole, at rest. A plain line answers the question — what's left over becomes the larder, the staff meal, a Sunday at St Aidan's, four issues of a zine. Then contact: applying as an apprentice, booking a workshop (first Monday of the month), press/food writers, and how to get hold of Brine.

## On a phone, and without the 3D
Same seven chapters, same text and photos every time — the HTML is always there underneath. On a phone: the grid becomes a stack or swipeable row, the timeline becomes a vertical list, hover becomes tap, the rail becomes a small jump menu. With reduced motion: each chapter opens straight into its resting state, no scroll-scrubbing, no drift. Without WebGL: real photographs and styled cards stand in for every jar arrangement. Without JavaScript: a plain HTML page, same seven sections in order, plain anchor links, nothing withheld.

## Look and type
Three options, decided by scrolling once it's built — my lean is the first:
1. **Linen day** — warm linen ground, near-black ink, deep brine green accent, clear glass jars with the photo visible through them.
2. **Larder dark** — charcoal ground, warm off-white ink, turmeric-amber accent, dark glass jars.
3. **Steel and salt** — cool grey ground, soft white ink, chilli red accent, brushed steel-and-glass jars.

Two typefaces: a plain working serif for running text (recipe-card register), and a stamped/handwritten label face for whatever's printed on a jar itself.
