## The concept

**Question the page answers:** *What do you do with what's left over?*
**Object:** seven to nine plain glass jars, same size and shape, a blank label on each face. At rest, they're your jar wall.
**Form:** your own ferment log — date started, salt, days checked, when it's ready — stretched into eight chapters.

## The page, top to bottom

**1. Started** (opening) — One jar, alone, settles into frame. Your name, "Head Chef, Saltmarsh, Leeds," and the question underneath. A "skip to the answer" link sits right here for anyone who wants to jump straight to the end. A rail on the right edge names all eight chapters — click any one to jump there.

**2. Logged** (who) — The same jar turns slowly; a label writes itself across it in a hand like your own kitchen labels. Reads: "Most of what I care about happens before service…" and then what you learned from — Sandor Katz's two books, and your grandmother, who never measured anything. Nothing to click; just scroll.

**3. Dated** (where from) — The one jar splits into a row of five, left to right, spaced by year: Leeds Kitchen College (2014–16), Brassica (2016–19), the Fjaerland stage sitting slightly apart from the line (Jan–Mar 2018 — described exactly as what it was: ten weeks, unpaid, jars and labels and two batches of garum, never as research), Corn Exchange (2019–22), Saltmarsh (acting from March 2022, Head Chef from September 2022). Tap or hover any jar for its line.

**4. Salted** (how) — One jar enlarges; its label fills in, field by field, exactly like your ferment log: date started, salt, days checked, ready. Ends on "the menu, written last, not first." Scroll scrubs it; nothing to click.

**5. Checked** (what) — The row closes into a grid: the jar wall itself. Jars light up one by one carrying real photographs — your own larder shots first, then Priya's, credited visibly on screen ("Photographs: Priya Nair"). One jar holds the koji room, in your own words: "it is a cupboard." Two jars carry a line and a link: **Apply to the apprentice programme** and **Book a workshop**. Tap any jar to open its photo full size.

**6. Ready** (what came of it) — Three jars tip out of the grid and pour into three cards: **14 kg → 5 kg** of kitchen waste a week (the whole kitchen's, prep and plate only, weighed over a month in 2023 — bones never counted, they go to stock); **31 → 12** suppliers at Corn Exchange; **2 apprentices a year** since 2023. Tap a card for the line explaining whose result it is and over what time.

**7. Tasted** (what others say) — The wall dims, one jar comes forward, and Callum's line fills its label: *"Ines turned our bins into a larder…"* Nothing to click.

**8. Opened** (the close) — All jars return to the whole wall. The answer sets over it: *"What do you do with what's left over? You keep it."* Below: links to apply, book a workshop, write to you, or read Brine.

**Someone who doesn't scroll at all** gets your name, role and the question on the very first screen, and one link that jumps straight to the answer and the ways to reach you.

## Look and type

Three options, built from glass and paper — the jar and its label:

1. **Larder** *(my pick)* — warm paper ground, near-black ink, a brine-amber accent. Pale glass, kraft-paper labels.
2. **Koji room** — near-black blue-green ground (like the cupboard itself), warm off-white ink, same amber accent. Jars catch light against dark.
3. **Jar wall, daylight** — cool plaster grey, deep brown-black ink, a muted pickling green. Flatter, brighter — closer to how the restaurant actually looks at midday.

Two typefaces: a plain warm sans for everything you read (starting point: Public Sans or Inter), and a second, unpolished hand-like face used only on the jar labels themselves (starting point: Caveat, used sparingly) — never for body text, never as a joke.

## On a phone, and without the 3D

- **Phone:** same eight chapters and captions, but the row and the grid stack into one column, one jar at a time. The side rail becomes dots at the top that expand on tap. Hover becomes tap. Fewer jars render at once so it stays smooth on an older phone.
- **Reduced motion:** each chapter appears already settled — row already laid out, grid already closed, cards already poured — no turning or tweening.
- **No WebGL:** jars are replaced by the real photographs that would've sat on their faces, same order, same captions, same links.
- **No JavaScript:** one plain static page — eight headings in order, each with its photos and text, a plain list of links at the bottom. Nothing is missing, nothing moves.
