## Someone with thirty seconds

The first screen (Started) carries her name, her role ("Head Chef, Saltmarsh, Leeds"), and the question. A "skip to the answer" link under the question jumps straight to Opened — the wall, the answer, and the links to apply, book, write or read Brine. The right-edge rail lets a visitor jump to any chapter directly, so a reader can land on "Checked" or "Ready" for the proof (the jar wall, the figures) without scrolling through the rest.

## The look and the type

The object is glass and paper: a jar, and the label on it. Three looks, each built from that:

**1. Larder** (recommended) — Ground: unbleached paper, `#F3ECE1`. Ink: warm near-black, `#232019`. Accent: brine-amber, `#C98A2C`. The jars render pale and warm, catching light like glass on a shelf near a window; labels are kraft-paper beige with a slightly uneven, stamped edge.

**2. Koji room** — Ground: near-black blue-green, `#14191B`, as in the cupboard itself. Ink: warm off-white, `#EFE7DA`. Accent: the same brine-amber. The jars catch light against dark, as they would in the cupboard she calls the koji room.

**3. Jar wall, daylight** — Ground: cool plaster grey, `#E9E6E0`. Ink: deep brown-black, `#1E1A16`. Accent: a muted pickling green, `#7C8A54`. Flatter, brighter light; closer to how the restaurant itself actually looks at midday.

Two typefaces: a plain, warm sans for all reading text (body copy, captions, the figures), and a second face only for what's printed on the jars' labels — something with the unevenness of a hand-written kitchen label, not a script font or a joke of one. Exact families are a build-time choice; the brief for whoever picks them is "legible first, warm second" for the reading face, and "looks written by hand in a kitchen, not designed to look that way" for the label face.

Recommended pairing to start from: **Public Sans** or **Inter** for reading text; **Caveat** or a similarly unpolished hand face, used sparingly and only on label surfaces, never for body copy.
