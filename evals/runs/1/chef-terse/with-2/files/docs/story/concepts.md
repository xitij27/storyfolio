# Concepts — Ines Okafor

Three concepts, differing in form and object. Tested against swap, backdrop, depth, emblem, honesty and thirty-seconds before being written up here; the crate/order-sheet line that was drafted first failed the swap test (a "farm-to-table" crate is close to a restaurant-site cliché) and was reworked into the zine below.

---

## 1. The ferment log, kept in jars

**The question:** What are you doing while it waits?

**The form** — a ferment log entry, the document she named she works from day to day:

| Chapter | Role |
|---|---|
| Started | Opening — her name; what she started from (Katz's books, her grandmother, who never measured anything) |
| Logged by | Who — who's asking |
| Batch | Where from — Brassica, the Corn Exchange, Fjaerland, Saltmarsh, each a dated entry |
| Salt % | How — trim, whey, stems, bones; prep before service |
| Checked | What — the larder, *Brine*, Sunday Pot, the apprentice programme, shown working |
| Ready | What came of it — the waste cut, the apprentices taken on, four issues of *Brine* |
| Note | What others say — Callum's line, on a label |
| Ready to serve | Close — the answer, and how to reach her |

**The object:** a jar. Five to twelve of them, each a plain cylinder with a label plane that carries real photos and real numbers on its face. At rest they stand as the jar wall — the first thing people already ask her about, so this isn't a picture chosen for the site, it's the one she already has.

**What it becomes, in three chapters:**
- *Batch:* the jars line up and gather into a row of dated labels — Brassica '16, Corn Exchange '19, Fjaerland '18, Saltmarsh '22 — a timeline made of jars instead of a line.
- *Salt %:* a jar is shown in cross-section as a diagram: trim at the bottom, salt, liquid rising — her method, in the one object that already holds it.
- *Checked:* the jars become the wall itself, each label now a real photo or figure a visitor can open — the larder, the zine, Sunday Pot, the apprentices.

**Opening and close:** It opens on an empty jar and a date — hers, and what she started from. It closes with the jar wall whole again, the question answered, and a line for an apprentice applicant, a food writer or someone booking a workshop to write to her.

**What it stands on:** the ferment log description; the larder (40+ ferments); the jar wall as the thing people already ask about; Fjaerland (washing and labelling jars, two batches of garum); *Brine*; the apprentice programme; the waste figure; Callum's quote.

**What it needs from her:** Priya's photos of the larder and jar wall, credited to Priya; her own phone photos of the larder. Does not need Olu's drawings — it can run entirely without them.

**Why it's hers:** the jar wall is not a picture we chose to stand for fermentation — it's the one thing visitors to her actual kitchen already ask about first, and the log's own fields (salt, checked, ready) are hers before they're anyone's metaphor.

*Caution:* a jar is close to what people already picture when they hear "fermentation" — the risk is it reading as the trade's emblem rather than hers. What keeps it from that is specificity: no label is empty, and no jar says more than the fact sheet does.

## 2. The prep list, kept on tickets

**The question:** What turns scraps into the best thing on the menu?
— this is Callum's own line turned into a question.

**The form** — the prep list, the other document she named she works from:

| Chapter | Role |
|---|---|
| Top of the list | Opening — her name, and what the list always starts with |
| Section | Who — who's asking |
| Mise | Where from — Brassica, the Corn Exchange, Fjaerland, Saltmarsh, each a line prepped in order |
| Method | How — trim, whey, stems, bones; the menu written last, not first |
| On the pass | What — the larder, *Brine*, Sunday Pot, the apprentices, shown working |
| Done | What came of it — the waste cut, the apprentices, the zine's four issues |
| Chef's note | What others say — Callum's line |
| Service | Close — the answer, and how to reach her |

**The object:** a prep ticket — a small flat card, five to twelve of them, each carrying a real photo, dish or figure on its face. Tickets are not an emblem of cooking the way a chef's hat is; they're paperwork, which is exactly what she said she runs the kitchen on.

**What it becomes, in three chapters:**
- *Mise:* tickets clip onto a spike in order, forming a timeline of where she's worked.
- *Method:* a ticket flips, scrap written on one side, the finished dish on the other — her method as a single turn of a card.
- *On the pass:* the tickets fan out into a wall, each face a real photo or number — the larder, the zine, Sunday Pot, the apprentices.

**Opening and close:** It opens on a blank ticket, her name written at the top. It closes with every ticket filed — "done" — and a line to write to her.

**What it stands on:** the prep-list quote; "trim, whey, stems and bones"; the menu written last; the waste figure; Sunday Pot (cooking from what's left over is prep-list thinking taken to its logical end); the apprentices; Callum's quote.

**What it needs from her:** Priya's photos, credited; her own larder photos. Like the jar, it does not depend on Olu's drawings at all.

**Why it's hers:** the question is Callum's own sentence about her, not a line written for the page, and the ethos "the menu is the last thing written" is a detail nobody else's site would have reason to hold.

## 3. The zine, kept on folded sheets

**The question:** What's worth writing down before it's gone?

**The form** — a zine's real sections, the shape of *Brine* itself:

| Chapter | Role |
|---|---|
| Cover | Opening — her name, the question, what she started from (Katz, her grandmother) |
| Welcome | Who — who's asking, in her voice |
| Feature | Where from — Brassica, the Corn Exchange, Fjaerland, Saltmarsh, told as a profile piece |
| How-to | How — trim, whey, stems, bones, her method as a recipe spread |
| Noted | What — the larder, Sunday Pot, the apprentices, *Brine*'s own four issues |
| By the numbers | What came of it — the waste cut, the apprentices taken on |
| A letter | What others say — Callum's line |
| Back page | Close — where to find her, how to book or apply |

**The object:** a folded sheet — five to twelve of them, each a single page that folds and unfolds, carrying real photos or text on its panels.

**What it becomes, in three chapters:**
- *Feature:* the sheets unfold end to end into a long folded strip, a timeline of places.
- *How-to:* a sheet folds, origami-like, step by step, into a diagram of her method.
- *Noted:* the sheets stand upright in a row like zine issues on a shelf, each face a real photo or figure.

**Opening and close:** It opens on a single folded sheet, blank but for a date. It closes with all the sheets standing as a shelf of issues, and a line to write to her.

**What it stands on:** *Brine*, four issues, 2022–2025; what she learned from (Katz, her grandmother); Callum's quote; Sunday Pot; the apprentices.

**What it needs from her:** this is the one that most wants Olu's drawings — they're *Brine*'s own look. It can run on her own photos and typeset text alone if Olu says no, but it will read thinner without them, and that permission is still outstanding.

**Why it's hers:** *Brine* is a thing she already makes, on her own four-issue schedule, not a form borrowed to describe her.

## Which I'd choose

**Concept 1, the ferment log kept in jars.** It's the one every fact she gave leans toward: the jar wall is already the first thing visitors to her kitchen ask about, Fjaerland was jar-washing and labelling, *Brine* is about what goes in them, and the log's own fields — started, checked, ready — give the page a beginning, a middle and an end without having to invent one. It also doesn't depend on Olu's still-open permission.

Concept 2 is close behind and shares that last advantage — if the emblem caution on the jar concerns you, it's the stronger fallback, built on the same two documents you actually named. Concept 3 is the one I'd drop unless Olu says yes soon; it leans on the one asset still outstanding.
