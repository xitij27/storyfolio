# Concepts — Ines Okafor

## A. The Ferment Log

**The question.** How long does it take to turn waste into something worth eating?

**The form** — the ferment log, in the order a jar actually moves through it:
1. *Started* — The opening: her name, the question, and what she began with (her grandmother, Katz's two books, the stage at Fjaerland where she first washed and labelled jars).
2. *Salted* — Who: her approach — prep and plate waste only, bones go to stock; the discipline a ferment and a kitchen both run on.
3. *Checked, day by day* — Where from / How: her path charted as check-ins — Brassica (larder, garnish, sauce), The Corn Exchange Kitchen (the pass, the supplier list), Fjaerland (the stage), Saltmarsh (acting head chef March 2022, Head Chef September 2022).
4. *What's on the shelf* — What: the larder itself — 40+ ferments on rotation, the jar wall, the menu written last each week, the apprentices on the line.
5. *Tasted* — What others say: Callum's line, as a tasting note pinned to a jar.
6. *Ready* — What came of it, and the close: the waste figure (14 kg to 5 kg a week, the whole kitchen, weighed over a month in 2023), two apprentices a year since 2023, Sunday Pot, Brine. The jars stand whole again. How to apply, write in, or book a workshop.

**The object** — a wall of ten jars, simple cylinders with a paper label on each face. Stood together at rest, they are the jar wall from Priya's photographs.

**What it becomes:**
- *Started*: one jar, label blank, the question written onto it as the page opens.
- *Checked, day by day*: the ten jars line up into a dated strip — each a kitchen, each a year.
- *What's on the shelf*: the jars turn to face forward, their labels becoming the real photographs — the larder, the wall itself.

**The opening and the close.** Opens on one unlabelled jar. Closes on the full wall, ten jars strong, with a line to get in touch.

**What it stands on.** The ferment log's own fields (confirmed by Ines), the job dates and figures as confirmed, Callum's quote (cleared), the Priya Nair photographs (cleared, with credit), her own phone photos, the Fjaerland stage as a stage and not a research post.

**What it needs from her.** Nothing uncleared — it does not use Olu's drawings. Could use one photo of Ines herself if she wants her own face on the site; that's hers to decide, not Olu's or a colleague's.

**Why it's hers.** The ferment log's exact fields, the Fjaerland stage, the grandmother, the specific waste figures and Callum's line belong to no one else. It could not sit on another chef's site unchanged.

## B. The Prep List

**The question.** What has to be ready before anyone sits down?

**The form** — the prep list, read as the stations she has actually worked, ticked off one by one:
1. *Larder and garnish* (Opening) — Brassica, where she started.
2. *Then sauce* (Where from) — Brassica, continued.
3. *The pass* (How) — The Corn Exchange Kitchen: a 90-cover service, the supplier list brought from 31 to 12.
4. *The stage* (Where from) — Fjaerland: washing and labelling jars, two batches of garum.
5. *The larder, again* (What) — Saltmarsh: the jar wall, 40+ ferments, the menu written last.
6. *What came of it* — the waste figure, the apprentices, Callum's line.
7. *Still open* (The close) — the one ticket that never comes down, because the larder is never finished. How to reach her.

**The object** — a kitchen ticket rail: eight chits clipped in a row, each a small card that can flip to show a photograph or a figure on its back.

**What it becomes:**
- *Opening*: one blank chit, clipped on; the question is written onto it in order ticket ink.
- *Where from*: chits multiply along the rail, each stamped with a kitchen and its years.
- *What*: chits flip to show the jar wall and larder photographs on their backs.

**The opening and the close.** Opens on an empty rail with one chit. Closes on the rail full, the last chit left open rather than taken down.

**What it stands on.** The same job history and figures as A, read as stations rather than ferments.

**What it needs from her.** The same photographs as A. Nothing uncleared.

**Why it's hers.** Her own run of stations — larder and garnish, then sauce, then the pass, then the larder again — reads like a real prep list only because it is one.

## C. The Workshop Running Order

**The question.** What can you learn in one Monday morning?

**The form** — a workshop's running order: Welcome, What we're making, Why it works, Hands-on, Taste, Questions, Take home.

**The object** — a tasting flight of nine spoons laid in a row, each able to hold a photograph, a figure or a line of text in its bowl.

**What it becomes:**
- *Welcome*: the spoons lie empty; the question appears along the handles.
- *What we're making / Why it works*: each spoon fills with a stop on her path — the stage, Brassica, the Corn Exchange pass, the Saltmarsh larder.
- *Taste*: spoons show the jar wall and larder photographs, and the waste figure.

**The opening and the close.** Opens on nine empty spoons. Closes on the flight complete, with how to book a workshop, apply as an apprentice, or write in.

**What it stands on.** The same facts as A and B, told through the one thing people already ask her about by name: the workshop.

**What it needs from her.** The same photographs. Nothing uncleared.

**Why it's hers.** She named this workshop herself, unprompted, as one of the four things people ask her about.

---

## Decision

Ines picked **A, the ferment log.**

## Which I would choose

**A, the ferment log.** It is the form she named first and most exactly — she gave its fields unprompted, in order. It carries her strongest material (the waste figures, the jar wall, Callum's line) without leaning on anything still uncleared: Olu's drawings for Brine can be left out entirely and nothing about the concept weakens. B and C are both sound but tell the same path through a thinner form — a rail of tickets or a flight of spoons says less about what she actually spends her time on, which by her own account is what happens to a jar between being started and being ready.
