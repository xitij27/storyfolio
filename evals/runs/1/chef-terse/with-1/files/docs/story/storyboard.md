# Storyboard — Ines Okafor, "The Ferment Log"

Object: a wall of jars (nine or ten, grown through the page). Question: **How long does it take to turn waste into something worth eating?**

## A fixed bar, present throughout

So a visitor who never scrolls still gets the name, what she does, and a way in: "Ines Okafor — Head Chef, Saltmarsh, Leeds. Fermentation, whole-vegetable cooking, apprentices." A rail jumps to any chapter. One link, "Get in touch," skips straight to the close.

## Someone with thirty seconds

The fixed bar carries the name and what she does. The first screen (beat 1.1) carries the question. "One proof" is the jar wall photograph itself, held in the bar as a small thumbnail once the visitor has scrolled past chapter 4 — before that, the bar's text line stands in for it. The rail jumps to any chapter; "Get in touch" skips to 6.2.

## Without the scene

| Where | What the visitor gets |
|---|---|
| On a phone | The same six chapters, stacked vertically. The scroll-jacked strip in chapter 3 becomes a plain vertical list of four stops. Jars render as flat illustrated rows, not a 3D wall. All text and photographs are full width. |
| With reduced motion | Each chapter appears already settled into its resting arrangement — no jars animating into place. Labels and photographs are present on load, not revealed by motion. |
| With no WebGL | The 3D jars are replaced by flat drawings of the same jars (or the real photographs themselves) in the same arrangements. All text, photographs and captions remain. |
| With no JavaScript | One plain HTML page: the six chapters as headed sections, top to bottom, every caption, photograph and credit present in reading order. A text list of links stands in for the rail. |

## The look and the type

**Larder** (the one I'd start with) — ground: warm unbleached linen; ink: near-black brown, the colour of reduced vinegar; one accent: a deep amber-gold, the colour of garum; the jars render as lightly textured glass with kraft-paper labels.

**Pantry at night** — ground: dark slate, the colour of the inside of the koji cupboard; ink: warm cream; the same amber accent; jars glow softly from within, labels lit as if by a single bulb.

Two typefaces: a plain, humane face for reading text, and a second, hand-stencilled face — like a marker on a kitchen label — for anything printed on a jar.
