## Someone with thirty seconds

The first screen (1.1–1.2) carries the name, the role, and the question. A rail on the side jumps to any chapter by name, plus two shortcuts: "Proof" (jumps straight to 5.2, the live map) and "Contact" (jumps straight to 7.2). Someone who never scrolls past screen one still gets the name and role; one tap on "Proof" gets them the one checkable piece of work; one tap on "Contact" gets them how to reach him. The CV link and the live map URL work as plain links everywhere, not only inside the animated scene.

## Without the scene

| Where | What the visitor gets |
|---|---|
| On a phone | Same seven chapters, same captions, same order. The camera is locked — no drag-to-orbit, since that fights scrolling on touch — but scroll still drives every beat transition. Chapters 4 and 5 render fewer blocks on screen at once to hold frame rate. Captions stack below the scene. A block's face is read by tapping it, not hovering. |
| With reduced motion | No camera movement, no blocks sliding or stacking. Each beat appears already in its resting state and cross-fades in on scroll. No easing, no parallax. |
| With no WebGL | The 3D is replaced by a flat, static graphic of the same abstracted grid (an SVG skyline), one version per chapter where the arrangement changes. All text and captions are unchanged and in the same order. |
| With no JavaScript | One plain HTML page. Every chapter's text runs top to bottom in the same order as the script. No animation, no scroll effects. Email, CV, the live map, and LinkedIn are ordinary links that work. |

## The look and the type

**Ground:** near-black, #0B0D10.
**Ink:** warm off-white, #F3EFE8.
**Accent:** one terracotta/ochre, #C9643B — used only for what's live and checkable: the Lisbon Rent Map block, links, the question mark, the contact buttons. Nowhere else, so it keeps its meaning.
**Surface of the pieces:** matte dark resin with a thin backlit edge in the accent colour — like an architectural model standing on a lightbox. Chosen over flat blue-and-white azulejo tiling, which is the first thing anyone reaches for on a Lisbon site and reads as a postcard, not as him.

**Running text and headers:** a geometric grotesk sans, slightly condensed, no quirks — reads like it belongs on a dashboard.
**What's printed on the blocks' faces:** a monospace — names, dates, tool names, numbers — because that's the register his own tools actually output in.
