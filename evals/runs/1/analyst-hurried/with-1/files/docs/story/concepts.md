# Concepts — Rafael Costa

Rafael asked me to decide everything. Three concepts below, differing in form and object, not colour. "The Map" is chosen; reasoning at the bottom.

---

## A. The Lineage

**The question:** What does it take before a number is trusted enough to act on?

**The form:** a dbt project's own lineage — Raw → Staging → Models → Exposures → Docs. Chapters: Raw (opening), Staging (how he works), Models (the work), Exposures (what came of it + what others say, since a dbt "exposure" literally is who relies on a model — Beatriz Lima's quote and the 200+ Meridian staff both are exposures), Docs (close, the whole DAG lit up).

**The object:** 8 rectangular block-nodes, one per employer/project (Atlantico, DataTugas, Helix, Meridian, FraudShield, AutoPilot, Lisbon Rent Map, the hackathon win), each a flat slab with a header strip and text rows, like a database table card. At rest they sit disconnected. They line up into a pipeline, fan into a grid of project tiles, and close as a single connected graph.

**What it becomes:** Staging — blocks stack into layers (SQL/Python at the base, dbt/Statistics mid, Tableau/Power BI on top), showing his actual toolchain. Models — blocks fan into a wall, each face carrying one project's real text. Docs — blocks reconnect into one lit graph, nothing left stray.

**Stands on:** skills list, job list, project list, dates — all confirmed.
**Needs from him:** a yes on naming Helix/Meridian and quoting Beatriz Lima.
**Why it's his:** dbt is a skill he actually listed, not a device I imported.

---

## B. The Ledger

**The question:** What does a monthly number actually tell you?

**The form:** a metrics report-out — Period, Headline numbers, Drivers, Flags, Actions, Next period.

**The object:** a stack of flat report sheets, one per role/project, that fan like a hand of cards, stand as a bar-chart row of KPI cards, and flip to show outcomes.

**What it becomes:** fans into a timeline, stands up into a KPI wall, flips to a one-sheet summary at the close.

**Stands on:** the figures in the fact sheet — several of which are disputed between his resume and his LinkedIn export, so this concept leans hardest on exactly the numbers that are least settled.
**Needs from him:** the disputed figures resolved before any of it can be built honestly.
**Why it's weaker:** a report-out and a stack of KPI cards belong to almost any analyst. It fails the swap test — it isn't distinctly his, and it's the concept most exposed by the numbers he hasn't confirmed.

---

## C. The Map — chosen

**The question:** What does it take before a number is trusted enough to act on?

**The form:** the actual shipping ritual behind a public data product — the shape of building something like Lisbon Rent Map, start to finish. Chapters: The Question (opening), The Analyst (who), The Data (where from), The Model (how), The Map (what), The Readers (what came of it / what others say), The Next Release (close).

**The object:** 8 simple extruded blocks — abstracted city blocks, not a literal accurate map — one per employer/project (Atlantico, DataTugas, Helix, Meridian, FraudShield, AutoPilot, Lisbon Rent Map, the hackathon). At rest, dark and unlit, scattered on flat ground. In "The Data" they line up along a single timeline, each one's height set by actual tenure length in months — a real number, not a decoration. In "The Model" they lift and stack into toolchain layers (same device as concept A, reused here because it's honest, not because it's free). In "The Map" they resolve into a loose grid — an abstracted skyline, explicitly not-to-scale — each block now a readable tile; the Lisbon Rent Map block is lit and links out to the live site.

**What it becomes:**
- The Data: a skyline-timeline, block height = real tenure in months.
- The Model: toolchain layers, SQL/Python at the base, dbt/Statistics mid-layer, Tableau/Power BI as the visible "roof."
- The Map: the full abstracted grid, each tile tappable, the one live, checkable project lit brightest.

**The opening and close:** opens on flat, dark, unlabelled ground — no block lit yet, because nothing has been measured. Nothing biographical is claimed here; it's true of the craft before it's true of him. Closes with every block lit and resolved into the grid, the question answered in his own words — "I make dashboards people actually use" — and a link to the one piece of proof anyone can click right now: the live rent map.

**Stands on:** job history and dates (confirmed), skills (confirmed), project titles and descriptions as given (confirmed), the live rentmap.example URL (confirmed).

**Needs from him:** nothing to build the structure — only to confirm what gets said on top of it (see "waiting on you," below).

**Why it's his:** the object is literally the shape of the one project of his anyone outside his employers can visit and check today. It can't be swapped onto a colleague's site unless that colleague also built and shipped a real public map. It isn't the field-of-dots-into-a-chart emblem every data person's site reaches for.
