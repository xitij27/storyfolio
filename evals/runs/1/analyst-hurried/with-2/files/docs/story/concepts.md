# Concepts — Rafael Costa

Three concepts, differing in form and object. Chosen without the person's answers (he said: decide everything). Built only on facts that needed no confirming — see `facts.md` for what's held back.

---

## Concept A — The Pipeline (recommended)

**The question:** "What turns raw numbers into something people actually trust?"

**The form:** a dbt-style data pipeline — the exact shape of one of his own listed tools, run start to end.

| Chapter | Role |
|---|---|
| Sources | Opening + Who |
| Staging | Where from |
| Intermediate | How |
| Marts | What |
| Tests | What came of it + What others say |
| Exposures | The close |

**The object:** nine identical small blocks, like dbt model nodes, joined by directional edges. At rest they sit as a layered lineage: raw at the bottom, exposed at the top — recognisable as a pipeline diagram standing still.

**What it becomes, in three chapters:**
- *Staging* — the blocks line up left to right as a timeline, one per employer or school, each face printed with its name and years (Atlantico Telecom, Helix Genomics Institute, Meridian Bank, DataTugas, Universidade do Douro).
- *Marts* — the blocks cluster into three larger shapes, one per side project (FraudShield, AutoPilot, Lisbon Rent Map), each face carrying the project's name and its own one-line description.
- *Exposures* — every block reassembles into the full lineage, whole again, and the final node is the contact point.

**The opening and the close:** the opening shows scattered, unconnected points of raw data being pulled together by the scroll into the first clean source blocks — the daily shape of the job (messy in, trusted out), not a biographical claim. The close resolves to one exposed node and a way to reach him.

**What it stands on:** his name, headline and summary line; employer/school names, titles, one-line project descriptions; skill list; award name; the shape of the career timeline (dates that do not conflict).

**What it needs from him:** the contested figures confirmed or dropped, a real contact address, a yes on using Beatriz Lima's quote by name, and whatever he can dig up to put on a few of the faces later (even a rough screenshot).

**Why it's his:** the lineage — raw feeds becoming trusted, exposed tables — is the literal shape of dbt, a tool he named, and mirrors his own sentence about himself ("dashboards people actually use") more exactly than a generic chart ever could.

## Concept B — The Dashboard

**The question:** "What makes a dashboard worth opening twice?"

**The form:** a live analytics dashboard, read top to bottom.

| Chapter | Role |
|---|---|
| Header & filters | Opening + Who |
| Connected sources | Where from |
| Query & transform | How |
| KPI row | What |
| Drill-down | What came of it |
| Annotations | What others say |
| Export | The close |

**The object:** eight tiles, like dashboard KPI cards, at rest forming a grid.

**What it becomes:** in *Connected sources*, the tiles tip onto their edges to read like data-source chips (his employers); in *KPI row*, they flatten into a single row, one per project; in *Export*, they fold down into one card.

**What it stands on / needs:** the same facts as Concept A.

**Why I would not choose it:** its whole payoff is numbers on tiles, and the only numbers in the material are the ones in dispute (200+, 35%, 40/60%, 5,000/3,000). The form asks for exactly what we don't yet have confirmed. A card grid at rest also reads closer to an ordinary "project cards" page than the pipeline does — it is further from passing its own swap test.

## Concept C — The Query

**The question:** "What do you get when you run the right query?"

**The form:** a SQL query, read top to bottom: FROM, WHERE, GROUP BY, HAVING, ORDER BY, SELECT.

**The object:** slab-like clause blocks stacking as the query is "typed," resolving at the end into a result table.

**Why I would not choose it:** it is clever, but it is wordplay on his job title more than a document he actually hands someone — the skill is explicit that a form's voice should carry no jokes. Mapping GROUP BY to "skills" and HAVING to "outcomes" takes forcing. Concept A gets the same technical, trade-accurate feeling without the pun.

---

## Which I'd choose, and why

**Concept A, the Pipeline.** It is the only one of the three built from a form that is distinctively his trade's (not every analyst's), it does not depend on the figures currently in dispute to make its point, and its opening metaphor — scattered data becoming trusted — needs no fact I don't have.
