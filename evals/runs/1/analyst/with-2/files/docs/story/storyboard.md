# Storyboard — Rafael Costa, "The Dashboard"

Concept B, decided. The question the page answers: **"What makes a dashboard something people actually open?"**

The object: eight tiles, the real shape of a dashboard panel. At rest they sit as a loose grid, grey and unlit, like a dashboard that hasn't loaded yet. One tile per real, confirmed thing: Atlantico Telecom, DataTugas, Helix Genomics, FraudShield, Lisbon Rent Map, the Health Data Hackathon, Meridian Bank, AutoPilot. At the close, all eight become one.

## Someone with thirty seconds

The first screen (beat 1.1–1.2) carries his name, his role and tagline, and one proof — the Meridian tile resolving to "200+ staff" before any scrolling is needed. A nav rail down the side jumps straight to any chapter. A single "get in touch" link in the rail skips straight to the close, with his email and CV, without reading anything in between.

## Without the scene

| Where | What the visitor gets |
|---|---|
| On a phone | The 8 tiles stack into a single column instead of a grid or row. The date-range travel becomes a vertical list, ordered the same way. Tap replaces hover throughout. Chapters change by a straight cut between settled states rather than a camera move, to keep it light. The filter chips wrap onto their own line. |
| With reduced motion | No camera movement and no in-between animation. Each chapter simply shows its own settled state (grid, row, wall, grid-of-numbers, pinned note, merged tile) as the visitor scrolls past it, with a plain fade. Nothing is removed — only the motion between states. |
| With no WebGL | The 3D canvas doesn't render. Each chapter instead shows the same beat as a flat, static layout — styled boxes in CSS/SVG, same numbers, same text, same tap-to-expand panels. No fact or figure is lost, only the sense of a physical object. |
| With no JavaScript | A single plain HTML page, all chapters in document order, all captions and panel text present as ordinary text (not hidden behind taps). Tiles render as static cards. The nav rail becomes plain anchor links. The CV is a direct download link; contact is a plain `mailto:` link. |

## The look and the type

Three looks, named from the object's own material — a dashboard panel:

1. **Night shift** — ground: near-black graphite. Ink: off-white. Accent: a single warm amber, the colour of a dashboard's "live" indicator. Tile surface: dark glass, a faint grid-line texture, like a BI tool in dark mode.
2. **Morning standup** — ground: warm off-white. Ink: near-black. Accent: a cobalt blue. Tile surface: light card, soft shadow, thin hairline borders — a clean light-mode dashboard.
3. **Printout** — ground: pale graph-paper grey-blue. Ink: dark navy. Accent: a red pen-mark red, for flags and annotations. Tile surface: paper-white with a faint dot grid — closer to a printed weekly report than a screen.

He picks by scrolling through all three once the site is built.

Two typefaces: a humanist sans for all running text and captions, and a monospace with tabular figures — the kind a dashboard actually sets its numbers in — for everything printed on a tile's face (the years, the KPIs, the tool names).
