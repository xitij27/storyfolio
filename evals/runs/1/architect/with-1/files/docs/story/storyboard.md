## The look and the type

The object's material is structural timber — a post, a beam, a joint — so the look should read as workshop and drawing board, not screen-gloss. Three candidates; she picks once there's something to scroll through.

**A — Drawing board.** Ground: warm cream, the tone of tracing paper. Ink: soft near-black graphite. Accent: a single terracotta-red, an architect's pencil colour. Pieces: pale, visible-grain timber with fine linework etched onto their faces, as if drawn on rather than photographed.

**B — Night site.** Ground: charcoal near-black, a site at dusk. Ink: warm off-white. Accent: work-light amber. Pieces: dark-stained timber, grain picked out by the amber light, as if lit by a floodlight on scaffolding.

**C — Lime and linen.** Ground: pale limewash grey, the colour of breathable render — a nod to retrofit. Ink: deep slate-blue. Accent: a muted moss green, echoing Calder Court's winter gardens. Pieces: raw, untreated spruce, pencil-thin annotations.

Two typefaces throughout: a plain humanist sans for all body text and captions; and, for whatever is lettered directly onto the timber's faces (labels, the title block, her name on the ridge beam at the close) a technical, drawn lettering face — the kind used to title a hand-drawn sheet, not a screen UI font.
