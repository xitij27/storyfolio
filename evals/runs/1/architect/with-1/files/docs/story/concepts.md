# Concepts

Trade forms considered, that Marta named or that an architect works from: the drawing set, the RIBA Plan of Work, the snag list, the condition survey, the specification. Three concepts below use the three she named herself: the drawing set, the RIBA Plan of Work, the snag list.

The question, common to all three, drawn from her own words ("make an old building worth keeping") and tested against the fact sheet — it claims nothing beyond it:

**"What makes a building worth keeping?"**

---

## Concept 1 — The Drawing Set

**The question:** What makes a building worth keeping?

**The form — the drawing set, in her own stated order:**
1. Location plan — *the opening*: her name, the question, where she sits (Glasgow, Associate, Field & Rowe)
2. Plans — *who*: her own words on retrofit, in first person
3. Sections — *how*: project architect, technical design to handover — not "designer"
4. Elevations — *where from*: Gdansk, Clyde, her father's workshop, the book of timber joints
5. Details — *what*: the work itself — Calder Court's wall build-up, Kirk Street's section, the cabin's joints, the school's structure
6. Schedules — *what came of it*: the real numbers, each labelled for what it is — 96 flats, 68% modelled (not yet measured), 6 flats from a hall, shortlisted 2024, built 2021–22
7. The close — the answer, and how to reach her

**The object:** six to eight drawing sheets, true to an architect's actual sheet proportion, edge-on. At rest they sit stacked, like a set on a plan chest.

**What it becomes:**
- *Elevations* — the sheets stand up and line out side by side, like a building's elevations.
- *Details* — one sheet turns to face the visitor and fills with a real detail drawing (the 1:5 wall build-up, if the practice agrees; her own cabin joints if not).
- *Schedules* — the sheets lie flat and tile into a grid, like a schedule, each cell a real figure.

**Opening and close:** the sheets begin stacked and closed, title blocks hidden. At the close they restack, title block forward, her name on it.

**What it stands on:** the stated drawing-set order; Calder Court and Kirk Street as described; the cabin; the school boards; the figures as qualified in the fact sheet.

**What it needs from her:** the practice's yes for the wall build-up detail and the Tom Reid photographs, or confirmation to proceed on cabin and school material alone.

**Why it's hers:** the sheet order is the one she named, in that order, and the details chapter holds the actual drawing she did, not a generic blueprint.

## Concept 2 — The RIBA Plan of Work (recommended)

**The question:** What makes a building worth keeping?

**The form — the RIBA Plan of Work, stages 0 to 7, grouped into six screens:**
1. *0–1, Strategic Definition & Briefing* — **the opening**: her name, the question
2. *2, Concept Design* — **where from**: told as where the idea comes from, and whose it usually is — a partner's, a brief, a competition call. Her own origin sits here instead: her father's workshop, the book of Swiss timber joints bought at nineteen, Gdansk
3. *3–4, Spatial Coordination & Technical Design* — **how**: this is where she comes in. The detailing, the wall build-up, the structural scheme and sections she did for the school boards
4. *5, Manufacturing & Construction* — **what**: the work built — the cabin frame going up, the site at Calder Court and Kirk Street
5. *6, Handover* — **what came of it**: 96 flats, 6 flats, 28 m², shortlisted 2024, each figure labelled for what it is
6. *7, In Use* — **the close**: Calder Court's monitoring starts this winter; she still teaches one day a week; the invitation — an old house, a small plot, a school needing a critic

**The object:** eight simple timber-frame member cross-sections (a post, a beam, a joint), standing in a row like a frame mid-erection — the shape of the cabin going up.

**What it becomes:**
- *Concept Design* — the members lie scattered and flat, as raw timber before assembly, while the origin story plays.
- *Technical Design* — they lock together at a single joint; the camera moves into that joint, which carries the real detail (wall build-up, or the school's structural section).
- *Manufacturing & Construction* — they rise upright one by one, echoing the cabin time-lapse, until the frame stands.

**Opening and close:** opens as raw, unconnected timber. Closes as a complete standing frame, lit, her name across the ridge beam.

**What it stands on:** the RIBA stages exactly as she named them; her explicit account of where her own part starts (stage 3/4, not stage 2); the cabin time-lapse; the figures as qualified.

**What it needs from her:** the school's structural section drawing (hers to show, per her answer); the cabin time-lapse; the practice's yes for the wall build-up, with a cabin joint as fallback if that's not granted in time.

**Why it's hers:** the frame assembling from raw timber to standing structure is the literal shape of the cabin she built, and starting her own story at stage 3 instead of stage 2 says, structurally, what she said in words — the concept isn't always hers, the execution is.

## Concept 3 — The Snag List

**The question:** What makes a building worth keeping?

**The form — the snag list, as she described it: a walk-round, raised, checked, closed:**
1. *Scope* — **the opening**: her name, the question, what's being walked
2. *Walk-round* — **who / where from**: who's doing the checking, and what trained her eye — Gdansk, Clyde, her father's workshop
3. *Items raised* — **how**: what she actually looks for, project by project
4. *Items closed* — **what / what came of it**: each project as a snag closed — before and after, the figures attached
5. *Re-inspection* — **the close**: this winter's monitoring at Calder Court as an open item still being checked; the invitation to reach her

**The object:** nine or ten snag tags, hung loosely, red and open at rest.

**What it becomes:**
- *Items raised* — tags spread out, each showing a real photograph or drawing on its face when turned.
- *Items closed* — tags flip to green, one at a time, project by project.
- *Re-inspection* — one tag (Calder Court) stays half-turned, neither fully open nor closed, for the monitoring that hasn't happened yet.

**Opening and close:** opens as a loose hanging cluster, mostly red. Closes mostly green, in a tidy row, with the one open tag honestly left open.

**What it stands on:** the snag list as she named it; the qualified figures; the still-open monitoring at Calder Court.

**What it needs from her:** confirmation that "still open" (for the monitoring) reads as honest rather than unfinished; the same image permissions as the other two concepts.

**Why it's hers:** it is the only one of the three built around an admission — one tag that doesn't close — which matches a retrofit architect's actual caution about a modelled figure, not a chef's or a developer's.

---

## Which I would choose

**Concept 2, the RIBA Plan of Work.** It is the only one of the three that uses its structure to make the honesty, not just hold it: starting her chapter at stage 3 instead of stage 2 says "the idea isn't always mine" without a line of copy having to say it, and the object — a timber frame going from scattered members to a standing structure — is the literal shape of the one thing in the material that is entirely, unambiguously hers: the cabin. It also survives the swap test best. A colleague who claims concept design on their own projects could not use a form whose second chapter is about where ideas come from *instead of* their own concept work.
