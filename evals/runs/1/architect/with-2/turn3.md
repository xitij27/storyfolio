Here's the full script, written to the snag-list concept. It's also in `docs/story/storyboard.md`.

**The idea.** A snag list is the document an architect carries at the end of a job — one item per fault, open until it's closed out. The site repoints it to open the story instead: every building Marta's touched is an item, open until closed. The object is five snag tags on string — Calder Court, Kirk Street, A School That Grows, the Cabin at Kashuby, and her teaching — hanging off the plain outline of an ordinary building at rest.

**The question the page answers:** *What's worth fixing, and how do you know it's fixed?*

## The seven chapters, in order

**1. Opening.** One tag hangs off a bare building outline. On screen, unscrolled: her name, title, the question, and one proof figure (96 flats; 68% less heating demand, modelled). This is the whole thirty-second read — a rail to jump to any chapter, and a link straight to contact, are both visible from here.

**2. Logged by.** The same tag turns to show its back: her standing to say any of this — Associate, ARB since 2023. Caption is her own words, from her profile: *"the most useful thing an architect can do is make an old building worth keeping."*

**3. Location.** All five tags lift off and scatter onto a map: Gdansk twice, Glasgow three times, with her degrees and jobs landing as each tag settles. Two small, non-tag markers sit alongside — a workbench and a book — tappable for what she learned from: her father's workshop, and a book of Swiss timber joints she's had since nineteen.

**4. Item.** The tags regroup and turn face-forward, each showing what it found wrong: a cold 1960s block, an empty hall, a bare plot, a brief for a school that needs room to grow. The teaching tag shows something different — a studio, Tuesdays — since teaching isn't a fault to fix. Caption names what she actually works from: the drawing set in order, the RIBA stages nought to seven, the snag list itself.

**5. Action taken — the main weight of the page.** Each tag unfolds in turn into its own exhibit:
- *Calder Court:* the tag becomes a layered wall section, building up insulation, membrane, cladding. She's named as project architect from technical design to handover — concept was a partner's. The 68% figure is stated as modelled, not measured, monitoring starting this winter.
- *Kirk Street:* unfolds into the section through the hall, becoming six flats.
- *The cabin:* unfolds into the timber frame itself, raised piece by piece from her own 200 photographs and time-lapse — the one piece needing nobody's permission but hers.
- *The school:* unfolds to show only the structure and sections she drew, labelled "with a team of four."
- *Teaching:* stays flat — a line of text, no exhibit.

**6. Status.** The tags fold flat and sort into Closed (Kirk Street, the cabin) and Open (the school, Calder Court). Calder Court's tag doesn't join either pile — it hangs apart with a faint, slow swing, because its figure genuinely hasn't been checked yet.

**7. Close.** All five tags settle into a quiet row. Everything stops moving except Calder Court's tag. Caption: what she wants next — her own practice in two years; until then, an old house, a small plot, or a school needing a guest critic — and how to reach her.

## What a visitor can click

A rail (visible from the first screen) jumps to any chapter by name. In chapter 5, clicking a project's tag jumps straight to its exhibit. Tags are hoverable/tappable throughout for their one-line facts (dates in chapter 3, status in chapter 6). One link from the first screen skips straight to contact.

## The look and type

Three options, named by the object's own material — pick once it's built, by scrolling through each:

- **Site:** concrete grey ground, near-black ink, snag-string orange accent, matte worn kraft tags.
- **Drawing:** cream tracing-paper ground, graphite ink, a single snag-red accent, fine linework on the tags' faces.
- **Timber:** pale ply ground, charcoal ink, builder's-twine orange accent, numbers burned into thin plywood — closest to the cabin's own material.

Two typefaces throughout: a plain humanist sans for all reading text, and a stencilled site-stamp face only for what's printed on the tags.

## On a phone, and without the 3D

| Where | What you get |
|---|---|
| Phone | Same seven chapters, one column, same real photos and captions. Scatter/unfold/sort become shorter and simpler, chapter 5's exhibits play as a plain sequence rather than something you orbit. Rail becomes a bottom sheet. |
| Reduced motion | Pieces jump straight to each beat's settled state. Calder Court's open status is said in words, not shown by swinging. |
| No WebGL | Flat drawn illustrations stand in for every 3D moment, same positions, same text and photos. |
| No JavaScript | One plain HTML page, all seven chapters, every real photo and caption present as ordinary text and images. Nothing in substance is missing. |
