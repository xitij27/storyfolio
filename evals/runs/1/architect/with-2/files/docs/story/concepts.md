# Concepts — Marta Kowalczyk

Three ways to build the page. They differ in form or in object, not in colour. Tested against swap, backdrop, depth, emblem, honesty, and the thirty-second read.

---

## Concept 1 — The wall section

**The question:** What makes an old building worth keeping?

**The form — the RIBA Plan of Work, 0 to 7** (one of the two things she named that she works from):

| Chapter | RIBA stage(s) | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | 0 — Strategic Definition | Her name, the question, the existing building as it is |
| Where from | 1 — Preparation and Briefing | Gdansk, her father's workshop, the book of Swiss joints, Clyde School |
| How | 2–3 — Concept Design, Spatial Coordination | Her way of working: the drawing set's order, the stages, the snag list |
| What | 4 — Technical Design | Calder Court's detailing and 1:5 wall build-up, Kirk Street's section, the cabin's joints |
| What came of it | 5–6 — Manufacture & Construction, Handover | The flats delivered, the shortlisting, the cabin finished, the modelled 68% |
| Close | 7 — Use | What hasn't been proven yet; what she's looking for next; how to reach her |

("What others say" is left out — nothing in the material fills it.)

**The object:** a cross-section through a wall, in 6–7 flat slabs stacked on edge — existing masonry, new insulation, a membrane, a timber batten, cladding, a timber frame member. At rest they sit as one plain, unimproved wall.

**What it becomes:**
- *Opening:* the slabs are one grey, unmarked block — the building before anyone has touched it.
- *What:* the slabs separate and layer, piece by piece, into the actual Calder Court wall build-up at 1:5 — the one drawing she named by name.
- *Close:* the wall reassembles, complete — but one slab stays faintly translucent, unfixed: the heating figure that's modelled, not yet measured. The monitoring starts this winter, and the page says so.

**The opening and the close:** opens on a bare, ordinary wall — nothing proven about it yet. Closes on the same wall, finished, with the one honest gap left visible, and a line to reach her.

**What it stands on:** the 1:5 wall build-up detail (Calder Court), the 68% modelled figure with its caveat, the cabin's timber frame and joints, her father's workshop, the book of Swiss joints, the RIBA stages as her stated working order.

**What it needs from her:** the practice's yes to show the wall build-up detail (or her redrawing of it), and her yes that "concept by a partner, hers from technical design to handover" reads right on Calder Court.

**Why it's hers:** the object is the exact drawing she pointed to when asked what she works from, built from the same material she builds her own cabin in. No colleague doing new-build work has this wall, or this particular unmeasured honesty at the end.

## Concept 2 — The drawing set

**The question:** What does a building need drawn before it's worth trusting?

**The form — the drawing set, in the order she gave it: location, plans, sections, elevations, details, schedules** (the other thing she named):

| Chapter | Drawing type | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Location | Location plan | Her name, the question, where she sits now |
| Plans | Floor plans | Where from: Gdansk, Brandt Lindgren, Clyde School, Field & Rowe, laid out as a site plan of her own path |
| Sections | Sections | How she works, cut open: the stages, the snag list, her part in the school boards |
| Elevations | Elevations | What: Calder Court, Kirk Street, the cabin, stood up and faced |
| Details | Detail drawings | What came of it: the 1:5 build-up, the cabin's joints, the outcomes and figures |
| Schedules | Schedules | Close: what she's looking for, how to reach her |

**The object:** six thin, translucent drawing sheets, stacked and bound at rest like a set pulled from a tube.

**What it becomes:**
- *Plans:* the sheets fan out flat and settle into a map, pinned at Gdansk, Glasgow, the school.
- *Sections:* one sheet tilts up on edge and slices through the stack, showing the layers beneath — her method, laid bare.
- *Details:* one sheet zooms to a small inset, the 1:5 build-up, drawn at full scale while the rest fall out of focus.

**The opening and the close:** opens on the bound set, closed, on a drafting table. Closes on the same set, restacked and tied — finished, but a tube left open beside it, as if there's a next one still to draw.

**What it stands on:** the stated order of the drawing set, the 1:5 detail, the school sections, the cabin's hand-and-CAD drawings.

**What it needs from her:** either the practice's cleared drawings, or her agreement to redraw the relevant sheets herself, schematically, in a form that doesn't depend on the practice's files.

**Why it's hers:** the chapter order is the exact order she said, not a generic "process" page — though of the three, this is the one closest to how any architect might describe their trade.

## Concept 3 — The snag list

**The question:** What's worth fixing, and how do you know it's fixed?

**The form — the snag list**, the document she carries on site at the end of a job, repointed at the start: every old building she's touched treated as an item still open, until it's closed out.

| Chapter | Role |
|---|---|
| Opening | An ordinary, unloved building, one tag hanging off it: Item 1 |
| Logged by | Who: her standing to say this — ARB, Associate, Field & Rowe |
| Location | Where from: Gdansk, Glasgow, her father's workshop, pinned like tags on a map |
| Item | How: each project as a thing found wrong — a cold block, an empty hall, a bare plot, a brief |
| Action taken | What: the technical design, the wall build-up, the cabin's frame, the school's structure and sections |
| Status | What came of it: closed (Kirk Street, occupied), closed (the cabin, lived in), open (the school, shortlisted not built), open (Calder Court's heating figure — modelled, not measured, monitoring starts this winter) |
| Close | What's not closed yet, what she's looking for next, how to reach her |

**The object:** six or seven snag tags — small numbered tabs on string, the kind tied to a fault on site — hanging at rest off the outline of a plain building.

**What it becomes:**
- *Location:* the tags scatter onto a map and resettle at Gdansk, Glasgow, a workshop.
- *Item:* each tag turns to show, on its face, a short line: what was wrong before she touched it.
- *Status:* the tags sort into two piles, closed and open — and one keeps swinging, unclipped, at the very end.

**The opening and the close:** opens on one tag, hanging. Closes on the same tags, mostly gathered into "closed" — except the one still swinging, because the heating figure genuinely hasn't been checked yet. That's not a device; it's true.

**What it stands on:** the "most useful thing an architect can do is make an old building worth keeping" line, the modelled-not-measured figure and the winter monitoring, the shortlisted-not-built school, the cabin as fully closed and fully hers.

**What it needs from her:** her yes that framing the unbuilt school as "open" rather than unfinished business reads right, and isn't read as a knock against a project she's proud of.

**Why it's hers:** a snag list only ends honestly open if the person telling it really hasn't measured the result yet — which is true of her and Calder Court this winter, and wouldn't be true, in the same way, of almost anyone else's site right now.

## Tests

| Test | 1. Wall section | 2. Drawing set | 3. Snag list |
|---|---|---|---|
| Swap | Passes — the object is her named detail, not any retrofit | Weakest — order is hers, but any architect's site could wear it | Passes — the honest-open ending only works because of her exact, unmeasured figure |
| Backdrop | Passes — the wall *is* the content in every chapter | Passes | Passes |
| Depth | Passes | Passes | Passes |
| Emblem | Passes — a wall build-up is not architecture's poster image | Risk — drafting sheets are close to the trade's own cliché (the blueprint) | Passes — a snag tag is a working tool, not a poster image |
| Honesty | Passes, and built on the caveat she gave | Passes | Passes, and strongest here — the open tag is simply true |
| Thirty seconds | Passes | Passes | Passes |

## Which I would choose

**Concept 3, the snag list.** It's the only one of the three where the ending isn't a flourish — it's just what's actually true right now: the heating figure is modelled, not measured, and the monitoring starts this winter. Everything the skill warns against (a mood with no destination, an emblem, a backdrop) is harder to fall into here, because the object is a real thing she ties to a real fault on a real site, not a metaphor built around her.

Concept 1 is close behind, and shares the same honesty about Calder Court — if the wall section's layering feels like a stronger visual carrier than a handful of tags once we're looking at it on screen, it's an easy switch, since both stand on the same facts.

Concept 2 I'd drop: the order is genuinely hers, but a set of drawing sheets is close enough to architecture's own emblem that it risks reading as "a nice way to lay out a portfolio" rather than something only she could have made.
