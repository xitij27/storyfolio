# Concepts — Rafael Costa

The question for all three: **"What makes a dashboard something people actually use?"** — his own self-description, and the one thing that actually runs through Helix (adopted by a lab, stopped double-booking), AutoPilot (six teammates use it weekly), and Lisbon Rent Map (3,000 visitors a month with no mandate to exist at all).

---

## Concept 1 — The analysis memo

**Form:** the document Rafael named himself as what he works from, in its own order.

| Chapter | Role |
|---|---|
| The Question | Opening — his name, the question |
| The Data | Where from — Porto to Lisbon, Economics to Data Science, the three jobs |
| What I Did | How — the memo/dashboard/weekly-report cycle he works in |
| What I Found | What — Helix, AutoPilot, Lisbon Rent Map, shown working |
| (within What I Found) | What came of it — 5h→2h, six teammates, 3,000 visitors/mo |
| A note in the margin | What others say — Beatriz's line |
| What I'd Do | The close — his ask, and how to reach him |

**Object:** eight identical report cards, stacked like a memo on a desk at rest. They fan into a timeline for "where from," align into a labelled flow for "how," enlarge one at a time into exhibits for each project, flatten into number-tiles for outcomes, and one takes Beatriz's line as a handwritten note in its margin. At the close they restack into a single closed memo cover bearing his name and the line "I make dashboards people actually use."

**Opening/close:** the stack sits closed and grey; scrolling lifts and fans it, the top card printing his name and the question. It closes exactly the way a memo does — not on a flourish, but on a recommendation: "What I'd Do," his actual ask (the role he wants), and contact.

**Stands on:** the memo itself (his own words), the Helix figure, AutoPilot's six users, Lisbon Rent Map's traffic, Beatriz's quote.

**Needs from Rafael:** a Lisbon Rent Map screenshot or embed (the one thing fully clear to show); a plain, non-confidential diagram he's willing to redraw from memory to stand in for the Helix dashboard and the AutoPilot report shape, since neither original can be shown.

**Why it's his:** this shape — Question, Data, What I Did, What I Found, What I'd Do — is the actual document he said he works from, not a website's borrowed "Hero/About/Work" skeleton. Nobody else's site ends on a recommendation because nobody else's day runs on this memo.

## Concept 2 — The weekly report that writes itself

**Form:** the weekly report — the document AutoPilot now fills in, and the one Rafael built by hand at Helix before that: Last Week / This Week / What Changed / Who's Affected / What's Next.

| Chapter | Role |
|---|---|
| This week's report, dated today | Opening |
| Prepared by | Who |
| What Changed (read backwards, as a career) | Where from |
| This Week (his working cycle) | How |
| Who's Affected | What — the sequencing core, six teammates, Lisbon's renters |
| Line items | What came of it |
| A reviewer's comment in the margin | What others say |
| What's Next | The close — his ask, undated, because it hasn't happened yet |

**Object:** a bound pad of 8–10 dated sheets, sitting closed like a desk calendar at rest. Sheets flip forward through the chapters — one becomes a change-log of his roles, one a diagram of his weekly cycle, three become the projects, several become number line-items, one carries Beatriz's line as a margin comment. The final sheet is today's, mostly blank under "What's Next."

**Opening/close:** opens on "last week's" closed report; closes on this week's sheet, unfinished — the one entry even his own script can't write for him.

**Stands on:** AutoPilot (it automates exactly this document), the Helix figure, Beatriz's quote, Lisbon Rent Map's traffic.

**Needs from Rafael:** same as Concept 1 — a Lisbon Rent Map shot, and a safe stand-in diagram for the Helix report and AutoPilot's shape.

**Why it's his:** the whole site becomes the one report he's automated himself out of writing by hand — and the joke, gently made, is that the last page is the one entry AutoPilot can't fill in.

## Concept 3 — The dashboard

**Form:** the dashboard itself, as an artifact: Title & Scope / Filters & Source / Tiles / Drill-down / Footer (last updated, owner, purpose). The site presents itself, directly, as a dashboard about Rafael.

| Chapter | Role |
|---|---|
| Title & Scope | Opening |
| Source | Who |
| Time-range filter, dragged 2017→now | Where from |
| One tile, expanded, showing his working cycle | How |
| Three tiles, each enlarging into a project | What |
| KPI tiles | What came of it |
| A "data quality" tile | What others say |
| Footer: last updated, his ask, contact | The close |

**Object:** a grid of 8–10 flat dashboard tiles, sitting idle at rest exactly like a real BI dashboard. Tiles lift out one at a time, rotate, enlarge into each chapter's content, then settle back; at the close the whole grid is lit and whole.

**Stands on:** the "200+ staff" figure, the three projects, Beatriz's quote as a trust/annotation note.

**Needs from Rafael:** same visual gaps as the others, plus a couple of mockup tiles for the "methodology" chapter.

**Why it's his — with a real caveat:** framing the site as "a dashboard about the person who builds dashboards" is a sharp, specific joke that fits him exactly. The caveat: a grid of dashboard tiles sits closer to the field's actual emblem than the other two objects do — it's one step from "the chart made of data points" this process warns against. It also has the least natural beginning-middle-end of the three; a dashboard is a thing you scan, not a thing that unfolds, so chapter 2 ("filters") and chapter 3 ("one tile, expanded") have to work harder to earn their place in a scroll.

---

## Which I'd choose

**Concept 1, the analysis memo.** It's the one Rafael named himself — he didn't say "I work from a dashboard," he said the memo comes first, then the dashboard, then the report. A memo also has a shape no other concept here has: it ends on a recommendation, which is exactly what a job-seeking site needs to end on, without having to invent a "Contact me" section bolted onto the end. And its object — plain report cards — carries whatever's put on its face without fighting the content for attention, which matters a lot here given how little Rafael has to show visually right now.

Concept 2 is the strongest runner-up — the AutoPilot tie-in ("the one entry even his own script can't write") is a genuinely good close — but it leans on a joke landing well in 3D, which is a bigger execution risk than Concept 1's plainer, sturdier shape.

Concept 3 I'd avoid: it's the most literal idea but also the closest to the generic "dashboard/chart" emblem this process exists to get away from, and dashboards don't naturally build toward an ending the way a memo or a report does.
