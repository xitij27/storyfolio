## Concept 1 — The Memo

**The question:** What makes a dashboard something people actually open twice?

**The form — the analysis memo** (his own: the question, the data, what he did, what he found, what he'd do), five chapters stretched to the roles the site needs:

1. *Question* — opening: his name, the site's question
2. *Data* — where from: Porto, Lisbon, the years, the employers — the sources behind him
3. *Method* — how he works: talks to the people who'll use it, ships small, weekly
4. *Finding* — what: the work shown working (Lisbon Rent Map live, AutoPilot in use by six people, FraudShield as a capstone that taught him something), then what came of it (hours saved, staff count, visitors) — plus Beatriz's line as a footnote to the finding
5. *Recommendation* — close: the answer, and his ask — what he'd want a product team to know, and how to reach him

**The object:** a stapled stack of 7 memo pages, onion-skin paper, each carrying a real fragment on its face — a filter sketch, a line of SQL from AutoPilot, the Rent Map screenshot, Beatriz's quote.

**What it becomes:** opening — the pages fan out from under one staple; where-from — the pages lay flat in a line across the years and cities; the work — the pages spread into a grid, each one a project card with its real figure; close — the pages restack into the clipped memo, finished and dated.

**What it stands on:** the "what he works from" line in the fact sheet, and every confirmed figure.

**What it needs from him:** a screenshot of the Lisbon Rent Map (the one thing not under NDA); a line for the final recommendation/ask.

**Why it's his:** he named this document himself, unprompted, as what he actually works from — it isn't a guess at his trade's form.

## Concept 2 — The Weekly Report

**The question:** What's worth doing again every Monday?

**The form — the weekly report**, a ritual, not a one-off document:

1. *Subject line* — opening: his name, the question
2. *Distribution* — who: who this is for
3. *Last week / this week* — where from: Atlantico, Helix, Meridian, as a run of weeks across years, not a list of jobs
4. *What changed* — how he works: the pattern underneath all three — found the report that was costing someone hours, rebuilt it
5. *The numbers* — what came of it: the 60% cut at Helix, 200+ staff at Meridian, six people on AutoPilot, 3,000 visitors a month on Rent Map
6. *Flagged for follow-up* — what others say: Beatriz's line, as the kind of note a report earns when it's working
7. *Sign-off* — close: this week's sheet, dated today, his ask and how to reach him

**The object:** a pad of report sheets, torn off one at a time — 8 to 9 sheets.

**What it becomes:** opening — one sheet fills itself in; where-from — the sheets lay out as a strip across the years, dated; the work — the sheets pin up like a status board, each one a project with its real number; close — the current sheet, dated today, carrying his ask.

**What it stands on:** the weekly report at Helix (cut from 5 hours to 2), AutoPilot (automates his team's weekly report), the dashboards at Meridian and Helix that feed one.

**What it needs from him:** sign-off on the dated, calendar-strip treatment of his career; the Rent Map screenshot; his ask for the close.

**Why it's his:** the weekly report isn't one job of his — it's the thing that shows up, confirmed, in nearly every job and project he has. The site's spine is the thing he actually kept rebuilding.

## Concept 3 — The Dashboard

**The question:** How do you know a dashboard is actually working?

**The form — the dashboard** itself: filters, KPI tiles, a trend, a drill-down, an export.

1. *Filter* — opening: his name, the question, as the one control on an empty dashboard
2. *View as* — who: who's asking, set as a dashboard's audience toggle
3. *Trend* — where from: the years and places, as a single line moving across a timeline tile
4. *Tiles* — how he works, then the work shown working and what came of it: Rent Map, AutoPilot, FraudShield, each its own tile with its real figure
5. *Annotation* — what others say: Beatriz's line, pinned to the Helix tile like a comment on a chart
6. *Export* — close: the answer, and a share/contact action

**The object:** a grid of 9 flat dashboard tiles, rounded rectangles, each carrying a real metric or screenshot on its face.

**What it becomes:** opening — one tile switches on; where-from — the tiles line up into a single trend chart built from his own jobs; the work — the tiles spread into the full grid, each a project; close — the tiles reassemble into one dashboard, live, with an export action as the ask.

**What it stands on:** every job and project in the fact sheet is, literally, a dashboard or feeds one.

**What it needs from him:** real figures for each tile's face (already confirmed) and the Rent Map screenshot; sign-off that a dashboard-of-tiles doesn't read as generic to him.

**Why it's his:** it's the literal medium he works in, not a stand-in for it — but it's the one of the three closest to a trade emblem, which is why it's listed last.

## Which I'd choose

**Concept 2, the Weekly Report.** The memo (Concept 1) is true to his words but is a single document — it tells one analysis, not a career. The dashboard (Concept 3) is the right medium but risks reading as "what any analyst's site looks like," since a tile grid is close to the thing every data person already reaches for. The weekly report is the one pattern that is *confirmed*, repeatedly, across his real history: Helix's report cut from five hours to two, AutoPilot built to automate his own team's report, Meridian's dashboards existing to feed one. It turns "I make dashboards people actually use" into a story with a beginning (an intern doing churn analysis), a middle (cutting report time, automating it away), and an end that lands exactly on the job he's going for — someone who finds the report that's costing a team its Monday, and gets rid of it.
