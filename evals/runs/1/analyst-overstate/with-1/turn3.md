## The plan: The Weekly Report

**The idea:** the one thing confirmed, over and over, in your real history is a report that's costing someone time, and you rebuilding it — Helix's weekly report, AutoPilot automating your own team's, the dashboards at Meridian that exist to feed one. The site is shaped like that report. The question it asks and answers: **what's worth doing again every Monday?**

**The object:** a pad of 8 report sheets — Atlantico, DataTugas, Helix, FraudShield, the hackathon, Lisbon Rent Map, Meridian, AutoPilot — the same 8 pieces throughout, rearranged chapter to chapter, never swapped out.

### The 7 chapters, in order

1. **Subject line** (opening) — one blank sheet types out your name, your tagline, and the one proof figure (Helix's five-hours-to-two), then the question. Nothing to click yet, but a nav rail and a "skip to contact" link are already on screen.
2. **Distribution** (who) — the stack loosens; each sheet gets tagged with who actually reads it: the sequencing core, 200+ bank staff, six teammates, Lisbon renters.
3. **Last week / this week** (where from) — the 8 sheets drop onto a dated line, 2020 to today. Tap a sheet for its title and dates.
4. **What changed** (how you work) — the Helix sheet alone turns over: dense and handwritten on one face ("5 hrs"), clean and typed on the other ("2 hrs"). A toggle flips it.
5. **The numbers** (what, and what came of it) — all 8 sheets spread into a wall, each with its real figure. Tap to expand; the Rent Map sheet links out to the live site.
6. **Flagged for follow-up** (what others say) — Beatriz Lima's line pins itself to the Helix sheet.
7. **Sign-off** (close) — all 8 sheets restack; a fresh sheet lands on top, dated today, with your answer to the question and your ask. Email, CV download, and the Rent Map link sit beside the stack.

Full beat-by-beat detail, including every caption, is in `docs/story/storyboard.md`.

### On a phone, and without the 3D

Same 7 chapters, same order, same captions, always — the 3D is never where the only copy of anything lives. On a phone the wall and timeline flatten into stacked illustrated cards, taps replace hovers, the nav rail becomes a bottom sheet. With reduced motion, every beat shows its settled end-state with no flying sheets. With no WebGL, the same flat illustrations stand in for the canvas. With no JavaScript, it's one plain HTML page in the report's own order — header, who reads it, a dated history, the Helix before/after as a paragraph, a list of all 8 projects with their figures, Beatriz's quote as a blockquote, and a closing paragraph with a mailto: link and your CV.

### Look and type

Leading option, **Printed Monday** — warm paper white, charcoal ink, one stamp-red accent, sheets as faintly ruled paper. Two alternatives in the storyboard: a dark **Night shift** dashboard look, and a navy-and-amber **Ledger** look closer to a bank's own paperwork. Two typefaces throughout: a clean sans for running text, a typewriter-style monospace for anything printed on a sheet's face.
