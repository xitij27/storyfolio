## The idea

Question: **What makes a migration boring on the day?**
Form: your own runbook — preflight, shadow, dual write, cutover, soak, decommission — the exact stages from your resume.
Object: plain stencilled shipping containers. Not a server rack, not a dashboard — containers, because you've spent four years moving a freight company's services while "the trucks kept running," in your own words.

## The scroll, top to bottom

- **Opening** — your name, the question, one closed container alone in an empty yard.
- **Preflight** (Who/Where from) — the container becomes three, stencilled with each job and its years: the library, Lumen Pay (shown only as "card payment systems," nothing more), Kestrel.
- **Shadow** (How) — faint mirror outlines beside the stack; one separate container tips and spills — the 2014 dropped table, the origin of why you care about rollbacks; the runbook, checklist and rollback plan appear as labels, then the two books.
- **Dual write** (What) — two stacks, on-prem and cloud, with crates crossing one at a time toward 310 as you scroll; one container relabels 11→15 (the Postgres upgrade); ferry and the talk appear as their own small containers, each linking out.
- **Cutover** (What came of it) — the stacks snap into one; faces flip to your real numbers: 99.98% (order API, 26 months, two sub-ten-minute internal incidents), 85→22 pages/week ("we," the team of nine), 40 teams on the runbook.
- **Soak** (proof that held) — the stack just sits still for a beat. Nothing moving is the point.
- **Decommission** — the old on-prem containers lift away and fade; your old 2019 site fades out too.
- **Close** — the full set stands whole; the answer to the question, then email, CV, ferry, the talk, the slides.

## Touch, phone, and the fallbacks

A fixed rail jumps to any chapter and always has a "Close" shortcut, so someone with thirty seconds gets your name, the question, one number, and how to reach you without scrolling at all. On a phone, the same beats play from pre-rendered stills advanced by scroll position, tap instead of hover. With reduced motion, beats cut instead of animate. With no WebGL, flat stencilled illustrations stand in, same order, same text. With no JavaScript, it's a plain HTML page — same chapters, same captions, same links, just no pieces.

## Look and type

Three options to pick from once there's something to scroll: **Dockyard overcast** (slate grey, near-white ink, stencil-orange accent, weathered steel), **Night yard** (near-black navy, amber floodlight accent, higher contrast), or **Manifest paper** (warm kraft tone, ink-blue, flatter illustrated containers — lighter and more approachable). Body text in a plain sans; anything stencilled on a container face uses an actual stencil typeface.
