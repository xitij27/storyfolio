I've gone with Concept 1 — **The Job Card** — and written the full script to `docs/story/storyboard.md`. Here's the shape of it:

**The question:** How do you know a fix actually worked?

**The object:** 7 workshop tags — the rectangular card kind tied to a bike with string — each carrying one real piece of your work: the 1984 frame, the wheel's cracked flange, the seized bottom bracket, the stripped paint, the pedal-box bracket, the hand-crank charger, and one left deliberately blank.

**The 7 chapters**, each a section of a real job card:
1. **Ticket** — your name, your year, the question; tags sit as one closed stack.
2. **Service history** — your timeline, with the July 2025 team result marked plainly as before you joined.
3. **Mechanic** — who you are, what you're looking for.
4. **What's wrong** — your diagnostic habit, in your own blog's words (the flange, the seized cup).
5. **The work** — all 7 tags fan out flat; the bracket and charger get pulled forward with their real figures.
6. **The test ride** — each tag flips to show what's actually been tried: the charger's honest four-minutes-for-one-percent, the bike repairs ridden, and the bracket stamped "not yet — November."
7. **Sign-off** — tags re-clip into one stack; the answer to the question, your email, your CV.

It also covers: a 30-second read (name, field, the 18% proof, a jump-to-contact link), what happens on a phone / with reduced motion / with no WebGL / with no JavaScript, three look options (workshop kraft, shop-floor dark, clean service desk — you pick by scrolling once it's built), and a build/later/never list for everything else a site needs (CV download: build; testimonials: never, since none exist; a page per project: later).

Everything I decided without asking is in `docs/story/decided-for-you.md`.

Does this storyboard get a yes, or is there something to change? Once you say yes, this planning version stops here — building it is a separate next step.
