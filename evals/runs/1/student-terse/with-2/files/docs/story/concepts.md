# Concepts — Mina Park

## Concept 1 — The Job Card

**The question:** How do you know a fix actually worked?

**The form** — the job card, the ritual Mina named for the co-op, stretched to also hold her engineering side:
1. Ticket — *Opening*: her name, the question.
2. Service history — *Where from*: a dated line — co-op since 2023, Hallam Racing from Oct 2025 (with the July 2025 12th-of-68 marked clearly as before her time, team context only), the 1984 bike.
3. Mechanic — *Who*: a short line on who she is and how she works.
4. What's wrong — *How*: her diagnostic habit — the rim that wasn't the problem, the cup that seized, the charger that technically worked.
5. The work — *What*: the bracket (CAD + simulation), the charger (built), the bike repairs (done), shown as real pieces.
6. The test ride — *What came of it*: honest outcomes side by side — charger tested (four minutes for one percent), bike repairs tested (ridden), bracket not yet tested (machined in November).
7. Sign-off — *The close*: the answer to the question, and how to reach her.

*("What others say" is left out — there are no testimonials, and none will be invented.)*

**The object** — a small stack of rectangular workshop tags, the kind tied to a bike with string, 7 to 9 of them, each carrying a real photo or CAD image of one piece of her work on its face.

**What it becomes:**
- In *Service history*, the tags string out along a line in date order.
- In *The work*, the tags fan out flat, face up, like parts laid on the garage floor.
- In *The test ride*, each tag flips to show its outcome stamped on the back — tested, or not yet.

**Opening and close:** It opens on a single blank tag, being written on. It closes with the tags clipped back into one stack.

**What it stands on:** the job-card ritual Mina described; the bracket (confirmed CAD + simulation, 18% lighter, machined November, not yet tested); the charger (built, works badly, four minutes for one percent); the bike repairs from the blog; the corrected timing on the July 2025 result.

**What it needs from her:** the bracket CAD images, charger photos, bike-part photos she already has. Nothing from anyone else.

**Why it's hers:** it is the exact ritual she named, stated in her own words, and it is the one thing that holds a bike mechanic's Saturday and an engineering student's November machining run in the same frame — a colleague at either job would recognise it, and it could not sit unchanged on another second-year's site.

## Concept 2 — The Logbook

**The question:** What's worth writing down before you trust a number?

**The form** — the logbook, the other ritual she named, used in the workshop:
1. Entry — *Opening*: date, name, question.
2. Setup — *Where from*: what's on the bench, her timeline.
3. Reading — *How*: the measurements and what they're checked against (the 18% figure's CAD-and-simulation basis).
4. Snag — *What*: where a project went wrong (the charger's four minutes for one percent, the cracked flange).
5. Fix — *What*: what she did about it.
6. Next time — *What came of it*: what's left unresolved, honestly (the bracket's November test).
7. Sign-off — *The close*: answer and contact.

**The object** — loose ruled pages, 6 to 8, each carrying a photo or sketch.

**What it becomes:** pages laid end to end as a timeline; one page opened wide for a single project's detail; pages gathering into a closed, bound logbook at the end.

**What it stands on / needs:** the same material as Concept 1.

**Why it's hers:** it favours her measuring and checking habit over the service framing — a slightly more introspective, numbers-first telling of the same honest-outcomes story.

## Concept 3 — The Inventory

**The question:** Keep, fix, or replace — how do you decide?

**The form** — lifted directly from her own blog post, "An inventory": Laid out, Keep, Fix, Replace, Reassembled.

**The object** — small labelled part-tags laid flat, 8 to 10, one per real part or project (headset, bottom bracket, the pedal-box bracket, the charger's gear train).

**What it becomes:** parts scattered flat, as on the garage floor; sorted into three groups under their keep/fix/replace marks; reassembled into one standing whole at the close.

**What it stands on / needs:** the blog's inventory post and photos; the same project material.

**Why it's hers:** it is word for word a ritual she has already written about, not one described to me secondhand.

## Picked

**Concept 1, the job card.** Mina asked me to go with my recommendation.

## Which I would choose

**Concept 1, the job card.** It's the only one of the three that puts her two worlds — Saturday mechanic and engineering student — through the exact same test, in her own language, and it turns the bracket's unfinished state (not yet tested, machined in November) from a gap into the honest, forward-looking thing the whole page has been asking about. It also gives "what others say" a clean reason to be absent rather than a hole.
