## Chapter by chapter

**1. Identify.** Opens on five cards loose above the fold, backs showing — then they settle into a tidy stack. Top card: "Aoife Brennan — CNM1 · Nursing Informatics Link Nurse, one day a week · Cardiac Step-Down, St Brendan's University Hospital, Dublin." A small corner tag reads "71% → 94% · 9 months." The question types in beneath, letter by letter: *why does the safe way have to be the slow way?*

**2. Situation.** The stack unfolds into a row along a timeline. Four cards, one blank and held back: BSc Nursing 2011–2015 → Staff Nurse, Coronary Care 2015–2019 → CNM1, Cardiac Step-Down 2019–present → MSc Health Informatics (part-time) 2024–2026. Caption: "Eleven years a nurse, six of them in charge of a shift."

**3. Background.** The row closes into a slow-turning ring of what she works from every shift: ISBAR, the early warning score chart, the care plan, the audit cycle. Click any to flip it — ISBAR's back: "The form she led a redesign of. Now used on six wards." Audit cycle's back: "Plan, do, study, act — the shape of the scanning study below."

**4. Assessment — the longest chapter.** The ring opens into a fan.
- *The sheet, before and after* — the old and new handover sheet, blank of patient data, with a toggle between them.
- *Forty hours* — 29% of doses missing a scan → 40 hours observed, 22 nurses interviewed → the three workarounds, named one at a time (wristbands that won't scan after a shower, scanners kept away on a trolley, a printed sheet kept in a pocket). Caption: "None of it was laziness. Each saved a nurse one to four minutes a round."
- *71 to 94* — the fix (scanners moved, wristband stock changed, printed sheets stopped), a number climbing from 71 to 94 as you scroll, with a standing line beneath it: "A ward result: pharmacy, IT, the night staff and me."
- *The rest of it* — preceptor to fourteen nurses; dashboards for the falls and pressure ulcer audits (no outcome claimed — the material holds none).

**5. Recommendation.** The fan gathers back into the stack. Top card gives the answer: "It doesn't — once you move the friction, and not the nurse." The once-blank fifth card turns face-up: "Clinical informatics work, full-time, from 2026." The stack settles where it opened, beside email, CV, and LinkedIn.

## Thirty seconds

The first screen alone carries her name, her role, and one proof (the 71→94 tag). A fixed rail — **I · S · B · A · R** — jumps to any chapter; the **R** tab doubles as the one link straight to contact.

## Without the scene

| Where | What you get |
|---|---|
| Phone | Ring/fan/row become a vertical list with light 2D slide/fade. The rail becomes a sticky top tab bar. Tap replaces click. Numbers still climb — cheap in 2D. |
| Reduced motion | Nothing drifts, spins, or counts up. Every beat appears already finished the moment it's in view. Flips and toggles are an instant swap. |
| No WebGL | Flat HTML/CSS cards, same words and order. Ring→grid, fan→stacked list, row stays a row. Only the depth and turning are gone. |
| No JavaScript | One static page, top to bottom, Identify→Recommendation. What a flip would reveal is just printed under the main line instead. Rail is plain anchor links. Contact is a `mailto:` link and a direct CV download. |

## Look and type

**Ward paper** (my lean): pale warm off-white ground, near-black charcoal ink, a muted clinical teal-green accent, matte card surfaces with a faint printed header rule. Calm, daytime, reads as a working document.

**Night chart** (alternative): deep navy-charcoal ground, warm pale cream ink, amber-gold accent, cards lit faintly from within like a clipboard under a nurses'-station lamp.

Type: one humanist sans for everything the site says to you; one typewriter-leaning face for anything printed on the cards themselves — the sheet, the tool names, the figures — so the site's voice and the document's voice never blur. The real pick happens by scrolling a built version, not now.
