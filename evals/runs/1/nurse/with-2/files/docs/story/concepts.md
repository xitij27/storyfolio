## A. The Handover

**The question.** Why does the safe way have to be the slow way?

**The form.** The ISBAR handover sheet — the form she redesigned, the one every nurse in Ireland is trained on. Its five sections, in order:

| Section | Role it holds |
|---|---|
| Identify | Opening + Who — her name, CNM1 and Nursing Informatics Link Nurse (one day a week), St Brendan's University Hospital, Dublin, and the question |
| Situation | Where from — BSc Nursing 2011–2015, Staff Nurse Coronary Care 2015–2019, CNM1 Cardiac Step-Down 2019–present, part-time MSc Health Informatics 2024–2026 |
| Background | How — the documents and rituals she works from daily: ISBAR itself, the early warning score chart, the care plan, the audit cycle (plan, do, study, act) |
| Assessment | What + What came of it — the handover sheet redesign (led a group of five, now on six wards), the scanning study (29% missed doses, 40 hours observed, 22 nurses interviewed, three workarounds, none laziness), what changed (scanners moved, wristband stock changed, printed sheets stopped), the result (71% to 94% in nine months, named as the ward's, with her part inside it), fourteen nurses precepted, dashboards for falls and pressure ulcers |
| Recommendation | Close — the answer, what she is looking for (clinical informatics work, full-time, from 2026), how to reach her |

No "What others say" chapter: there is no quote or reference in the material to hold it.

**The object.** Five to seven handover cards: flat, rounded-corner, the shape and proportion of the real sheet, standing upright in a row like cards filed in a tray. At rest they read as a short stack of handover sheets — recognisable to any nurse at a glance.

**What it becomes.**
- *Identify* — the cards sit loose and scattered, and draw together into one tidy stack as the visitor scrolls in: the site assembling itself out of the one document she fills in every shift.
- *Situation* — the stack unfolds into a line, one card per job, spaced along the years.
- *Assessment* — the cards fan out face-up like a hand on a table, each face carrying one real piece of work; the scanning study's number climbs from 29% toward 94% as the visitor scrolls past it.
- *Recommendation* — the cards close back into the single stack they opened as.

**The opening and the close.** Opens on the blank ISBAR sheet she actually uses, loose, gathering into one card. Closes the same way: one stack, her answer on its face, beside a way to write to her.

**What it stands on.** Every confirmed line under "Each job," "The title," "What she works from," and "The project behind the numbers" in facts.md.

**What it needs from her.** The two handover sheets, blanked — as real images if she can send clean copies, or redrawn from her description if not.

**Why it is hers.** The handover sheet is the one document in the material she personally redesigned, works from every shift, and can show blank — no colleague's site opens on this particular sheet.

## B. The Chart

**The question.** How much warning does it take before someone acts?

**The form.** The early warning score (NEWS) chart — routine observation through to review:

| Section | Role it holds |
|---|---|
| Observation | Opening + Who — her name, her titles, where she is now |
| Trigger | Where from — the years, the pattern she kept finding: that the safe thing was the slow thing, evidenced by the measured number (29% of doses missing a scan), not a story |
| Escalation | How — the audit cycle and the tools she used to move from a number to a fix |
| Response | What + What came of it — the three workarounds found, what changed, the result climbing to 94%, the dashboards, the handover sheet |
| Review | Close — her answer, what she's looking for, how to reach her |

**The object.** A row of five to nine round observation discs, the size and shape of the score circles on a real chart, laid out evenly — recognisable at rest as a line of score dots, the way a nurse reads a chart at a glance.

**What it becomes.**
- *Observation* — discs sit evenly spaced, calm, undisturbed.
- *Trigger* — discs tip from calm toward alert one at a time as the visitor scrolls, climbing toward 29%.
- *Response* — discs settle back one at a time as each fix lands, tracking 71 toward 94.
- *Review* — the row is calm again, in a line.

**The opening and the close.** Opens on one disc, alone, calm. Closes on the full row, calm, beside a way to reach her.

**What it stands on.** The scanning study's figures, and the line naming the early warning score chart as something she works from daily.

**What it needs from her.** Nothing beyond what is already confirmed.

**Why it is hers.** She named this chart herself as something she reads every shift, and her one provable story already has its shape: a number crossing a threshold, then brought back down.

## C. The Cycle

**The question.** Does the audit change anything, or just the paperwork?

**The form.** The audit cycle — plan, do, study, act — with an opening and a close either side:

| Section | Role it holds |
|---|---|
| Opening | Opening + Who — name, titles, the question |
| Plan | Where from + How — the years, and the tools she plans with: ISBAR, the early warning score chart, the care plan |
| Do | What — the 40 hours observed, 22 nurses interviewed, three workarounds found, the handover sheet redesigned |
| Study | What came of it — the measured result: 71% to 94%, six wards, fourteen nurses precepted, the dashboards |
| Act | What came of it, cont'd — what was made to last: scanners fixed in place, wristband stock changed, the printed sheets stopped |
| Close | Close — the answer, how to reach her |

**The object.** A grid of six to nine small square tiles, the shape of a dashboard screen, standing upright in rows like a bank of monitors at a nurses' station — recognisable at rest as a small wall of screens.

**What it becomes.**
- *Plan* — the tiles are dark, waiting.
- *Do / Study* — tiles light one at a time, each carrying one real figure or finding, building into a lit wall.
- *Act* — the lit tiles arrange into the four letters of the cycle, P-D-S-A, as one diagram.
- *Close* — they settle into a calm, evenly lit grid.

**The opening and the close.** Opens on a single dark tile. Closes on the full lit grid, calm, beside a way to reach her.

**What it stands on.** The scanning study's figures, the dashboards line, and the audit cycle named directly as something she works from.

**What it needs from her.** Nothing required; her own dashboard screenshots, blanked of patient data, would let the tiles carry real images rather than built graphics, if she wants to send them.

**Why it is hers.** Plan-do-study-act is the exact shape of the one project she can prove start to finish, and the tiles are the kind of dashboard she actually builds, not a stock image of one.

## Which I would choose

**A, the handover.** It is built on the one document in the material she both redesigned and can show, blank, as a real artefact — nothing else here has that. Its five sections map onto exactly what the site needs with no join forced, and "Recommendation" closes the page as both the clinical term and the literal ask, without a joke being made of it. B and C are honest and buildable, but lean on a chart and a cycle she reads rather than one she made; A ends on something she built.
