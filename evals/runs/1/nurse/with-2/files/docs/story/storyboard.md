## Someone with thirty seconds

The first screen (1.1, Named) carries all three: her name, what she does (the titles), and one proof of it (the small "71% → 94% · 9 months" tag on the stack's corner).

A rail of five tabs — I · S · B · A · R — sits fixed to one side (top on a phone) and jumps straight to any chapter. The R tab is also the one link that skips straight to the close: on hover or long-press it reads "Recommendation — get in touch."

## The look and the type

Two looks, each read from the object's own material — a printed ward form:

**Ward paper.** Ground: a warm, pale off-white, like paper. Ink: near-black charcoal. One accent: a muted clinical teal-green. The cards' surface: matte, with a faint printed header rule, a soft short shadow beneath the stack. Calm, daytime, legible.

**Night chart.** Ground: a deep navy-charcoal, like a ward dimmed for the night. Ink: a warm pale cream. One accent: a warm amber-gold, the colour of a call light. The cards' surface: softly lit from within, as if under a nurses'-station lamp, with a thin glowing edge.

My lean is Ward Paper as the default — it reads as a working document, not a mood board — with Night Chart as an easy second if she wants the site to open the way the ward feels at 3 a.m. Either way, the real choice happens by scrolling a built version; nothing here commits her yet.

Type: one humanist sans, plain and warm, for every word the site says to the visitor — headings, captions, body. One typewriter-leaning face, the kind of thing that looks typed onto a form, for anything printed on the cards themselves — the sheet, the tool names, the figures. The two should never be mistaken for each other: one is the site talking, the other is the document itself. Exact families are a build decision, not a planning one.
