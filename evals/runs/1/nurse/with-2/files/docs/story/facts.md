## The project behind the numbers: the scanning study

- On her ward, 29% of medication doses were given without scanning both wristband and medicine. — *poster-abstract.md*
- She observed for 40 hours across day and night shifts, and ran short interviews with 22 nurses. — *poster-abstract.md*
- Three workarounds accounted for most missed scans: wristbands that would not scan after a shower, scanners kept on a trolley away from the medication room, and scanning from a printed sheet of barcodes kept in a pocket. — *poster-abstract.md*
- Each workaround saved a nurse between one and four minutes a round; none was laziness. — *poster-abstract.md*
- What changed: scanners were fixed beside the medication room door, the wristband stock was changed, and the printed sheets stopped. Scanning rose to 94% in nine months. — *poster-abstract.md*
- Presented as a poster, Irish Nursing Informatics Conference 2025, single author (A. Brennan, RGN). — *poster-abstract.md*

**Answer:** The result (71% → 94%) was the ward's: pharmacy, IT, night staff and her. Her own part: the forty hours of watching, finding the three workarounds, and getting the scanners moved to the medication room door.
