# Visual criteria calibration, known examples

11 October 2026. No model invocation. Twenty original images were opened
individually, not as a contact sheet. [The inventory](visual-calibration.json)
records their paths, bytes and SHA-256, plus original reports and source
binding records. [The script](visual-calibration.mjs) reproduces the inventory,
not the visual judgements. Nothing in the original studies was edited.

These are known-positive and known-negative examples: the author accepted
the five-beat manual prototype and rejected the seven-beat actor site.
This unblinded sanity check cannot establish general scoring accuracy,
independent reproduction, or an effect caused by a skill change. Review
uses the prospective questions already in the reproduction plan. No new
criterion was tuned against a new actor baseline, which does not exist.

## Boundaries

Accepted paths above are relative to
`evals/runs/visual-recovery-depth-1/evidence/review/`; rejected paths are relative
to `evals/runs/codex-build-completion-1/embedded-completion-1/baseline-1/acceptance/result/`.
The complete paths and original report hashes are in the inventory. The
accepted capture's initial stale Board-description assertion failure remains
in its original report; the later correction and verification are separate
records. This calibration does not erase that chronology or rerun controls.

Factual review and visible evidence distinctions must remain independent
from attractiveness. The rejected site correctly distinguishes the 72-hour
run from the two-year projection in reviewed evidence text. The accepted
site also separates measured currents and assumed interval from the observed
run, and marks its teaching model illustrative. This is evidence for these
examples, not a substitute for reviewing every future beat and fallback.

The new automated checker is calibrated with separate tiny good/bad records
and browser fixtures. Those tests check measurement behavior, not creative
quality. Actual originals and real author review remain separate gates.
