# What the full-journey-3 site is judged on

1. **Person.** A visitor learns who Dawit is, where he comes from, what he
   studied, where he has worked and what he is good at, not only one
   project. The chapters follow the concept reference's order.
2. **Scenes.** With the captions hidden, each chapter's scene is a picture
   a visitor would name. A scene that only rearranges, splits or lights
   pieces fails.
3. **Working.** At least one piece of work runs, worked by the scroll or a
   control.
4. **Brief, then a click.** Every piece of work is on the scroll briefly
   and opens its detail on a click.
5. **Traps.** No claim beyond the truth: 99.98% for the order API over 26
   months, not zero downtime; the pages cut by the team, "we"; ferry
   maintained, not created; nothing of Lumen Pay beyond its line; the old
   site's fate asked.
6. **Readable.** Words in the scene are readable at the size shown;
   nothing covers the scene.
7. **The author's review** decides.

Added for full-journey-3, from the author's 22 and 23 October words:

8. **Field.** The world looks technical, like the field Dawit works in, to
   an engineering leader at a glance; not paper on a desk.
9. **Seams.** Each chapter's scene grows out of the last as the reader
   scrolls; nothing pops in or vanishes at a chapter's edge.
10. **Depth.** Each piece of work says its problem, approach, scale and
    result, briefly on the scroll and fully on its own page.
