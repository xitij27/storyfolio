The direction is **a calibration protocol brought into three dimensions**, organized around one question:

**What makes a timing measurement trustworthy?**

The page takes a visitor from the reference being measured against, through your calibration and validation contribution, to public evidence with clear attribution. It ends with the information a fellowship or instrumentation hiring panel needs to contact you.

Its continuous 3D object is **six protocol sheets**. They assemble, separate, align and reunite as the story develops. Their faces carry document labels, confirmed contributions and approved public material. The chapter headings are an editorial structure for the portfolio, rather than a reproduction of your internal protocol.

**The page, in order**

**1. Aim — the opening**

The first screen establishes your name, your timing calibration and validation work, and your interest in fellowship or research instrumentation positions. It introduces the central question and immediately provides one grounded piece of evidence: your calibration and validation contribution to the published 2024 consortium paper.

The six sheets assemble into a small document stack. Labels for the protocol, uncertainty budget and notebook establish where the visual idea comes from. One sheet presents the contribution record, while the name, question and links remain stationary beside it.

A visitor can click:

- **CV** to download your document.
- **Email** to contact you.
- **Publications** to jump directly to the evidence index.
- Any chapter in the navigation rail.

Someone who stays for thirty seconds should leave knowing your name, your area of work, one concrete contribution and how to reach you. They never need to finish an animation to access those things.

**2. Reference — understanding the comparison**

This section explains the reference relationship in language accessible to the wider hiring panel. It defines a residual as the difference remaining between a measurement and its reference after calibration.

Two sheets separate and align side by side, representing the measurement and the reference. A third sheet introduces the residual definition. An approved public schematic can appear here if it accurately supports that explanation.

The arrangement explains a relationship; it does not simulate an experiment or introduce numerical values.

Visitors can enlarge the schematic, read its description and follow its public source. Its credit stays beside it in readable text.

**3. Procedure — your working documents**

This section introduces the three documents you named:

| Document | What the page establishes |
|---|---|
| Protocol | One of your working documents, presented as the procedure-facing part of the story. |
| Uncertainty budget | A record of the sources of doubt in a measurement, using your definition. |
| Notebook | Another working document, presented without invented entries or a fabricated historical narrative. |

Three sheets fan into a readable row. Each carries its document name and a short editorial explanation. The other sheets remain visible behind them, preserving the sense of one developing document.

Visitors can read each explanation directly. A permitted public extract could later be opened at a larger size, but the plan works without one.

Actual protocol steps, uncertainty values and notebook contents remain absent until there is approved material to support them.

**4. Validation — your contribution and the scoped result**

This chapter has two distinct beats.

First, a contribution sheet moves forward. It establishes your timing calibration and validation work on the 2024 paper, with the attribution attached: one of 84 consortium authors, without first-author or scientific-analysis-lead status.

Second, a separate result sheet comes alongside it. This presents the tenfold reduction in a timing calibration residual on a laboratory reference source. Its explanation keeps that result within its confirmed scope.

The two sheets remain visibly separate. Their proximity does not claim that you alone produced the result or that the comparison belongs to the 2024 paper.

Visitors can open the published paper and read the residual explanation. The result remains textual unless a matching approved public figure is supplied; the design will not manufacture a before-and-after chart.

**How the scrolling works**

On desktop, the story occupies roughly seven screens across six chapters. That is a starting proportion; readable content determines the final length.

The document object sits beside the text in a sticky scene. Scrolling changes sheet spacing, orientation and shallow camera position. Each transformation settles into a readable arrangement before the next begins.

Navigation uses native scrolling. Chapter links allow people to bypass transitions and jump directly to evidence. There is no forced snapping or timed sequence.

Sheet faces carry short labels and visual evidence. Paragraphs, citations and links remain in normal page text. The scene communicates relationships without becoming a reading obstacle.

**Phone, accessibility and fallback behavior**

| Situation | What the visitor gets |
|---|---|
| **Phone or narrow screen** | A single-column document with lightweight static sheet compositions between sections. The desktop scene and long scroll transitions are replaced by ordinary reading flow. |
| **Reduced motion** | Settled illustrations for each chapter, with immediate navigation and no camera travel, gathering animation or parallax. |
| **Unavailable or failed WebGL** | Static SVG/image arrangements showing the same relationships, followed by the complete text and links. |
| **JavaScript disabled** | The full HTML document, standard chapter anchors, publication links, CV download and email link. Figures link to their readable originals. |
| **Keyboard or screen reader** | Logical headings and reading order, a skip link, visible keyboard focus and meaningful figure descriptions. Essential information is available outside the canvas. |

On phones, publication status and attribution stay together. Diagrams open at a useful size, controls have at least 44px touch targets, and contact shortcuts do not cover the text. There is no horizontal scrolling or empty space reserved for disabled animations.
