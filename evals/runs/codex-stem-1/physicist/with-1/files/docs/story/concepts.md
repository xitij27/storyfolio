## Foundations

Trade forms present in the material: calibration protocol, uncertainty budget, lab notebook, manuscript, authorship contribution statement and grant work-package section. The first three are explicitly Iris's day-to-day documents. Proposed chapter headings are editorial structures, not claims about her actual lab templates.

## A. Calibration protocol — selected

- **Question:** What makes a timing measurement trustworthy?
- **Form:** A calibration protocol. Six chapters: Aim (opening and identity), Reference (what the comparison means), Procedure (personal calibration work and working documents), Validation (personal validation and carefully scoped result), Record (all supplied research outputs with attribution/status), Handover (answer and contact).
- **Object:** Six thin protocol sheets, square corners, clipped together at rest. Faces carry sourced identity and contributions, an approved reference schematic, explicitly editorial summaries of the three documents, the residual claim with its scope, output records, and contact links. The clip is optional; sheets carry the meaning.
- **Transformations:** Reference lays two sheets side by side to distinguish measurement from reference without inventing values. Procedure opens three document roles as a readable row. Record stands sheets upright as publication and contribution records, keeping works separate. Other sheets remain visibly part of the same object.
- **Opening:** The sheets assemble from the supplied protocol, uncertainty budget and notebook document names. This is an editorial representation of current practice, not an origin story or simulated historical notebook.
- **Close:** Sheets reunite into a documented handover beside CV, email and publication links. The answer identifies Iris's timing calibration and validation contribution and its limits; final copy remains to be written.
- **Stands on:** Confirmed working documents, residual definition, 2024 contribution, separate 2026 preprint, grant writing role and target hiring audience in `facts.md`.
- **Needs:** Actual approved schematic/figure and credit lines; CV PDF, publication URLs and public email. No internal protocol extracts required.
- **Why hers:** Each transformation supports the distinction between Iris's contribution and broader laboratory/consortium claims, using the three documents she actually works with.

## B. Uncertainty budget

- **Question:** How do you show what a measurement can be trusted to say?
- **Form:** An uncertainty budget with six editorial chapters: Quantity (opening/identity), Reference (comparison), Sources (document roles), Evaluation (calibration and validation contribution), Reporting (outputs, status and limits), Review (answer/contact). Do not claim this is Iris's actual budget structure.
- **Object:** Six ruled ledger leaves, stacked as a budget at rest, bearing confirmed facts and approved figures. No invented numeric contributions, combined uncertainty or error bars.
- **Transformations:** Sources separates the leaves into aligned document roles. Evaluation pairs contribution statements with their supporting public work. Reporting aligns outputs with explicit scope/status fields. None of these alignments represents an actual numerical uncertainty calculation.
- **Opening:** The budget opens on the user-confirmed definition: sources of doubt in a measurement. Sheets use text until a real public budget extract is provided.
- **Close:** All leaves align as a record of evidence and limitations; contact offers the next conversation.
- **Stands on:** User's uncertainty-budget definition and the confirmed attribution distinctions in `facts.md`.
- **Needs:** Same public links/credits/contact as A; a permitted budget extract would materially strengthen this direction but is absent. Do not fabricate one.
- **Why hers:** It makes the specific residual/sensitivity and author/lead distinctions visible as limits on what the evidence supports.
- **Tradeoff:** Strong intellectual fit, but a convincing visual budget needs evidence we do not yet have; risks making clarification feel defensive.

## C. Laboratory notebook

- **Question:** How do you leave calibration work that someone else can inspect?
- **Form:** A notebook, six editorial chapters: Purpose (opening/identity), Reference notes (comparison), Working record (document roles), Checks (calibration/validation contribution), Output index (all work/status), Next entry (answer/contact).
- **Object:** Six notebook leaves with restrained ruling and a shared binding edge. Pages carry public evidence and source-backed contribution records, not invented dated entries or handwriting.
- **Transformations:** Working record fans out the three document roles. Checks places evidence beside the attribution it supports. Output index becomes an open spread of separate publication/grant records; it is an index, not a chronological claim.
- **Opening:** A notebook opens on Iris's current working materials. No invented first experiment, formative event or private note.
- **Close:** Pages settle into a notebook with a final contact tab.
- **Stands on:** Confirmed notebook use, document names, 2024 role and public-output distinctions in `facts.md`.
- **Needs:** Public links/credits/contact. A permitted real notebook extract would help; absent one, use typed editorial summaries labelled as such.
- **Why hers:** The leaves link her own document practice to the particular calibration, validation and writing contributions she confirmed.
- **Tradeoff:** Warmer and more personal, but without real notebook material it could become a generic research scrapbook.

B and C remain alternatives, not additional scenes within A.
