11 October 2026. Model visual inspection of all fourteen original movement
JPEGs, four original control PNGs, and the settled middle and closing PNGs
in [the final desktop capture](evidence/final/desktop/report.json). Twenty
images were opened individually, rather than judging a contact sheet or
inferring quality from pixel hashes. This is a review of saved images,
not the author's approval or a performance measurement.

The sampled journey preserves the identity of its four bench parts, opens
their assembly into a useful command-to-observation relationship, then
reassembles them around the observation/projection distinction. The arrows
and controls explain that relationship. A visible polish defect remains:
the observation face's two text layouts overlap during the reverse
crossfade. Its settled evidence face is clear. The evidence therefore
supports a concrete prototype for author review, with a disclosed movement
issue, rather than an unqualified claim that movement is visually clean.

## Arrows, depth and separation

The settled [middle](evidence/final/desktop/middle.relationship.png) uses
three exposed arrows with deliberate gaps at the module edges. They read
as relationships between parts, not as cable plugs or electrical routes.
Their pairing remains command to board, board to fixture, and fixture to
observation in the inspected moving frames. Forward 05 and reverse 03
show the first arrow rotating as command moves. The arrows fade out when
the parts become an overlapping assembly. No arrow visibly points to the
wrong part or cuts through a title in these samples.

Occlusion gives the assembly depth: the fixture forms its backing,
command and observation sit in front, and the board remains recognizable
by its green surface, mounting hardware and gold contacts. Temporary
occlusion is substantial in forward 06 and reverse 02 to 03, where the
observation display hides some or all of command. This is a transition
state, not missing geometry: command is visible again at both settled
ends and in the middle. During those frames its selection brackets can
outlast its unobscured face, making the selection momentarily less clear.

The text stays in the left column and the parts remain in the right.
None of the twenty inspected images shows the 3D scene covering the HTML
headings, descriptions or controls. Caption fading makes incoming and
outgoing text temporarily faint, especially forward 05 and reverse 06;
settled copy is opaque. The fixed illustrative-model note remains visible
at the bottom. Screws, clamps, knobs, buttons and edge contacts make the
parts distinguishable, but small face annotations and the response
board's fine print are too soft for dependable reading at this scale.
Essential ownership and evidence limits therefore still depend on the
clear HTML copy.

## Remaining issue and limits

The double text in reverse 02 is an actual visible intermediate state,
not a failed functional assertion. A future polish pass should avoid
blending two incompatible text layouts on top of each other, for example
by fading one fully out before the other becomes readable. This review
does not implement that change. It is a disclosed visual issue for the
prototype review; the images do not establish how distracting it feels
at a user's own scroll speed.

Seven samples per direction cannot establish continuous smoothness,
absence of every transient intersection, hardware frame rate, or physical
phone behavior. Source changes after capture require new evidence. This
review does not cover the other six browser conditions or establish
equivalence to the originating portfolio. It does not substitute for the
author's actual movement and phone review, and does not authorize full-site
expansion.

### Why the clearance change addresses the observed defect

The earlier connector code trimmed each endpoint by a module-edge
allowance and then calculated the distance between those trimmed
endpoints. When the allowances exceed the centre-to-centre spacing, the
endpoints reverse. Euclidean distance discards that sign, so the old code
still rendered a positive-length shaft and retained the original
arrowhead direction inside overlapping modules.

A read-only calculation used the actual `formations.ts` and
`formation.ts` modules, transpiled in memory with the installed TypeScript
compiler. It applied the existing `atRest`, `stagger`, `smoothstep` and
`mixFormation` functions, including the four pieces' different travel
progress. For the opening-to-middle fixture-to-observation connection:

| Raw travel progress | Centre spacing | Combined edge allowance | Signed free span | Old positive shaft length |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.65 | 1.3086 | 2.3553 | -1.0467 | 1.0467 |
| 0.75 | 1.7920 | 2.0969 | -0.3049 | 0.3049 |
| 0.80 | 2.0808 | 2.0201 | 0.0607 | 0.0607 |

These are deterministic geometric samples, not a claim that the earlier
JPEG was rendered at exactly one of those progress values. They explain
why the earlier overlapping observation/fixture state could show an
apparently free connector across the instrument.

The final implementation checks the signed free span before drawing,
requires room for the full 0.13-unit arrowhead, shortens the shaft by that
length, and places the head so its tip ends at the destination clearance.
This directly prevents the reversed-endpoint case and the insufficient
arrowhead-space case. It is a bounded geometry correction, not a general
collision detector for every possible third module. The visual samples
provide the separate observation that the reported crossing is no longer
visible in this captured journey.
