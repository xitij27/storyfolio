11 October 2026. Read-only source and image review of the production build
captured under [production-checks-clean](production-checks-clean/manifest.json).
All 22 images listed below were opened individually. No application source

## Result and limits

The no-WebGL and no-JavaScript alternatives retain a meaningful visual
explanation: the same four illustrated units assemble, separate into a
connected relationship, and reassemble with the evidence distinction. The
middle arrows establish command to board to fixture to observation. The
diagram gives the colleague credit for circuitry and Ada credit for the
fixture, and visibly labels itself as illustrative and not a wiring plan.
The closing diagram says `72h run / 2yr projection`; readable adjacent HTML
distinguishes the longest observed bench run from a battery projection based
on bench currents and an assumed reporting cycle. No waveform, exact current,
actual circuit topology, field endurance or completed qualification is
invented in these views.

At the reviewed desktop sizes, complete diagrams and the main text are
readable and do not overlap each other or clip horizontally. Cropped remnants
of preceding/following beats at the viewport boundaries reflect normal page
scrolling. The fixed bottom explanatory strip temporarily covers content
passing underneath it, visible in opening and reduced-motion start samples;
the same content is readable after further scrolling. This is a minor
occlusion, not evidence that the text has been removed or cannot be reached.

The phone composition preserves the object and its connected middle above
readable HTML. No main caption, control or model is clipped in the three
390 by 844 frames. The middle heading now fits cleanly and the controls
remain on one row. The scene's fine face print is too small and soft to be
the only way to read its facts; labels, arrows, HTML descriptions and closing
figures supply the meaning. The 3D detail remains recognizable, but these
images do not establish physical-phone performance, tap comfort, screen-reader
behavior or touch scrolling quality.

Reduced-motion captures retain the 3D scene. The nine frames show distinct
assembly, relationship and evidence arrangements. Start samples include
adjacent-beat text during the scroll boundary; mid/end samples provide
complete readable captions. The report's paired hashes match after each
300 ms stillness probe. That checks absence of continuing animation at those
sample points; it does not prove a completely unchanging scene during scroll
or replace inspection of transition behavior.

Context-loss evidence needs a narrower description: its three numbered beat
images were captured **before** context loss and still show 3D. Only
`context-lost.png` shows the post-loss fallback, at the resulting lower-page
position. It retains the bottom of the evidence diagram, evidence figures,
scope statements and explanation. The report records zero canvases and
`scene: off` after loss, with no problems, but also preserves the warning
`THREE.WebGLRenderer: WEBGL_lose_context extension not supported.` This review
does not suppress that warning or claim all three beats were photographed
after context loss. The complete post-loss beat sequence remains outside
this image set.

The previous diagnostic's right-edge navigation overlap is absent in these
rendered desktop frames: navigation sits below the apparatus. No new factual
or visual blocker was found in this review. That is a bounded inspection,
not an overall quality score or approval to expand the full site.
