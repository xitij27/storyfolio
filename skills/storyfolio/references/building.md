# Building

## The look

Show the person's site in two or three looks: for the pieces, the engine's looks at `/preview?look=`; for scenes of your own, the opening, one middle chapter and the ending, built in each. Let the person scroll them, ask which they choose, and stop for their answer before building on. An unanswered question in this turn is not a refusal to choose or permission to choose for them.

## What opens on a click, and the words

After the look choice, build what opens on a click (each project's page or window, the CV) and any remaining chapters of your own. For the opening and closing wording, show three versions of each whole beat, including the surrounding fixed text. Recommend one and leave room for their own wording. Stop for the choice, then use it word for word.

## The scene

This holds for the pieces with the person's data in them, and for a scene of your own. Compare the running scene with the storyboard at its settled beats. With the captions hidden, does each scene show what the storyboard says it shows, rather than only names for it? Revise the composition when the explanation lives only in the surrounding prose. Draw each diagram flat with the engine's pen, as `references/diagrams.md` says. Keep that accessible prose; it does not replace what the scene was meant to show.

Inspect the changes between beats in both scroll directions, including intermediate positions. When words in the scene change, correct any overlap that makes the old and new words unreadable together. A readable settled beat does not establish a readable transition. A scene that pops in or vanishes at a chapter's edge is not done: grow it out of the last.

Read every word in the scene at the size it is shown on a desktop, a phone and a short screen. Words too small to read there go to the caption.
