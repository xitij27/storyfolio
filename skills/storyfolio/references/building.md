# Building

## The spine and its look

Build the opening, one middle chapter and the ending in two or three looks.
Let the person scroll those looks, ask which they choose, and stop for their
answer before building the remaining chapters. An unanswered question in
this turn is not a refusal to choose or permission to choose for them.

## The chapters and their words

After the look choice, build the remaining chapters. For the opening and
closing wording, show three versions of each whole beat, including the
surrounding fixed text. Recommend one and leave room for their own wording.
Stop for the choice, then use it word for word.

## The scene

Compare the running scene with the storyboard at its settled beats. Can the visitor inspect the work relationship or evidence the pieces were meant to show, rather than only read its names? Revise the composition when the explanation lives only in the surrounding prose. Keep that accessible prose; it does not replace what the scene was meant to show.

Inspect the changes between beats in both scroll directions, including intermediate positions. When words on the pieces change, correct any overlap that makes the old and new words unreadable together. A readable settled beat does not establish a readable transition.

Read every face at the size it is shown on a desktop, a phone and a short screen. Words too small to read there go to the caption. The rail and other page chrome stay clear of the object at each of those sizes.
