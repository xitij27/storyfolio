# The storyboard

The third stop: the script of the whole page, which the person says yes to. Write it to `docs/story/storyboard.md`.

A plan covers what its asker thought to ask for. Most people ask for the idea and for what moves. Nobody asks what the site does on a phone, or without the 3D, or what a shared link shows, and a script that leaves those out is half a script. So this one has a place for each, and an empty place shows.

## The chapters

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## The beats

Under each chapter, a row to a beat. A beat is one settled state of the scene and one block of text.

| Beat | Screens | How the pieces stand | What is on their faces | What the chapter adds | Its caption |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

- **Screens:** how much scroll the beat takes. One, or more where the scroll works something.
- **How the pieces stand:** a stack, a fan, a ring, a row, a wall, or what the chapter needs. The same pieces in every beat.
- **What is on their faces:** the person's real pictures and words. Name the picture.
- **What the chapter adds:** anything in the scene besides the pieces. Often nothing.
- **Its caption:** a title and a sentence or two, written from the fact sheet.

A caption says nothing the fact sheet does not say. What a project taught the person, what was wrong before they came, how often a thing is used: those are theirs to tell you. Where you want such a sentence and do not have it, write the question in its place.

## Someone with thirty seconds

Say how a visitor who does not scroll gets the name, what the person does, one proof of it, and how to reach them. The first screen carries the first three. A rail jumps to any chapter, and one link skips to the close.

## The look and the type

Both come from the object's material: plates are ceramic on linen, a status page is dark glass, a paper is white with dark ink. Name two or three looks, each as its ground, its ink, one accent, and the surface of the pieces. The person picks by scrolling, once it is built.

Two typefaces: one for the text, and one for what is printed on the pieces, which belongs to the form.
