# Reading a profile

The first stop. You read the profile material, write down what you found, and ask. You do not plan yet.

## Before a requested build

Before the first factual handoff, verify Node against `engine/README.md`, including native TypeScript execution; check Git; and actually open and inspect a neutral local page with an available browser. Distinguish installed skills from those enabled for this session, using the advertised skill list and relevant local metadata. Record what you ran, what worked and any limits in the handover. Supplied tool paths or another agent's setup are not your checks.

Consider whether a missing skill would materially help this build. If so, give its purpose, source and a source-verified installation route for this agent, noting the evidence's date and limits. Do not guess a route you cannot verify. The person installs or declines; you neither install nor enable it, and carry on with available tools. Recommend nothing when nothing useful is missing.

## What to read

**A folder is not permission to read every file in it.** List filenames first and read the accompanying source notes. Leave private correspondence, contact exports and participant records unopened, even when they are beside profile material. Do not sample their contents to decide whether they belong. Tell the person which files you left. If a file's purpose is unclear, ask what it contains before opening it.

Read the profile material: a resume, a LinkedIn profile, GitHub, writing, talks, notes to you, a site they already have. Read all of that before you write a word.

A site they already have is a source of facts and pictures. What becomes of it is theirs to say, so ask.

## LinkedIn

You cannot fetch a LinkedIn profile. It turns away anything that is not signed in. It reaches you in one of four ways:

- **A PDF.** On their own profile, in a desktop browser: **More** under their name (some accounts show **Resources**), then **Save to PDF**. The phone app does not have it.
- **A copy of their data.** **Me**, **Settings & Privacy**, **Data privacy**, **Get a copy of your data**. They can pick only the profile and the positions, and the link then comes by email within minutes. The whole archive can take a day.
- **The text,** pasted.
- **Their own browser,** where you have a tool that works it and they have said yes.

These steps were checked against LinkedIn's help pages on 10 October 2026. The names of its buttons change.

**A copy of their data holds more than a profile.** It has their messages, and everyone they are connected to. Those are other people's words and addresses. The person unzipped a folder: they did not hand you their inbox. Open the files that are about the profile: the profile, positions, education, skills, projects, honours, recommendations. Leave messages and connections unopened, and tell the person that you left them. Nothing from the archive goes into the repository but facts about the person.

## The fact sheet

Write `docs/story/facts.md`. Beside every fact, write where it came from.

- **Who they are,** in the words they use for themselves.
- **Where they have lived,** with the years.
- **What they studied:** where, what and when.
- **Each job:** the employer, the title, the dates, what they did there.
- **Each project:** what it was, what they did in it, who else did, what there is to show of it.
- **Each figure.**
- **What others have said** of them.
- **What they learned from:** a book, a person, a first failure.
- **Each picture, drawing or file** they say they have.

## What one source hides

Where two sources disagree, you will see it. The mistakes that reach a site are in one line of one resume, where nothing disagrees with it. A team's result reads as theirs. A lab's name suggests work they never did. A photograph has someone else in it.

So put these to every line of the fact sheet. Where the material does not answer, you have a question for the person.

| Of every | Ask |
|---|---|
| Figure | What does it measure, and over what time? Is it measured, modelled or a guess? Whose result is it: theirs, or a team's? |
| Result of a team, a firm or a club | Were they in it when it happened, and what was their part? |
| Job, employer or lab | What did they themselves do there, in a sentence of their own? Its name is not an answer, and neither is a line such as "operations" or "supported the team". |
| Project | What was it: something in use, a course exercise, a script? Did it ever run anywhere? Who else made it? |
| Title | Is it what they are on paper, or what they are called? |
| Picture, drawing or quote | Who made it? Whose is it to use? Who or what does it show? |
| Story about a patient, a client or a colleague | May it be told at all, and with what left out? |
| Anything of an employer's | May it be shown? |

End the fact sheet with three lists:

- **Gaps.** What a site needs and the material lacks: places lived, education, a role without years, a project with no picture, a job with no outcome.
- **Disagreements.** Where two sources differ, with both readings. You do not settle one yourself, and you do not take the one that reads better.
- **To confirm.** Every question the table raised.

## What you put to the person

One message, which is the whole of your reply:

1. Two or three sentences on what the material establishes about the person. Describe their own work, not an employer's field. If their responsibilities need confirming, give the employer and listed role without turning its name or their profile slogan into a speciality.
2. Your questions, numbered, so that they can answer by number. Plain words. Say why you ask wherever that is not plain. Every disagreement and every item under "To confirm" is one of them.
3. These, which no material answers:
   - Who is the site for, and what should a visitor do when they reach its end?
   - What must not be shown?
   - Where have you lived, what did you study, and where have you worked, with the years, wherever the material does not say?
   - What do you work with and make, day to day, by the names you call them?
   - For the pictures, in working terms: what does your work look like when it goes well, in the screens, systems, devices, drawings or sites involved? In each role and in your studies, what did you build, run or work on that a colleague would recognise? What one picture would explain a project to a colleague? A sentence each. Their answers, not the labels on their CV, are what the scenes are made from. The site is for clients and employers, so the pictures stay in their working world, not their private life.
   - What did you learn from?
   - If they have a site already: what becomes of it?
4. No concept, outline or look. For a requested build, a brief setup limit or optional-tooling note may accompany the fact questions.

When they answer, write each answer into the fact sheet beside its fact, before you do anything else.

Every question then stands as one of two: answered, in their words, or not answered. One they passed over is not answered, and it stays under "To confirm". Silence is not a yes. That they have photographs does not say whose the photographs are.
